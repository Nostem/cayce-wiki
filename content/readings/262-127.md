# TEXT OF READING 262-127

**Reading [[262-127]]** · Series: 262 · Year: 1941 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 4/23/1941  Sex: M  ReadingID: 1507

This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at his home on Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 23rd day of April, 1941, in accordance with request made by [[Norfolk Virginia|Norfolk]] Study Group #1 of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno. Esther Wynne, Florence Edmonds, Hannah Miller, Frances Y. Morrow, Helen Ellington, Ruth LeNoir and [[Hugh Lynn Cayce|Hugh Lynn Cayce]]. Also Mae Verhoeven and Ruth Denney.]

Time of Reading 
3:30 to 3:55 P. M. Eastern Standard Time.

1. GC:  You will have before you the first twelve lessons which we have revised for the book, A [[Search for God Study Groups|SEARCH FOR GOD]], which we hope to publish.  Please comment on each lesson, as I call the title, pointing out any changes or thoughts that should be added.  You will then answer the questions, as I ask them:

2. EC:  Yes, we have the information as given, and the revision as indicated here.  In most of these, as we find, there is accord between that attempted to be indicated and that as appears prepared for publication.  Little or no change would we now make in any of the subjects indicated.  While there should be the first twelve lessons submitted, and also that upon [[Meditation|Meditation]], we find that [[Meditation|Meditation]] should come first; not as a preface or introduction but as to prepare the individual - or individuals - who would use the lessons as a study.

3. And each affirmation for the lesson should preface that lesson, or be upon a page alone facing the lesson.

4. The rest we would keep in the manner indicated, though we would discard the questions - or most of them.

5. To be sure, the material will receive a great deal of commendation and also a great deal of disputation.  A great deal of anxious moments will be spent by some.

6. Only ask that each individual TRY living that as presented here, and there will be a real [[Revelation (Book)|revelation]] to all who will use - not abuse; or to all who will apply that suggested here.

7. There is little, that has been offered for the student, as well prepared as this material.

8. DO NOT print in a form that is to be just thrown aside.  It deserves a good house.  Give it such.

9. Ready for questions.

10. (Q)  First lesson, - Cooperation.  Any changes, or thoughts that should be added?
(A)  Though this should be listed as the first lesson, put [[Meditation|Meditation]] in the front.  Let each know what it is about. And the form and manner of the meditations as presented here is among the first of the occidental ideas of an oriental theme according to that presented by [[Jesus Christ|the Master]].

11. (Q)  Any other comment on the paper on Meditation?
(A)  Publish it first - in the book.

12. (Q)  Cooperation - any suggestions for changes or additions?
(A)  Good.

13. (Q)  Know Thyself.
(A)  Very well.

Let each lesson be prepared so that the affirmation as given is presented first, on a page alone facing the title of the lesson.

14. (Q)  What Is My Ideal?
(A)  Very well.

15. (Q)  Faith.
(A)  Good.

16. (Q)  Virtue and Understanding?
(A)  Very well.

17. (Q)  Fellowship.
(A)  Very well.

18. (Q)  Patience.
(A)  Good.

19. (Q)  The Open Door.
(A)  Very good.

20. (Q)  In His Presence.
(A)  Good.

21. (Q)  The Cross and the Crown.
(A)  Good.

22. (Q)  The Lord Thy God is One.
(A)  Very good.

23. (Q)  Love.
(A)  Excellent.

24. (Q)  Which of the questions and answers should be cut out, and what principle should be followed in selecting those to be used?
(A)  If any, add a great many more.  We would leave out the questions.

25. (Q)  Should all Biblical quotations be listed as from the King James version or as just from the Bible?
(A)  Just from the Bible.

26. (Q)  Any other advice as to the publishing or distribution of the book?
(A)  There will be a great many manners and ways of distribution.  A great many of various groups, of various institutions or stores, have asked; and many more will ask when other books are published.

Give them to commentators, critics, lecturers many of them. Ask individuals to sponsor ones to be sent to ministers, to Bible houses - even though they send them back to you, offer them!

27. We are through for the present.

> 2/39 Letter from Rev. J. B. Clower, Jr.:  "[[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|The Association]]
> for Research and Enlightenment has requested me, as a
> Protestant minister, to read and evaluate, from my point of
> view, these 'First Six Lessons on [[Soul|Soul]] Development'.  As I
> understood the request I was not being asked to evaluate the
> work of [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]], nor any intended use of the
> 'Lessons', but rather to appraise the 'Lessons' themselves
> solely on the basis of their content value.
> 
> "Truth is truth, regardless of the source from which it comes
> or the style of the package in which it is wrapped.
> Nevertheless, it has been my observation, which critical
> self-examination seems but to confirm, that those of us who
> 'deal in the dispensing of truth' are usually very hesitant
> about recommending any parcel of it which is done in a new
> wrapper, or which does not bear our own particular label.
> 
> "These six 'Lessons' are being published by an organization
> associated with a very remarkable man and a very remarkable
> work.  I have known this man and his work for five years, and
> during that time have had abundant opportunity to study them
> both.  There is still much about them that I do not
> understand (as more learned and careful observers than I are
> equally ready to admit), and there are some philosophical
> assumptions underlying the work that is being done which I
> find congenial, but which I have not yet been led to accept
> dogmatically.
> 
> "Consequently, when I was asked to give my reaction to these
> 'Lessons' I found myself wondering just what my honest
> reaction would be.  And so I began to read.  But as I read
> I began to realize that here was nothing new, nothing
> startling.  Here was good devotional reading.  I felt myself
> spiritually quickened by much of what I read.  True, I found
> a few implications here and there which I felt inclined to
> question as I have already intimated.  But with these
> exceptions I found nothing in the substance of the 'Lessons'
> as such which, in my judgement, could not appropriately be
> published with the imprint of my own church's denominational
> publishing house.  The lessons are Christian in theme and
> [[Purpose|purpose]]."

> A SEARCH FOR GOD
> 
> REVIEW by MISS ESTHER WYNNE
> 
> AT THE TENTH ANNUAL CONGRESS of the ASSOCIATION FOR RESEARCH
> AND ENLIGHTENMENT, INC.,
> 
> JUNE 19, 1941.
> 
> As many other branches of the work of the Association for
> Research and Enlightenment, Inc., the departments of
> publication and study groups were small and seemingly
> insignificant in the beginning of the organization.  The
> reason for this is, the message is first to the individual,
> then to groups, then to classes and finally masses.
> 
> It was more like an experiment in faith than an organization
> of activities through which others might be helped,
> stimulated and uplifted; though faith was enough evidence of
> things hoped for, to encourage many "to keep on keeping on."
> 
> It was the evening of September, 14, 1931, that a group of
> nineteen members of the Association offered themselves as
> channels through which lessons might be compiled, that if
> lived would, as the readings put it, "be lights to a dying
> world."
> 
> The members organized themselves into the first study group,
> which afterwards became a unit of the department of
> publication of the Association.  Mr. Cayce gave readings
> regularly over a period of ten years to the group for
> direction and as a basis for the work that was to be
> undertaken.  The members applied themselves diligently to the
> study of the readings, to [[Prayer|prayer]] and [[Meditation|meditation]], and in their
> weak way attempted to make practical what they learned.  This
> was the beginning of the book, "A Search for God."
> 
> The book is composed of a group of twelve lessons and a
> lengthy discourse on meditation and [[Prayer|prayer]].  "The intent is
> not to furnish new information, but rather to give that which
> if lived will answer to that within self for the assurance as
> to not only soul awakening, but the continuity of life."
> 
> In the first lesson, Cooperation is defined as losing sight
> of self and becoming a channel through which blessings may
> flow to others.  During the compilation of this lesson, as in
> all others that followed in the series, the members
> experienced much that was humiliating and much that called
> for selflessness, yet in all they were happy since they had
> been counted worthy to be called into service.
> 
> 2. A Search for God.
> 
> The second lesson, Know Thyself, is only a beginning of a
> study mentally, physically and spiritually.  The [[Purpose|purpose]] of
> this lesson will not be fully understood or appreciated until
> there is a study and application of "[[Revelation (Book)|The Revelation]]" and the
> "[[Seven Churches|Seven Spiritual Centers]] of Man," better known as the
> endocrine system.
> 
> The subject of the third lesson is What is My Ideal.  From
> this lesson we gather that the "True Ideal is the highest
> spiritual attainment to be reached in this material world."
> "[[Ideal|The Ideal]] must be of a spiritual nature, that has its
> foundation in truth, in God. - The gift to man is an
> individual soul, which may be one with Him and may know
> itself to be one with Him and yet individual in itself with
> attributes of the whole, yet not the whole."
> 
> Faith is the subject of the fourth lesson.  In this lesson
> faith is declared to be spiritual, and attribute of the soul.
> "It is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of
> things not seen."  It accepts or rejects without basis of
> reason, or that which is perceived through the five senses.
> "In the material world we often mistake confidence for faith,
> for we are prone to depend upon knowledge gained through the
> physical senses.
> 
> Then follow lessons of Fellowship, Patience, The Open Door,
> In His Presence, the Cross and the Crown, The Lord Thy God
> is One and Love.  Each helps to awaken a better understanding
> of God the Father and His relationship to His children.
> 
> The discourse on [[Prayer|prayer]] and meditation will be of inestimable
> value to those who seek the awakening of the inner self,
> to those who would meet Him face to face; for it points the
> way to harmony, peace and protection that is found in the
> Christ.  "The form and manner of the meditation as presented
> here is among the first of the occidental ideas of an
> oriental theme according to that presented by [[Jesus Christ|the Master]]."
> 
> Those who read and study these lessons are asked only to
> try to live them, for there will be a real [[Revelation (Book)|revelation]] to
> those who will use, not abuse, in their daily lives, that
> presented in them.
> 
> 6/42 Review of SFG I by Reverend William McLaughlin, Catholic
> Priest missionary:  "The [[Psalms|Psalmist]] says:  'Whither shall I go
> from thy spirit?  or whither shall I flee from thy presence?
> If I ascend into heaven, thou art there:  if I make my bed
> in hell, behold, thou art there.  If I take the wings of the
> morning, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea; even
> there shall thy hand lead me, and thy right hand shall hold
> me.  If I say, Surely the darkness shall cover me; even the
> night shall be light about me.  Yea, the darkness hideth not
> from thee; but the night shineth as the day; the darkness and
> light are both alike to thee.'
> In these divinely inspired words, we know that wherever the
> true believer makes a search for God, He will be found.  We
> realize, as did the [[Psalms|Psalmist]], that God is Spirit and can be
> found in the spiritual world, although He inter-penetrates
> all matter.
> 
> Reading Edgar Cayce's remarkable book, A SEARCH FOR GOD, one
> comes to the deep conviction that he has found God, but in
> his deep humility, he claims to be searching for Him.
> 
> St. Francis of Assisi said that humility is the greatest
> Christian virtue and we know that it is the rarest, for the
> human Ego is inclined to exalt itself, forgetting the
> Magnificat of the Blessed [[Virgin Mary|Virgin Mary]]:  "He hath put down the
> mighty from their seats, and exalted them of low degree."
> 
> In reading this book, one is impelled to conclude that any
> true appraisal of it, will be equivalent to writing a
> condensed commentary on the [[New Covenant|New Testament]], for Edgar Cayce
> culls from this sacred volume the quintessence of its
> teachings and keeps the reader's mind and attention at a high
> altitude of contemplation and awareness of the Divine.
> 
> To those persons who have had the privilege of listening to
> one of Edgar Cayce's readings, it would be adscititious on my
> part to maintain, as I do, that he lifts a corner of [[Veil of the Temple|the veil]]
> separating us mortals on the physical plane from our true
> home in the spiritual world, for we are primarily spiritual
> beings, encased for a time in mortal flesh.
> 
> Edgar Cayce conveys to us the knowledge that we must not let
> burden of the flesh overpower our spiritual inheritance, or
> that portion of the Spirit inherent in us from manifesting
> in us.  Not, of course, quite in the sense in which the
> Church through the ages has preserved the manifestation of
> the Godhead in the person of [[Jesus Christ|the Christ]] in the Feast of the
> Epiphany, or as it was called in the first three centuries
> after [[Crucifixion of Jesus|the Crucifixion]], the Feast of the Theophany."
>
