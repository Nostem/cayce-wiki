# TEXT OF READING 428-13 F 57 (Widow, Artist, Protestant)

**Reading [[428-13]]** · Series: 365-574 · Year: 1941 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 4/3/1941  Sex: F  Age: 57  ReadingID: 3217

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at his office on Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 3rd day of April, 1941, in accordance with request made by the self - Mrs. [428], Associate Member of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Hugh Lynn Cayce|Hugh Lynn Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading 
3:50 to 4:10 P. M. Eastern Standard Time.           ..., N.Y.

1. HLC:  You will have before you the body and enquiring mind of [428] ..., N.Y., who seeks information, advice and guidance, as to how she may find work suitable to her abilities and from which she may gain remuneration sufficient to support herself, her mother and sister.  You will give the body that which will be of the greatest help at this time, answering the questions, as I ask them:

2. EC:  Yes, we have the body, the enquiring mind, [428].

3. In considering conditions existent, the abilities of the body, - to be sure [[Air Shafts (Pyramid)|the channels]] through which aid for self and others should be sought are in the field of art.

4. Where, when and how - then - is the body to make same practical, to meet the needs in the present?

5. As we find, the work or activities in the present field should not be discarded, but continued only in part time.

6. And, through those agencies that seek those who do decorative art, the body should seek those activities to meet the present needs; to make for better security in the care of self and others.  At the same time, when there is not the full activity in these directions, continue in the present field until there is sufficient of the work for a display - or an opening for the present activities.

7. Seek especially in those fields such as represented by Conti or Hudnut, in preparing the decoration for the booklets that are a part of the advertising.  To be sure, this comes under the head of commercial art; and the seeking through regular agencies where such contacts may be made will prove the more satisfactory.

8. For, then - under the present arrangements or environs - the body will be able to carry on the greater portion of this work in the home; thus making the most satisfactory arrangements for all conditions as exist or as may arise as progress is made.

9. Seek, then, in these fields of activity; and we will find that these will prove to be most satisfactory.

10. Ready for questions.

11. (Q)  Do I understand correctly that the decorative art mentioned is the commercial art for the perfume booklets, and what not?
(A)  This is correct.

12. (Q)  In continuing my own work in art, -
(A)  (Interrupting)  This should not be of the larger pieces, but more the smaller type - or miniature.  And this may be done on ivory, cellulose or the like.  These would be the parts to stress.

Such connections or associations will also give outlets, or lead to channels through which there may be a greater appreciation of - and the outlets for distribution of the present work, see?

13. (Q)  Should I consider an exhibit of my miniatures?  If so, where?
(A)  As just indicated, this would come a little later; but, to be sure, an exhibit.  This should come in the early fall.

As to place, - consider that just indicated.  This is not to be for some months, and there are many contacts to be made before such is to be done.  Through the contacts there will be places provided for such; that, as indicated, will open a field or an outlet for the greater appreciation of the body's abilities, and also better facilities for same, see?

Do that, then.

14. (Q)  May any other suggestions be given regarding the best possible approach to Conti or Hudnut?  [Conti Advertising Agency, Inc?  N.J.?  Richard Hudnut, cosmetics?  [[New York City|N.Y.C]].?]
(A)  These contracts are let through regular agencies, see? Then the body should make contact with such agencies and indicate, or show its own work and its abilities in this decorative field.

15. (Q)  Should I continue living as I now am, or what changes would you advise?
(A)  As we have also indicated, in this field of activity this would be the more satisfactory arrangement than to make changes.  This is considering others as well as self.

16. (Q)  May any spiritual and mental advice be given at this time?
(A)  Hold fast to those [[Purpose|purposes]], those promptings, those desires that have so strengthened the body and the abilities, throughout the physical as well as mental trials through which it has been passing.

Know that the strength, the power, the might is in Him, - who gives all good and perfect peace.

Find that assurance of peace within self, and this will enable the body, the mind, to meet all conditions as they arise.

17. We are through for the present.

Copy to Self
"    " Ass'n file

> R3. 9/22/41 Dear Mr. Cayce,
> 
> 
> My last contact with you was concerning my reading, which I
> have worked at sincerely - and - hard to fulfill, but with no
> good results financially, and since you offered your further
> help in case I needed it, I feel that I owe it to you at
> least report on the outcome.  Mr. [257] has done so well with
> your suggestions, that I have not quite given up hope.
> 
> There is no fault to find with the reading, it was clear and
> definite, but the execution of same was much too difficult
> for me.  For instance to do the illustrations for Hudnut
> booklets I did not have the courage to attempt, because I
> have not the faintest idea how to go about it, never saw it
> done, did not know what medium to use nor the prevailing
> tendency.  Of all the arts, advertising illustrations needs
> much training and had I contacted the agencies, I would have
> only shown my ignorance.
> 
> Then I tried very sincerely to acquaint myself with miniature
> painting and found a good teacher, but as regards its
> execution, I found this to be the most difficult, and an art,
> which for this very reason is becoming a "lost" art.  It is
> so fine and has to be done so carefully, that even experts
> can only work at it two hours at a time.  I learnt that
> technique and on cellulose with oil painted for nice little
> pictures, which are greatly admired, but for which, after all
> that work I was offered $2.50.  This does not pay me, as at
> the utmost I can make three pictures a week; unless I can
> place them better.
> 
> My larger pieces do not find favor in any of the places where
> I showed them for various reasons, one being too perishable,
> too conventional, although pleasing, not what is expected.
> May be the art-trained eye they reveal my lack of training.
> On the whole my work has improved, but when it is not
> accepted, all the incentive to paint more is gone and I must
> earn something.  Furthermore the trend of the times is
> straining everybody to the utmost and artists are slashing
> prices to 1/2 and 1/3 of their original prices.  Very well-
> meaning men in the business and a very good friend of my
> cousin's have given me but little encouragement, in fact none
> and I have come to the conclusion that something more
> practical may be the answer.  I have a chance here on ... to
> learn [[Photography (Atlantean)|photograph]] retouching and [[Photography (Atlantean)|photography]] and thought with
> one profession learnt well, I might do better than work at
> art for which I have good talent but no training, unless it
> can be definitely designated where I can place my pictures
> for sale.  I have tried at 10 places and one Artist's Supply
> shop took three of them on commission, but at a figure so low
> that it does not pay, by the time I consider all my expenses
> of framing, carfare and time.
> 
> I must say that retouching photographs and finishing same
> appeals to me, and even if I cannot become a photographer,
> I might be able to work in a studio, but even for this
> profession I have to become acquainted with all the
> expressions and terms, but am willing.
> 
> I would like to hear from you as it is so hard for me to
> decide what to do.
> 
> With kind regards to you and all the members of your family,
> I am,
> 
> Very truly yours, [428]
> 
> R4. 9/25/41 EC's letter:  "Will be glad to make an appointment
> for a ck. up rdg. if you like (no cost to you).  But if you
> are really interested in photography, I would by all means
> take it up.  You possibly know that I was in that profession
> for twenty years and made a fairly good living at it - had
> several studios - so have some idea of the subject.  As a
> retoucher you could make excellent money, I am sure.  It
> would require that you take lessons for a few weeks, but
> after the first week or so you should be making something
> as you learn.  I believe there are several schools of
> photography in NYC - I do not know which is the better one,
> but just take up retouching and coloring photographs - that
> alone would be well, but there is more money in being a
> retoucher and able to color the photographs.  That you could
> perhaps do already, if you have painted even one miniature,
> and there is always a place for a retoucher - of that I am
> very sure."
>

> 
> Dear Mr. Cayce,
> 
> Your letter of the 25th. ult. was a great comfort to me and
> especially your opinion concerning my occupation as
> retoucher.  I was told that a good retoucher can always find
> work, so will go right ahead.
> 
> At the time of my writing I was greatly concerned about my
> finances, but I have since had the good news that a note,
> which Mr. [322] gave in '33 for $500.--, is going to be paid
> back with interest, which will amount to amount $800.--, so,
> while I am not going to relent in my efforts at earning, my
> mind is greatly relieved by the knowledge of this income.
> I am most grateful to you for your kind offer of a check
> reading, but right now at this writing feel that I would want
> to give the reading a fairer [[Trial of Jesus|trial]].  The slow working out of
> the reading may be due to the fact that my "whole" efforts
> are not devoted to it, but as I am still settling Mr. [322]'s
> affairs, I can do no different and as things are, it is by
> far more important that I collect outstanding accounts than
> paint.  I suppose all these things have to be taken into
> consideration.  I can say though, that I am very fond of
> retouching and would be willing to learn gladly all that is
> needed for it and whatever else I can absorb of photography.
> 
> The news of your son's, [[Hugh Lynn Cayce|Hugh Lynn]], marriage certainly is good
> news and you and [[Gertrude Cayce|Mrs. Cayce]] will be very happy to have a
> daughter in addition to your sons and to have him come home
> to work will be a continued comfort and help to you, I am
> sure.
> 
> Separately I am sending you a little gift, which you may give
> to the bride, with all my best wishes and the added
> explanation of what your readings have done to bring out
> dormant talent.  It is the same old story, when the first
> reading told me that I would paint, I thought it utterly
> impossible, but here it is, even on cellulose.  I hope it
> will give you pleasure to present, as well as that the dear
> bride will enjoy it in her home.  I am enclosing my
> congratulations to [[Hugh Lynn Cayce|Hugh Lynn]].
> 
> I will write to you again later and in the meantime hope for
> everything good in connection with the wedding and remain, as
> ever,

> 
> Sincerely your, [428]
