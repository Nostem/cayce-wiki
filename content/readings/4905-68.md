# TEXT OF READING 4905-68

**Reading [[4905-68]]** · Series: [[3001-5747 — Miscellaneous Physical and Life Readings|3001-5747]] · Year: 1927 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 1/17/1927  Sex: M  ReadingID: 12993

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at his office, 115 West 35th Street, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 17th day of January, 1927, in accordance with request made by Mr. M. B. Wyrick.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Mrs. Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno. Mrs. [[Zacharias and Elizabeth|Elizabeth]] E. Evans.]

Time of Reading 
5:10 P. M. Eastern Standard Time.      Bosque County, [[Texas|Texas]].

1. GC:  You will give the conditions of this well at the present time, telling us how many more feet until they pass through the obstruction they are working on, and how many more charges of acid they should use, and how they may make the success of these operations.  You will answer any questions I may ask you regarding same.

2. EC:  Yes, we have [[Well Shaft (Pyramid)|the well]], the operations here.  This we have had before.  The acid is working as is set for same, and it will be found that the present charge or load will make more than six (6) feet in this present load, see?  When this has been cleared and drilling resumed, until same shows the solid hard cutting again, two (2) more charges only will complete this, making then the full depth of the twenty-two (22) feet, since first encountering the obstruction, see? When this is cleansed, and well - WELL - swabbed, and the drilling resumed, the operations will soon bring the desired results - production.  Then, only care, attention, and being careful of tools, that same do not show the action of acid in tools, so that they easily crystallize - the operations will soon, then, be brought to the successful termination. Patience, care, persistence - for these will bring results - even as has been given.  Ready for questions.

3. (Q)  With the care and attention given, and no further complications, about when may production be expected?
(A)  When they have carried out those two operations further, as well as the one present in hole of the operation, with acid and drilling, and the drilling of the further distance necessary - then will be production.

4. We are through for the present.

> R5. 2/1/27 Mr. LaBryn's letter:

> 
> Morgan, [[Texas|Texas]]

> TO ALL SHAREHOLDERS:
> 
> No, - we have not quit nor lost faith.
> 
> But have been hard at work every day since the last report
> went out.  At that time we ordered 6100 feet of casing
> weighing 53 tons, which arrived at [[Well Shaft (Pyramid)|the well]] during July.
> This casing was run in the hole three times before it was
> successfully set.  The first time it buckled or bent leaving
> the lower section or about twenty two feet in the hole adding
> more trouble to be overcome.  The second time it was lowered
> for the [[Purpose|purpose]] of running cement through it in order to
> encase the lost bailer and piece of casing.  However, this
> was unsuccessful because of extreme heat, which dried the
> cement before reaching bottom of casing, plugging it up to
> the extent of one hundred and fifty feet, which forced us to
> again pull the entire string of casing.  This was very
> discouraging as well as expensive.
> 
> Drilling again was attempted without use of casing, and after
> making about thirty feet of hole in the hard lime, the drill
> started binding which forced us to stop.
> 
> It was again decided to run the casing in the hope that we
> could set it in this hole.  This operation was successful and
> we figured that our troubles were over, but when drilling
> operations were resumed again, we found we still had to
> overcome the obstruction which apparently was pushed down
> when the casing was lowered.
> 
> For the past four months since casing was set, our driller
> has been pounding away on the steel obstruction as well as
> using acid to help clear the way.  We feel that we have
> almost accomplished the drilling up of the obstruction after
> which we should have but very little trouble drilling through
> the lime into production.  The hole is now in the best of
> condition, so that production can be handled without any
> delay.  The purchase of the casing, labor, fuel, repairs,
> etc., has cost us in the neighborhood of $35,000. since
> losing the bailer in December, 1925.  The bulk of this amount
> has been supplied by a few men.
> 
> Remember, every effort is being made to complete this well by
> those in charge of operations.  It requires Patience,
> Persistence and CASH to overcome conditions.  We are
> fortunate in having the best driller in the country in
> charge, who knows every angle of the drilling game.  He feels
> sure of big production under the Pennsylvania lime rock we
> are now drilling in; so try and help us out AT THIS TIME, by
> sending us your check for whatever amount you can spare, and
> help lift the burden from the few men who have been so
> liberal in making it possible to carry on operations.
> 
> Sincerely yours, Geo. LaBryn
