# TEXT OF READING 1368-1 F 69

**Reading [[1368-1]]** · Series: [[801-1500 — Psychological and Dream Interpretation Readings|801-1500]] · Year: 1937 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 5/8/1937  Sex: F  Age: 69  ReadingID: 7059

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at his home on Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 8th day of May, 1937, in accordance with request made by the daughter - Miss [...], new Associate Member of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc., sponsored by Mr. [261].

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading 
3:20 to 3:25 P. M. Eastern Standard Time.          ..., N.Y. 
(Physical Suggestion)

1. EC:  Yes.

2. Now, as we find, conditions are such that the better conditions will be hospitalization for observance as related to those conditions that have arisen from the poor circulation, and the very bad conditions of the poor eliminations - thus forming in the superficial and the lymph circulation the accumulations as well as that effect upon the organs for the activities of these in the system, or between the liver and the kidney.

3. So, owing to the requirements that are necessary as to diet, the close care that may be had under hospitalization, we would advise such a move rather than attempting to - owing to the conditions - make applications that would be in adverse influence for those attending or waiting upon the body.

4. When these conditions have been reduced by the care and the application of the electrical forces as well as the diet - if operative forces are missed - then, upon the return from the hospital, we may give helpful forces for the body.

5. Ready for questions.

6. We are through for the present.

Copy to Mr. [261], ..., [[New York City|New York City]], Attention Miss [...]
"    " Ass'n file

> Mrs. [1368].

> rather serious complications, Miss [...] and I believe it
> would be better to have the reading without telling Mrs.
> [1368].  Then she will have no cause for worry because of it.
> It is suggested that the reading be held preferably in the
> morning when it can easily be arranged that Mrs. [1368] be at

> that the necessary arrangements can be made at this end of
> the line.
> 
> Miss [...] has suggested that the following questions or
> similar ones be asked at the time of the reading:
> 
> 1. In view of Mrs. [1368]'s lack of willingness to go to the

> 
> 2. Should she continue to take sleeping powders.
> 
> 3. What diet would be most beneficial.
