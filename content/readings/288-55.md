# TEXT OF READING 288-55 F 38

**Reading [[288-55]]** · Series: [[282-363 — Individual Life Readings (Reincarnation-Past Lives)|282-363]] · Year: 1943 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 2/4/1943  Sex: F  Age: 38  ReadingID: 1946

This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at the office of [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]], Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 4th day of February, 1943, in accordance with request made by the self - Miss [288], Active Member of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading                               ... Street, 
3:45 to 4:00 P. M. Eastern War Time.          ..., Virginia.

1. GC:  You will give the physical condition of this body at the present time, with further suggestions for corrective measures; answering the questions, as I ask them:

2. EC:  Yes, we have the body here, [288]; this we have had before.

3. As we find, changes are indicated since last we had the body here; some improvements, and the course of the disturbance in the portions of the [[Glands (Esoteric)|glandular]] forces - especially as related to the parts of the [[Glands (Esoteric)|thyroid]] and the centers about the mammary [[Glands (Esoteric)|glands]] [mumps] - indicated improvements.

4. Yet, there are disturbances to the general glandular system.

5. These, then, are conditions as we find them with this body in the present:

6. In the blood supply we find there are indications of a stoppage, or blocking, in some of the eliminating channels of the body [menstruation].  These make for a quickening in the pulse and those inclinations for temperatures in parts of body through varied periods, as we shall see the cause of same.

7. Unless these are removed there may be the resulting conditions in the superficial circulation, forming into boils, glades or the like.

8. In the nervous forces of the body there are the natural tendencies - anxieties in regard to the pathological forces, as well as the imaginative forces; indicating nerve tensions.

9. These, as we find, affect the general reactions in the body-forces.

10. And there are those impingements in the central nervous system that in part, as well as combined with other conditions, are the sources of some of the disturbances as combined with the general conditions.

11. In the organs of the body, - we find that head and throat, with those conditions in the mammary glands and parts of thyroid, show a plethora, or the swelling [Bags under chin, etc.], and the attempt of the body to disgorge those congestions produced by same; fever, temperatures through the areas being the manners of destruction of same - as there has been an increase in the leukocyte to care for same. These are being cared for, but will require precautions; else there may be after effects of such disturbances arising in various portions of the body.

12. Lungs, bronchi and larynx are nominal, though of course under the stress show a sympathetic reaction.

13. Heart, liver, kidneys, - here we find the circulation showing the lack of proper eliminations in the body.  These regurgitations, as in a disturbed kidney and liver circulation, produce nausea - as combined with the effects of properties that have been taken in body for the production of an activity in the glands and organs already choked by a suppression through the central nervous forces [stilbestrol tablets] [synthetic estrogen] [See 288-54, Par. R1 and 288-55, Par. R5.]; causing the organs in the pelvis to be adhered to walls of the vagina itself.  These prevent the normal activity.

14. In the structural portions of body, - as indicated in times back, - there has been a disturbance in the coccyx end of spine [causing periodic hemorrhoids].  This, with the injury or pressure produced in the sacral [from auto accident shock on 10/9/42] has caused a greater trouble, with a disturbance already in the pelvic organs.

15. As we find, these would be best regulated by those corrections taken osteopathically to correct those conditions in the sacral, the lumbar and the coccyx area.

16. It may require that there be local applications to prevent adherence of the womb to the side of the vagina; packing with medicated gauze; though this MAY be able to be straightened without such.  These we would do, through osteopathically; ALL of same.

17. For the first eight to ten of these, we would have these twice a week; and following ONE of these each week we would have the [[Hydrotherapy|hydrotherapy]] treatment, with the heat, sweat and rubdown.  Have the adjustment and THEN the [[Hydrotherapy|hydrotherapy]] treatment.  This is opposite, to be sure, from that ordinarily indicated, but these will respond better for this body if taken in this manner.

18. These as we find, with general precautions to keep the eliminations well, will bring the better conditions for this body.

19. Ready for questions.

20. (Q)  Who would you suggest to do the antiseptic packing of the vagina, in case this becomes necessary?
(A)  Whoever the body chooses; one in sympathy with those measures to be taken.

21. We are through with this reading.

Two copies to Self (one for Dr. M. L. Richardson, D.O.) Copy to Ass'n file

> During my time with Gladys (1979 to 1986) I kept a log in
> which was recorded in some detail the events of her life.
> Each office visitor and telephone call was recorded.  Dates,
> times, places, notes about conversations and events were
> recorded both in the daily log and on the pages of calendars
> both of which are now stored in E.C.F. Archives.  I did this
> to provide as accurate as possible an account for the
> future.
> 
> Gladys' last day in the Foundation office was Tuesday,
> December 31st, 1985.  She had been to Dr. Lewellyn for what
> she thought was a gum infection.  She said she felt "tired,
> just worn out."  She left at approximately 10:30 A.M.
> 
> Dr. O.M. Wakefield, long time friend and family doctor
> diagnosed Gladys' condition on Friday, January 3rd.  Gladys
> drove herself to Virginia Beach General Hospital and was
> admitted to room #419 with acute leukemia.  She agreed to
> what she called a "white cell treatment" but did not want
> chemotherapy or what she called "any heroic measures."
> Gladys fully realized what she had and the eventual outcome.
> She said to me quite vigorously, "Well, even if I do die
> from this whatever it is, I'm just going right back to
> work!"
> 
> Her niece, Ann Carlisle Wilson and her nephew Chapin Wilson
> arrived on Friday, January the 10th and from then on Gladys
> was literally cradled in and shielded by the love of her
> family.
> 
> Monday, January the 13th I told Gladys that Harold Allen
> died. She said, "Harold is such a good man, he was a trustee
> from 1947 to 1962."
> 
> Wednesday, January 15th Gladys had completed the last of the
> treatments and was allowed to go home under the care of her
> nephew who lived with her.
> 
> Gladys remained at home until January 22nd when she was
> re-admitted to room #416 with a private nurse named Clarice
> Dorian arranged for by Gladys' doctor.  (One of the symptoms
> of her condition was shortness of breath.  She was afraid
> of not being able to get her breath, especially at night.)
> The morning of the 22nd Gladys shared with me her "horse"
> [[Dreams|dream]] of the night before as follows:
> 
> January 22, 1986:  "I dreamed I was riding a horse and I was
> in full armor.  My horse was ahead of the troops, I was way
> out in front of the rest of them, and I was galloping full
> tilt, my goodness my horse was going fast, and I raised my
> sword, as if I were rallying the people on the horses behind
> me.  What do you 'spose that means?"
> 
> Saturday, January 25th Gladys presented me (with the help
> of Clarice Dorian) with a birthday card and flowers in
> a beautiful pink sea shell.
> 
> Tuesday, January the 28th Gladys watched the news about
> the space shuttle Challenger explosion.  Her only comment
> was a weak shake of her head at the tragedy. Wednesday,
> January 29th she had another transfusion which made her a
> little stronger.  Tuesday, February 4th she had a very long
> personal talk with me.  She was able to talk that night for
> about two hours.  February 5th her kidneys stopped.  She had
> Clarice call to tell me she didn't want any more Cayce
> treatments.  Bruce Baar had been sending the carbon ash.
> I felt badly that she didn't tell me in person but I kept it
> to myself and from this point on all she received was beef
> juice supplied fresh every three days by Ann Clapp and fresh
> [[Florida|Florida]] orange juice.
> 
> February 6th the Hickman catheter was removed, no more daily
> blood samples were taken and Gladys had to be catherized.  She
> had developed an infection, her throat was very sore, she was
> hoarse and it was hard to swallow.  At one point in the late
> afternoon she roused and spoke and her voice sounded like
> water running over rocks in a stream.  I thought "why this
> happened to Mr. Cayce too."  She would sleep and waken
> briefly and about 7:00 P.M. that night she mentioned a
> "pretty light" she could see in the room.
> 
> Friday, February 7th Gladys had her "good bye" day.  She had
> ultrasound in the morning and asked if I knew where Per
> Madsen's room was.  She had remembered he was on his way into
> the hospital for surgery at this same time.  Vic Reiffer flew
> in from Pennsylvania bringing a white rose from his son
> [[Joshua|Joshua]].  They sang I'LL BE SEEING YOU.  Before Vic had to
> go Gladys said, "tell Josh...I'll be seeing you in all the

>

> "Oh, Nell Clairemont or Marjorie Bonney.  We'll see how I
> feel."
> 
> Saturday, February 8th and Sunday February 9th Gladys was
> very ill and uncomfortable.  Saturday night the nursing
> staff found it necessary to restrain her to prevent her
> trying to get out of bed at night and injuring herself.
> Sunday afternoon, surrounded by her nieces and nephews,
> she spoke clearly and distinctly in an unknown language.
> She was coherent for a brief minute or two and seemed to
> be asking emphatically for something.  She repeated what
> she had said because we didn't respond to her and the
> sounds were the same.  Her niece Ann finally removed a
> pillow that had been placed between her knees and at that
> Gladys nodded in satisfaction and immediately fell asleep.
> 
> At 11:00 A.M. on Monday, February the 10th Gladys was given
> a dose of thorazine to quiet her and she was not conscious
> after that.
> 
> Tuesday evening, February 11th I was in [[Prayer|prayer]] while Gladys
> was sleeping. For some reason I got up and turned on the
> radio.  This was unusual as we never had the radio or
> [[Television (Atlantean)|television]] on unless Gladys requested it. I became aware
> that a song was being played repeatedly. It was WE'LL MEET
> AGAIN:
> 
> "We'll meet again, don't know where, don't know when, we'll
> meet again some sunny day.  Keep smiling thru, just like you
> always do til the blue skies drive the dark clouds far away.
> So will you please say hello, to the folks that I know, tell
> them I won't be long.  They'll be happy to know that as
> you saw me go, I was singing this song..."  I remember
> saying aloud, "Thank you Gladys, I understand, I'll tell
> them."
> 
> Wednesday, February the 12th Clarice sat in a chair on Gladys'
> right holding her wrist to take her pulse and blood pressure.
> I sat on the left side of the bed with my right arm under her
> shoulders speaking to her softly.  At precisely 10:00 P.M.
> her forehead wrinkled just the tinest bit and she did not
> breathe again.  The room was suffused with a sense of
> joyousness.  For a brief instant there was a feeling of
> indescribable love and laughter, almost like the feeling
> of a glorious party.  I looked at Clarice to see if she
> noticed but she was writing on the chart.
> 
> While the nurses did their work I called people to tell
> them she was gone.  I sat for a while in the empty room
> feeling as if she had gone off to some wonderful place
> and I was left behind.
> 
> The names of those who visited, helped in various ways and
> sent messages of comfort are all recorded in the archives.
> [[Grace|Grace]] Huffman, Micki Kluge, Bob Adriance, Ann Clapp, Erik
> Jensen are only a few.  I know of no one who inspired such
> affection and respect as this lady did. There will never be
> anyone in my life again like Gladys.
> 
> Sincerely,
