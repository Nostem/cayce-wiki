## Reports

> [!quote]- Reports & Follow-up
> R1. 1/22/21 Brother's letter:
> 
> The [[Alabama|Birmingham]] News
> The South's Greatest Newspaper
> [[Alabama|Birmingham]], Ala.
> 
> This is to certify that the writer, A. B. Bealle, associate
> editor of The [[Alabama|Birmingham]] News, has personal knowledge
> of several cases in which Mr. Edgar Cayce has revealed
> remarkable [[Clairvoyance|clairvoyant]] powers:  (1) In the case of his
> sister, Miss Estelle Bealle [4958], whose complication of
> ailments, with which Mr. Cayce was utterly unfamiliar, was
> accurately diagnosed by him while sleeping.  Although Mr.
> Cayce had never seen my sister, he described her troubles and
> prescribed a remedy, or treatment, which she is now taking.
> The remarkable part of this diagnosis is that while the
> writer was present at this reading Mr. Cayce described a
> certain dislocation of the spinal column about which I, the
> patient's brother, knew nothing; but the reading was verified
> later.  (2) In a reading given Dr. James E. Tinder, a
> Birmingham optometrist.  Mr. Cayce diagnosed and prescribed
> a remedy for a child of five years old [4956] who had been
> given up by the physicians.  Under this treatment the child
> is now in flourishing health.  (3) In another reading the
> wife [4955] of Dr. Tinder was advised to undergo an
> operation.  In his [[Clairvoyance|clairvoyant]] condition, Mr. Cayce described
> a previous operation to which Mrs. Tinder was subjected, but
> none of the auditors knew anything of the malady until Dr.
> Tinder himself, who was not present, verified the truth of
> the reading.  (4) While in Birmingham several months ago, and
> at the time of his reading for my sister, he cured a serious
> case of shell-shock which had puzzled the local medical
> fraternity.  Under his treatment, the young man [4957] (?) is
> perfectly normal.  (5) Another marvellous reading was that
> of a lady confined in the Norwood hospital, who had been
> stricken by paralysis of all her limbs.  The doctors had
> confessed their inability to give her any relief.  But in
> a reading accorded this lady, Mr. Cayce advised electric
> [[Massage Therapy|massage]] treatment.  In a very short while, the patient was in
> full possession of her limbs and is now perfectly restored.
> 
> The writer is confident that Mr. Cayce has remarkable
> [[Psychic Development|psychic powers]].  The only explanation I can make of these
> extraordinary phenomena is that he is the agent of some
> beneficent power outside the physical domain.
> 
> Respectfully,  [signed] A. B. Bealle [495]  Associate Editor
> The News
> 
> Subscribed and sworn to before me this 22nd day of January,
> 1921.
> 
> [signed and sealed] W. L. Fullerton  Notary Public.
> 
> R2. 7/7/21 Dr. Ventura Williams' (D.C.) letter to EC:
> 
> 
> J. H. DOWNING, M.D.
> [[Chiropractic|CHIROPRACTOR]] AND [[Osteopathy|OSTEOPATH]]
> 514-15-16 LYRIC BUILDING
> BIRMINGHAM, ALA.
> 
> Mr. Edgar Cayce
> [[New York City|New York City]],
> 
> Dear Mr. Cayce:-
> 
> As I am the [[Chiropractic|chiropractor]] who has been treating Miss Estelle
> Bealle [4958] - sister of Mr. Bealle of the Birmingham News,
> I feel very much interested in the reading you gave Miss
> Bealle during your recent visit to this city.  You diagnosed
> her case just as we had done and mentioned the subluxations
> of the cervical and dorsal regions of the spine just as
> we find them.  I think both readings you gave Miss Bealle
> perfectly wonderful.
> 
> Mr. Cayce, I wonder if it would be possible for you to give
> me a reading for Dr. J. H. Downing (James Henry).  He's
> the Dr. in charge of this Sanitarium.  He resides at 1112
> Princeton Avenue, West End, Birmingham, Alabama and may I
> request that you have a stenographer present at the time of
> reading.
> 
> Let me assure you that if you can give a few moments of your
> most valuable time it will be appreciated very greatly.
> 
> Yours very sincerely  (Miss) Ventura Williamson
> 
> [GD's note:  There is no correspondence on file re the above
> and no reading for Dr. Downing on record.  This does not mean
> he did not get one.  However, I am sure EC did not give one
> unless he, himself, asked for it.]
> 
> R3. 2/28/37 Mr. [370]'s letter to EC:
> 
> 
> ..., Ala.
> 
> Mr. Dear Mr. Cayce,
> 
> I have an urge to write you at this days climax.  I began
> the day after a day and night very much under par for me
> physically but bent on doing an errand to headquarters
> pertaining to my daily toil - with sprinkling rain about me -
> (that is dreary without the physical grunts).  Dropping into
> the office of our county Commissioner W. E. Dickson with whom
> I enjoy spending some pleasant visits when at the Courthouse,
> - I was led into pleasant conversation with his agreeable old
> maid secretary and found I had unsuspectingly, entered into
> deep and earnest conversations on health and psychic subjects
> or [[Soul|soul]] subjects, - and about Edgar Cayce.
> 
> When I came to I found her observing about her surprise that
> I should be interested in such things and to find that we
> both had enjoyed so brief a hour and half and to find that I
> was so much elevated in spirit that my GRUNTS and the weather
> were forgotten.  I hurried to another office before the noon
> hour should pass for what I expected to be a brief call on a
> gentleman I knew only passively but very respectfully - and
> thence to leave a small group's contribution to flood relief.
> Ensued there as I stood before his desk a seemingly inspired
> conversation on Tennessians [Tennesseeans?] and on through
> his experience with their pretty women and squirrel shooting
> men - on through the World War when he was Captain of
> Engineers.  The luster of his eyes (and very watery at times)
> were expressive of whole heartedness, sincerity and courage.
> Another hour gone so quickly, - yes one o'clock sure enough!
> and I hadn't sit down.  Joyfully I went about some small
> trading in town and home.  There I found my son [640] and
> wife [1344] expectant and solicitous in a very agreeable way.
> Also a large envelope with [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the association]] bulletin and a
> copy of [[Thomas Sugrue|There is a River]] [6/15/36 [[Thomas Sugrue|Thomas Sugrue]]'s booklet,
> preface to the book later entitled the same.  See under
> 1800-26 Reports, GC's autographed copy] was awaiting me.
> I read it all before meal time.
> 
> I felt a thrill and appreciation of "[[Thomas Sugrue|There is a River]]" many
> times more intense then when I read it before.  It was like
> reading it new entirely.  I have since been heaping praises
> and thanksgiving for you in my mind since reading.  And I
> have learned a greater appreciation of the writer.  The world
> truly is a great place to live in with such [[Soul|souls]] in it.  But
> what I say is not complete and I cannot neglect to complete
> the chain without [[Hugh Lynn Cayce|Hugh Lynn Cayce]].
> 
> I must go back to the old maid secretary [4958] of Com.
> Dickson's.  She was taking your address pursuant to writing
> you about a membership.  Before this she has impressed me as
> skeptical on things spiritual.  I had never had so complete
> an opportunity to talk to her as today.  She claims her
> sister is very much interested in psychic matters but I
> believe you will hear from her.  She had a reading some years
> ago from you but did not act on it faithfully.  She mentioned
> her father as having some connection with you.  He is now
> dead.  His name was [...].  Her name Miss [4958], ... So.
> ... St. B'ham.  If she writes give her all the encouragement
> you can.  I hope she becomes an active member.
> 
> I appreciate your personal letter and the one from Mr.
> Johnson [Douglas T. Johnston's letter in re [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|A.R.E.]] membership
> & expansion program - See 1800- 29 Reports].  I am glad to
> see [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the association]] begin this mode of activity.  It conforms
> to my wishes, it seems like an answer to [[Prayer|prayer]].  You know
> I one time dreamed that I could relieve the finances of the
> work with OIL.  But it did not materialize.  I feel now that
> some will have a better chance to help some [[Soul|soul]] along the
> way in a different manner and that will be better than all
> the money I dreamed of.
> 
> I hope you will over look my short comings in this rambling
> letter.  The cheerful thing about it all is that I too can
> claim you as MY FRIEND even as the author of There is a
> River.  And THAT is the strongest thing he says in his
> PREFACE.  I will be happy to see his completed volume
> when he crowds what he can say down between two lids.
> 
> Remember me faithfully and Sincerely  [370]
> 
> R4. 3/4/37 EC's letter to [370]:
> 
> 
> Dear Mr. [370]:-
> 
> Happy to have yours of the 28th, and to know about your
> conversation with some that have known of the work years ago.
> Yes, remember Miss Bealle [4958] very well.  Of course, every
> one knows of Battle Bealle and his work on the Bhm News,
> thought a great deal of him, think he was one of the best
> editorial writers I ever knew and will be very glad to hear
> again from Miss Estelle, should she write, have a great many
> letters from Mr. Bealle, as well as many clippings that he
> wrote one time or another in the News.
> 
> A Mr. Dickson, was once a member of a Court Reporters group
> who wrote many of the readings while I was in Bhm, at one
> time or another.  May be of same family, don't know, any
> way am glad you have had the opportunity to talk with others
> relative to the work, it brings back many memories those
> names.  You possibly know that I spent about six months there
> at the Tutwiler hotel, giving from two to three readings
> every day.
> 
> We have some of the booklets, they have been called "Cayce"
> booklets just off press, and will a bit later have another
> the Assn. Booklet.  Will send you as many of these as you
> wish, or that you can use to good [[Purpose|purpose]].  "There is a
> river" is written beautifully, and the author is supposed to
> have the complete volume ready by or before Summer.  Thanks
> for the contribution, am anxious to know what Mrs. [1344]
> thinks of her reading.  We have had another case here in
> Norfolk very similar to your own, the Dr's were about ready
> to put the man on the shelf, as he is a Gov. man but he has
> gotten well.  His Blood pressure is normal again, and he is
> to be sure back on the [[Book of Job|job]] with all the vim and vitality of
> a new man.  Hope you are keeping on the improve, and thanks
> many many times Mr. [370] for your letters, they do me lots
> of good.  Thanks too for joining in with the Assn. plans, and
> for all the nice things you say about all here with the work.
> We may be the means, the channel, but it is either God's
> work, or it is nothing at all.
> 
> Write every chance, and when you feel like it.  With best to
> you and yours, Sincerely, EC
