# TEXT OF READING 5179-1 F 20 (Catholic)

**Reading [[5179-1]]** · Series: 3001-5747 · Year: 1944 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 5/24/1944  Sex: F  Age: 20  ReadingID: 13315

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at the office of [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]], Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 24th day of May, 1944, in accordance with request made by the father, Mr. [...], new Associate Member of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc., recommended by the article in Coronet.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]] and Jeanette Fitch, Stenos.]

Time of Reading 
Set bet. 3:30 to 4:30 P.M. Eastern War Time. ..., Canal Zone.

1. GC:  You will go over this body carefully, examine it thoroughly, and tell me the conditions you find at the present time; giving the cause of the existing conditions, also suggestions for help and relief of this body; answering the questions, as I ask them:

2. EC:  Yes, we have the body here, [5179].

3. Much might be given as to the sources or causes of the conditions which exist physically with this body, but animosities, hates, prejudices, disturbance between individuals who have had and do have the control of the body have brought about much which has prevented, or may in the present prevent, betterment of conditions.

4. It is not, though, wholly necessary that this body should remain immobile or unable to use the locomotory centers.  It will require, to be sure, time, patience, persistence, faith, hope, love; all of these must be made manifest by those making administrations.  Not that it is not manifested in the body itself; for, some neglect has caused or allowed the creation of those conditions in the body which have brought that type of fever or of a form of strep which has been more recently existent in the body.  Yet, patiently the body tries, attempts to meet those conditions.  Lack of sympathy. All of these are needed most for this body.  Hope, patience, persistence, in love.

5. If there will be the manifestations of these, then add through mechanical means the low [[Wet Cell|Wet Cell Appliance]] which would carry Gold with charged [[Atomidine|Atomidine]] or [[Iodine|Iodine]] in solution.  We would, with the use of this for some three years, bring the body so it would be able to control the locomotories of the body, especially the lower limbs, though they are deformed in the present.

6. If these will be prepared, prepared in this manner:  Use the regular solution for the [[Wet Cell|Wet Cell Appliance]], the gallon and a half in a two gallon crock, using rain water or distilled water with the solutions.  The poles in these are to be nickel and copper.  Prepare with the regular charge, both the Copper Sulphate and the Zinc and acids to suspend in the solution.  Suspend in same at least three ounces of Tincture of [[Iodine|Iodine]].  Put this on low electric charge for at least thirty minutes, just as if charging a battery.

7. Then make those connections which would be applied:  The larger nickel plate would be attached to the umbilical and lacteal duct plexus, which on this body would be three fingers from the navel center to the right and three fingers up from that point.  The small copper plate, attach to the 4th lumbar or at the 4th lumbar and the basic axis.  This is to be given each day for thirty minutes.

8. The solution of [[Gold Chloride|Chloride of Gold]] Sodium is to be used in the proportions of one grain to one ounce of distilled water, using three ounces.

9. Use this regularly, of course, but have periods of rest. Recharge the Appliance every thirty days, and also change the [[Gold Chloride|Gold Solution]].  Charge Appliance the same with Tincture of [[Iodine|Iodine]] in same.  With the use of these in application, and then a gentle massage given daily with Cocoa Butter part of the time, and at other times using Olive Oil and Peanut Oil through the areas from the 9th dorsal downward, we would bring the abilities of the body to be free from being an invalid and one who may take care of its own activities.

10. Do, through the periods of the massage, use this time for [[Meditation|meditation]] and hope of [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]] as to what it will do with its abilities when there are the abilities for [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]] to carry about itself, to carry on in its desires, and we will bring not only joy for this body, but influences which may be brought by the activity of [[Entity (Cayce Term)|this entity]] not only in its work but in the very joy of living in others.

11. For there are abilities in the body for art, the higher arts, music and art itself.  These would bring the greater abilities in the love, the patience, the persistence for this body and springing out, as it were, of the very [[Soul|soul]] of the entity in activities.

12. Do that.

13. We are through with this reading.

Two copies to the Father (one to be sent to whomever is to be responsible for carrying out the treatments suggested) Copy to Ass'n file "   for indexing

(See extra sheet of directions [which was enclosed] to be sent to Mr. Godfrey; also article explaining theory of this Appliance.)

## Background

> [!note]- Background
> B1. Born 11/23/23 in [[New York City|New York]]; residence in N.Y.; single;
> divorced parents.
> 
> B2. 10/23/43 Father's letter from overseas:  "...I have a
> daughter who has been a cripple since birth.  I have just
> received a letter written for the child by her mother, dated
> 10/19, but postmarked 10/10/43 [copied from father's typed
> letter; apparently inaccuracy in dates] which I am enclosing
> [not in E.C.F. records].  The child will be 20 years old in
> November.  According to the diagnosis of specialists, her
> trouble is caused by having the central nerve injured at
> birth (breech).  She has had a teacher come to her home, and
> has learned to use a typewriter fairly well, and has written
> a few short stories.  The child has never taken a step,
> and has poor use of her arms and hands, but has shown great
> stamina in conquering her disability against all odds, her
> speech is difficult to understand.  Perhaps this will be too
> late, and she may have already gone to our Maker.  When I
> last heard from her, she was at ..., N.Y.  If you are able to
> do anything in the child's behalf and will let me know what
> the fee amounts to, I will start paying each month what I can
> spare payroll deductions and expenses incurred for the child.
> If you are able to aid the child, please advise her aunt...,
> N.J., as I am divorced and the mother is a devout Catholic;
> in 1934 she refused to let the child go to a Shrine Hospital
> which might have aided her..."
> 
> B3. 11/30/43 Letter to father from [5179]:  "After giving the
> machine [typewriter] a good rest, thinking it had outlived
> its usefulness, I took it out today, and found to my surprise
> that it still works.  I know you must be very worried about
> not hearing from me in so long.  I am sorry if I have caused
> you any distress.  I have steadily improved until now I can
> honestly say that I never felt better in all my life.  I
> realize that the letters Mom wrote for me did not satisfy
> you, but at the time nothing else could be done.  Both my
> brother and I were disappointed not hearing from you for our
> birthdays.  I guess you just forgot, eh, dad?  Well, I guess
> that's all I have to say, except please write soon."
> 
> [Comment by IMG:  "The original letter was very well typed,
> paragraphs and margins in line.  Signature in pencil, was not
> legible."]
> 
> B4. 12/8/43 Father's comments re [5179]'s letter of 11/30/43:
> "...while her speech is difficult to understand, she is
> really exceptionally intelligent and bright.  During her
> relapse which happened about the time I saw your name in
> 'Coronet', she was out of her mind, so this letter gives
> me great hope, and I feel sure she will answer your letter
> when you write her for whatever you desire in the way of
> cooperation...  I would greatly appreciate it if you write
> her, but suggest that you also send a copy of the letter to
> my sister...  I have received a letter from Mrs. H. A. H,
> and she is awaiting any word from you to cooperate, as far as
> possible with my daughter.  The child's mother works, and at
> present I do not know just what home arrangements are made
> for the child.  She was to have gone to a hospital, but
> as she must have constant care and there were no nurses
> available to give her the necessary attention, as she
> cannot feed herself or take care of herself in any way..."
> 
> B5. 1/20/44 First enclosed excerpt from [5179]'s letters to her
> father:  "Aunt I. R. E. showed me the letter from Mr. Caise
> [Cayce], telling me that he could not give me a reading until
> May.  As I don't have faith in Mr. Caise and as the Church
> forbids any doings with spiritualism, I do not intend to
> cooperate with him.  I appreciate your efforts because I know
> you have nothing but my best interests at heart.  We just
> have to continue to trust God and consult legitimate doctors
> instead of spiritualists who, most probably, are in league
> with [[Fallen Angels|the devil]]."
> 
> B6. 2/11/44 [5179]'s second letter to her father:  "Aunt I. R. E.
> has showed me the last letter which you wrote concerning Mr.
> Caise and the reading he promised May 24.  I must say, dad,
> that on reading the letter I was rather angered.  In it you
> did not compliment mother or me.  In fact, I might go as far
> as to say that you insulted us.  It is quite evident that you
> are sold on Mr. Caise and his unexplainable powers.  I read
> the article from 'Coronet' which you sent Aunt, and I must
> admit it made for fascinating reading.  There is one point
> however, that I do not understand; in the article, it said
> that Mr. Caise has refused many offers to become wealthy
> feeling that he should use his gifts to the benefit of others
> without thought of recompense.  Yet you had to join his
> society (whose fee is ten dollars a year) before he gave you
> a look-in.  How do you explain that?  I am very hurt by what
> you said about mother's standing in my way.  She has never
> done so; in fact she has done everything to help me and
> considering she did it alone as well as caring for J. A. Y.,
> I think she has done a wonderful [[Book of Job|job]].  Mother has taught me
> to think clearly, and I think I have learned well.  As I told
> you in my last letter I do not intend to cooperate with Mr.
> Caise in any way and why.  The Church absolutely forbids us
> to have anything to do with spiritualism or anything like
> it.  That is reason enough for me.  So please don't go
> around saying mother is standing in my way."
> 
> B7. 3/9/44 [5179]'s third letter to her father:  "There's not
> much to write.  I got the book about Mr. Cayce which I have
> read and found very interesting.  I am still undecided as
> to his legitimacy, but I intend to consult my confessor
> next week."
> 
> B8. 5/3/44 Father's letter:  "...I am also enclosing extracts
> from 3 letters I received from my daughter [5179], and
> delayed sending this until I had received her last letter;
> the poor child has been sick with a light touch of scarlet
> fever, but she said she felt better than ever, neglecting to
> mention whether she had consulted her confessor or not.  I
> answered her letter and asked her to please cooperate with
> you at the time set for her reading, and trust that she will
> do so.  You can see by the letters she writes me that she
> completely influenced by her mother and an older aunt that
> lives with them, so does not have much chance for free
> thinking for herself.  If you are unable to accomplish
> anything in her behalf, it will be no fault of yours,
> for you have done your part..."
