> THE TRIUMPHANT CAREER OF A HINDESE SCIENTIST
> 
> WE HEARTILY CONGRATULATE THE EMINENT HINDESE SCIENTIST,
> S. BISEY, UPON HIS COMPLETION OF HALF A CENTURY'S RECORD AS
> A SCIENTIST AND INVENTOR.  A SELF-MADE MAN, SUNKAR BISEY HAS
> SET A NOBLE EXAMPLE WORTHY OF EMULATION BY THE YOUTH OF INDIA
> AND OF THE WORLD.  HIS STORY DESERVES TO BE KNOWN FAR AND
> WIDE. - EDITOR (Haridas T. Muzumdar, Ph.D.)
> 
> To The Editor of "India Today and Tomorrow"
> 20 Vesey Street
> New York, N.Y.
> 
> Dear Sir:
> 
> Permit me to bring to the attention of the readers of your
> esteemed paper the following autobiographical sketch of a
> humble worker in behalf of Science, Truth and Humanity.
> 
> It is fifty years this month of May, 1933, that a certain
> incident came into my life that made me turn a new leaf and
> resolve to pursue my own career as a scientist and to
> endeavor to rise in this world and visit foreign lands,
> depending solely on my own efforts rather than to accept
> the financial help cheerfully offered by my wealthy father.
> And I am happy to say I fulfilled my ambition and met with
> a great deal of success in spite of handicaps.
> 
> In the year 1883 I was in India at Dhulia, in Bombay
> Presidency, where my father was a Civil Judge for the
> District.  I was then sixteen years of age and attending
> High School.  My father was indeed very kind to me and
> showed me great affection.  Being the eldest son, he
> naturally wished me to study law and to qualify myself
> for a good position in the Judicial Department with his
> influence.  But, fortunately or unfortunately, my tendencies
> were all toward mechanical and scientific work from my
> earliest years.
> 
> In those days of half a century ago, in India, it was
> considered unbecoming for the son of a dignified District
> Judge to dabble with mechanics, carpentry and such
> craftsmanship and so my father was naturally greatly
> disappointed to note my tendencies, although he never
> interfered with my experimental work nor declined to give me
> money to purchase materials and things I needed.
> 
> Sir R. V. Subnis, Ex-Prime Minister of Kolhapur State, was
> then Head Master of The Dhulia High School, and being an
> old friend of my father, used to visit us often.  Whilst
> I was studying in his High School at Dhulia, he gave me
> the Scientific American to read, knowing my tendencies
> toward scientific subjects he once presented me with a
> small chemical apparatus to carry on some interesting
> experiments with it.  Reading of the Scientific American,
> and particularly the interesting account of the Late
> Thomas A. Edison's inventions, filled me with the ambitious
> desire of becoming a scientist like him and some day go to
> America and meet him.  In fact, I began to look upon America
> as my dreamland.  Mr. Subnis admired my unusual scientific
> tendencies and often encouraged me.
> 
> In the month of May, 1883, fifty years ago, a peculiar
> incident took place which I owe my success.  One Sunday
> morning Mr. Subnis came to consult my father on some matter.
> My father then asked me to bring some papers which he had
> told me previously to keep ready but my mind being engrossed
> with some experimental work, I had altogether forgotten
> to carry out his instructions.  He was then naturally very
> angry with me and blamed me severely in the presence of
> Mr. Subnis about my negligence and for wasting my time
> doing useless experimental work instead of enjoying the
> summer vacation or studying as other boys did.  That being
> the first time my father had been so angry with me, I was
> much hurt.
> 
> Mr. Subnis then advised my father to encourage me for my
> scientific turn of mind and declared that some day I would
> be a renowed scientist and inventor if properly encouraged.
> After that my father, believing in Mr. Subnis's advice,
> never spoke a harsh word to me and left me alone to carry
> out my work.
> 
> But my father's blaming me in the presence of my teacher and
> showing his dislike for my scientific turn of mind had a
> different effect on my sensitiveness.  I went to my mother
> and cried bitterly.  I did not realize then that my revered
> father meant well.  I misjudged him instead and thought that
> as I often troubled him for giving me money for my
> experimental work he disliked such work.  In the heat of the
> moment I took an oath before my mother never to ask any
> more financial aid from my father for my scientific work
> and made a firm resolution to make my own way in scientific
> research, to visit America and to meet Mr. Edison some day.
> I resolved always to rely on my own work for finances.  That
> day I turned a new leaf in my life and gladly stuck to my
> resolution ever since through thick and thin, for the last
> half century and fulfilled my ambition.
> 
> I begged my mother not to tell my father about my resolution,
> as I did not wish to hurt his feelings.  For the first five
> or six years, it was difficult for me to meet expenses and
> many a time I had to go without luncheon at school to save
> money out of my weekly allowance.  After finishing high
> school I entered the Government Service to earn funds to
> carry out my scientific work.  When I made some little
> success with it, and began to attract public attention, my
> father was happy and expressed a desire to send me to England
> to learn some technical art or science.
> 
> I was then obliged to remind him of the incident which took
> place some seven years previously and respectfully declined
> his kind offer.  He was surprised and felt sorry about it,
> and tried to persuade me to break my boyish resolution and
> accept the offer of his help.  But I requested him to
> overlook my disobedience and not tempt me with his offer.
> 
> I was feeling happy in what I was doing and felt justly proud
> of my resolution which was teaching me a valuable lesson in
> self-reliance while setting an example for some of the sons
> of rich men to follow.  My father understood my attitude and
> felt happy and proud about it.
> 
> My father passed away in 1893.  In 1895 I accepted an offer
> to go to England where I spent six months and exhibited some
> of my scientific optical illusions invented by me and many
> English papers spoke highly about them.  In 1897 H. H.
> Sayajirao, Gaikwar of Baroda, gave me a valuable present for
> my scientific work at a special Durbar.  In 1898 I won a
> prize in England completing with several European inventors
> for inventing an automatic weighing machine.  It being the
> first time for a Hindu to win such a competition in England,
> I was hailed as an inventive genius.  Several prominent
> citizens of Bombay then urged me to resign my Government
> service and go to England as a pioneer Hindu inventor and
> scientist to prove to the world that the Hindus did not lack
> inventive faculty.
> 
> My mother strongly opposed my going abroad.  She could not
> understand why I should forsake a comfortable home, a young
> wife and a son and a promising Government position to under-
> take a risky [[Book of Job|job]] in foreign lands.  But I realized the
> importance of what seemed to me a national mission.
> I gladly answered the call to do my duty to my motherland,
> and came to England in 1899 and since then have been carrying
> out such work.
> 
> For sixteen years I carried out my work in England in which
> the late Hon. Dadabhai Naoroji, Sir Ratan Tata and Hon.
> G. K. Gokhale took a keen interest.  I invented type-casting
> machines on original lines and as I solved some intricate
> problems unsolved by European inventors I was called the
> Edison of India.  In 1908 I was honoured by the Indian
> National Industrial Congress at Madras for my scientific
> work.  Since 1916 I have been permanently established in
> this country.  I completed the type-casting machines.
> The Scientific American published an illustrated article
> about these machines in August, 1917, and the Inland Printer
> and other technical papers also published illustrated
> articles.
> 
> Besides the type-casting machines I put on the market an
> iodine compound known as Atomidine (atomic iodine).  It is
> acknowledged to be the only water soluble, non-poisonous,
> non-irritating, odorless compound of iodine known to science
> that has also the unique property of helping the growth of
> healthy tissue.  It is the result of ten years of my research
> work and is prescribed by thousands of physicians and
> dentists in this country and abroad.  When in December, 1930,
> the late Thomas A. Edison granted me a special interview and
> presented me with an autographed [[Photography (Atlantean)|photograph]] I felt that I had
> fulfilled my ambitions completely.
> 
> Among my several handicaps I was a native of India and my
> Hindu race was considered backward in science and invention.
> In fact, the prevalent impression was that the inventive
> faculty was not a natural heritage of the Hindu race, an
> erroneous impression which my work considerably helped to
> remove.  I had to carry out all my work in foreign lands
> with scanty capital and meantime I had to support my wife
> and children also in foreign lands.
> 
> Like other inventors, I had my share of uphill work, but
> being in foreign lands it was even harder.  Now, after 50
> years' labors, I am well established in this country, the
> land of my dreams, my work is known internationally, and
> my name referred to in the "Who's Who in America" and other
> reference books.  I feel, therefore, justly proud of my
> record as a self-made man coming from 'backward' India and
> establishing as a scientist and inventor in this leading
> land of the world.
> 
> In 1883 while in India, I used to read the interesting
> account of the opening of the new Brooklyn Bridge, the
> first to connect Brooklyn with New York.  This year -
> this very month of May, they are commemorating the fiftieth
> anniversary of that Bridge on a lavish scale and I am also
> commemorating the fiftieth anniversary of the completion
> of the record of my struggle, in my own humble way.  As the
> fates would have it, there is some sort of similarity between
> the two events, happening about the same time.  The Brooklyn
> Bridge was the first gigantic engineering enterprise of its
> kind and met many obstacles, but it succeeded in bringing
> Brooklyn so close to New York that it became part of the
> great metropolis.
> 
> The success of my work as a pioneer in foreign lands was much
> to be doubted.  There have been many instances of people in
> poor circumstances rising to a position in the world through
> hard work.  But my task was a self-imposed one.  I refused
> financial help cheerfully offered to me, so people thought
> I would soon come to my senses, when I met hard times.
> 
> In spite of handicaps I have succeeded in partly bridging
> the vast gulf between the East and the West through my work
> as a pioneer inventor and scientist of India, and I am very
> happy to be established in this, my dream land, America.
> 
> Born in Bombay on the 29th of April, 1867, at 4:36 A.M., I
> am in my sixty-seventh year.  I am thankful to Providence
> for having kept me in sound health, for having given me the
> courage to stick to my early resolution to fight my way to
> success in three continents and for having preserved my life
> while crossing the [[Submarines (Atlantean)|submarine]]-infested Atlantic three times
> during the world war.  On one such occasion, I am grateful
> to say, in 1918, Providence miraculously saved my life and
> that of my wife and children when our steamer in crossing
> the Atlantic barely eluded the torpedo fired from a [[Germany|German]]
> [[Submarines (Atlantean)|submarine]].  We landed safely at New York on July 4th,
> America's Day of Independence.
> 
> I am also thankful to my friends in India, in England, in
> America and elsewhere, for their kind help and cooperation
> toward the fulfillment of my pioneer mission.
> 
> Sincerely yours, SUNKAR A. BISEY

> LOTUS PHILOSOPHY OF LIFE
> 
> Leading to an Universal Religion of Truth, Reason and
> Understanding
> 
> By Sunkar A. Bisey
> 
> "There is nothing higher, more precious or pious than
> Knowledge." - Bhagavid-Gita.
> 
> "Ye shall know the Truth and Truth shall make thee free."
> - [[Jesus Christ|Jesus Christ]].
> 
> It is truly said that knowledge is power and truth opens
> the gateway to freedom.  During the last half century our
> store of knowledge has greatly been enriched by the enormous
> strides made by electricity, radio, chemistry, spiritism
> and other sciences, the rapid spread of education among
> the masses and backward classes, the easy intercourse and
> and exchange of views among all the nations of the world,
> equality of rights granted to women, freedom of speech and
> thought on religious and social subjects and their bearings
> on the scientific discoveries in various parts of the world.
> 
> The power gained through such knowledge has set even the
> orthodox people to take a broader, more enlightened and
> charitable view of the teachings of their own as well as
> other religions of the world.  The blind faith in believing
> the teachings of the priests and ministers and their
> explanation or version of the deeper meaning of the mystical
> writings of their respective religions or philosophies,
> is fast dying away and every religion is slowly but steadily
> losing its hold even on orthodox people.
> 
> There are six principal religions of the world.  Three
> in the Eastern Hemisphere, namely - the vedants and Hindu
> religion; Buddhist and Confucius religion, and in the Western
> Hemisphere the Mose or Jewish religion, the Christian and the
> Mohammedan religion.  The Fatherhood of God and brotherhood
> of mankind, universal love, peace, justice, faith and
> tolerance were the fundamental principles and cherished
> [[Ideal|ideals]] of the above named principal religions as they came
> originally from the same fountain source.  Although thousands
> of years have passed since their foundation, their teachings
> have failed to get the desired deep rooting in the minds of
> people and owing to their constant fighting with each other
> on religious, social and political matters, there is more
> money and energy spent in the world today on destructive and
> warlike measures than constructive or uplifting ones;
> consequently, several nations are at war today and preparing
> for more wars.
> 
> The great World War is over, but as it was principally fought
> among the Christian nations it gave a very rude shock to the
> cherished Christian [[Ideal|ideals]] of "Love thy neighbor" and "Thou
> shalt not kill" and consequently it has set in motion another
> war in the thinking minds as to the truth and value of such
> a religion.  During the World War regiments received the
> blessings in the churches to go to the war to destroy the
> enemy - Christians!  Several priests and ministers went to
> the battle fields with the Bible in one hand and a gun in the
> other and made a mockery of Christ's [[Ideal|Ideals]].  It is the
> priesthood that thus degenerated the true Christ's religion
> and His cherished principles by falling a prey to the
> capitalists' offering of gold.  Like the Christian religion,
> other religions also become more or less degenerate and
> most of the people lose faith in them.  It is, therefore,
> no wonder that earnest thinkers and even some broadminded
> priests and ministers of all religions all over the world,
> are sincerely endeavoring to find a way to unite all the
> principal religions of the world into one universal religion
> to oppose the onward march of atheism by unmasking the
> eternal truth underlying all the religions and arriving
> at a common basis of understanding that will appeal to
> common sense, stand to reason and bring real peace and
> happiness to the world.  Believing that any attempt made
> in that direction would be welcomed; I am emboldened to
> place my philosophical views before free thinkers and
> earnest seekers after the eternal Truth.
> 
> One of the vital problems of today is the finding of a
> religion that will fit in with the age of reason which
> is challenging the old age of faith.
> 
> Any philosophy or religion if [[Destiny|destined]] to be universal
> and readily acceptable must be based on scientific truth,
> must appeal to common sense, and must be free from any
> dogmas.  To do a thing because you are told or taught
> to do so or because others do it, is to do it mechanically
> with a hesitating and fettered mind, but to do it because
> it appeals to your common sense or because you know why you
> are doing it, is to do the same thing intelligently with a
> free mind.  The main object of my philosophy is to tell you
> in a simple way what you should do and why you should do it
> to enlighten your [[Soul|soul]] and achieve the salvation which is
> after all the main aim of all the religions and to explain
> to you how the laws of [[Karma|karma]], [[Destiny|destiny]], [[Reincarnation|re-incarnation]] and
> duty work out; why some religions differ from others on
> certain points and how they were all necessary for the
> progress of humanity at different stages of our evolution,
> and what really happens after death.

> THE NEW ERA
> 
> About a couple of centuries ago the whole world was ruled
> by kings and despots claiming Divine authority.  Gradually
> their despotism began to decline and at present many nations
> are ruled by representatives of the people and within a few
> years the whole world will have republican governments where
> the ruling power will belong to the people.  Thus despotism
> will be replaced by true democracy, men and women will
> have equal rights, and everybody will fully enjoy personal
> liberty of thought and action.  This changing condition
> will be brought about by the liberal and free education
> and scientific knowledge gained by the masses and their
> gradual realization of the eternal truth that is opening
> the gateway to freedom.  Our old theories and mistakes
> or misconstrued ideas about the Hell Fire and the actual
> conditions in the Spirit land, have undergone a great
> change due to the valuable knowledge gained through the
> daring research work carried on by several eminent scientists
> all over the world during recent years.  The world is now
> looking forward to an universal religion of democracy and
> deed and not of dogmas and deceit.
> 
> THE NEW RACE
> 
> The generation about sixty to seventy years ago showed signs
> is dissatisfaction towards the orthodox religions and their
> dogmas, but not being strong enough to openly bolt away from
> their elders and their orthodox views, it outwardly remained
> submissive while inwardly its mind was prepared for drastic
> reforms and consequently gave birth to the present generation
> now about thirty-five to forty years old, which is openly
> revolting from the established church, religion, social
> customs and conventions.  This generation again in its turn
> and with a mind less fettered and more prepared for
> revolutionary reforms, has been bringing into the world a
> new or free race, commencing about one year after the
> breaking of the World War, which in turn will be the parents
> of the so-called sixth race.  Young children belonging to
> this free race and now about sixteen to eighteen years of
> age are far more intelligent and inquisitive than their
> parents at similar age and require to be satisfied with
> sound explanation rather than blind faith.  In the World
> war, - first of its kind in the human history, those that
> lost their lives, realized on their awakening in the spirit
> world, that they were misled by false religious beliefs and
> dogmas and were merely made tools or fodder for guns by the
> cunning, power, greedy capitalists under the pretense of
> patriotism.  So with the spirit of revolt against such
> things and desiring to help humanity with the Divine
> knowledge, many of them re-incarnated in this world to form
> the new free race.  Any new philosophy and religion
> therefore has to deal with the present and more particularly
> with the coming generation, and nothing would satisfy them
> unless it has some scientific backing, and appeal to its
> common sense and intelligent understanding, and it must also
> fit in with the new era.  Mere blind faith will not succeed.
> The time has arrived for a new Messiah to bring to the world
> a common sense, democratic, liberal, scientific, intelligent
> and matter-of-fact philosophy and religion free from dogmas,
> deceit and Hell bogey.
> 
> This is the age of reason which is the highest product of
> our evolution.  Many scientific men turn agnostic to the
> belief in God because He does not measure to their yardstick.
> They forget one thing, however, that they can measure
> physical things with physical means, but Divine and abstract
> things must be measured with a mind clear, unbiased and
> impartial.  It is necessary to use a powerful microscope
> to see the millions of microbes in a drop of water that
> are invisible to the naked eye, and so to know the reality
> of the Divine knowledge one must concentrate with a clear
> mind in [[Meditation|deep meditation]] which acts like a microscope.

> Fifty years of my life I have devoted to scientific research,
> but the more I carry on my scientific research, but the more
> I carry on my scientific work I become convinced in the
> reality of a loving God and His Divine wisdom and the chief
> sources of my knowledge is in [[Meditation|deep meditation]] on Divine
> things.  With a pure mind and desire to know the truth to
> benefit humanity, answers to my [[Prayer|prayers]] are oftentimes given
> to me during meditation or psychic dreaming.  When I made
> up my mind to write my thoughts on philosophy I also made
> up my mind to tell the world the "truth" according to the
> light shown to me, without any hesitation or fear of
> contradiction or even ridicule.

> America is truly a melting pot of all races and religions
> and blessed with scores of broadminded philanthropists who
> have cheerfully and liberally been helping worthy and
> charitable institutions, and undertakings with the noble
> objects of benefitting humanity and increasing its welfare.

> pursuits has convinced me that compared to the European
> countries this country is very materialistic, and perhaps
> my fate has brought me here from England to publish my
> philosophy in this materialistic country to help spiritual
> awakening.  I hope my frank and common sense philosophy

> ADAPTING THE SPIRITUAL THOUGHT OF THE EAST TO THE WESTERN
> MIND
> 
> They say, "East is east and west is west."  The east takes
> one viewpoint of life and the west takes another viewpoint
> of life.  Suppose there is a gold mine, - two gold mines,
> one in the east and one in the west.  The miners work all
> day digging gold.  Hard work.  The eastern man says to
> himself, "well, I have worked the whole day, but my luck
> wasn't to find the gold; perhaps I will find it tomorrow."
> The western man says, "Well, I have looked and haven't found
> any gold; what's the use?"  The eastern man in happy and
> contented.  The eastern man's philosophy is that he is in
> the world just for a little while.  This is a prison life.
> You have come here for a certain [[Destiny|destiny]]; certain [[Purpose|purpose]],
> to learn a good lesson and find out your [[Purpose|purpose]] in life.
> After that is accomplished you leave the world and go to the
> spirit world.
> 
> When I was young, about 21 or 22 years of age, in India,
> I came in contact with a Yogi who took a kindly interest
> in me - took a fancy to me, and I became his disciple.
> The Yogis do not take any money.  They expect devotion and
> sincerity from the disciple.  I was prepared to do that and
> I was accepted.  For six or seven years I worked under him in
> doing my daily work or anything of the kind, how to
> concentrate the mind, how to meditate, and those kinds of
> things.  I completed that training.  Then in 1896 the
> bubonic plague broke out in Bombay, my home town.  It was a
> very infectious, contagious disease, so terrible and so
> contagious that even parents would leave their children
> affected by the plague and run away for the sake of their
> lives; that has actually happened.  So when the plague broke
> out, I was in Bombay.  The people did not know what to do.
> You would find hardly anybody in the town either night or
> day.  Then my Yogi friend told me, "Here boy, here is one
> challenge to prove to the public the power of faith in
> yourself, the power of mind over body.  Go and work with
> those plague-stricken people."  I worked with the plague for
> two and a half years every day, six or seven hours every
> day, in hospitals, in camps, in close contact with
> plague-stricken patients - did not take any medicine, did
> not have any inoculation.  My life was considered in the
> danger zone.  My faith in that science, Yogi Philosophy,
> made my mind confident that I was doing God's work, doing my
> duty as a citizen of Bombay with- out receiving any money
> and so no harm shall come to me.  For two and a half years I
> did not take the plague, which was very catching and
> dangerous.  Why?  Because I had confidence in myself and
> concentration, meditation and [[Prayer|prayer]].  Then you are safe, no
> disease can come to you, no matter how dangerous.  In 1899 I
> left the plague work and went to England as the pioneer
> inventor and scientist of India.
>

> not disturbed, quietly try to hear the very faint inner
> voice.  When you begin to hear the inner voice, and become
> more and more spiritualized, then you forget material things.
