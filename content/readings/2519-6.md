# TEXT OF READING 2519-6 F 33

**Reading [[2519-6]]** · Series: [[2001-3000 — Advanced Metaphysics and Astrology|2001-3000]] · Year: 1930 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 2/17/1930  Sex: F  Age: 33  ReadingID: 9477

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at his office, 115 West 35th Street, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 17th day of February, 1930, in accordance with request made by self and those in charge.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Mrs. Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno. Drs. Lydic and Parker, Mrs. [2519], nurse, and L. B. Cayce.]

Time of Reading                           [[Cayce Hospital|Cayce Hospital]], 
12:05 Noon - Eastern Standard Time.       [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va. 
(Physical Suggestions)

1. EC:  We have the body here, [2519].  This we have had before. Much improvements have come to this body, and with the proper care and with proper precautions taken, the body may now return home.  In precautions, do not overtax self by too much standing on feet, nor too much exercises that require the feet becoming cold, camp, or too much over heating of the body to UNNECESSARY or to ABNORMAL tendencies.  Keep much of that as has been given as for corrective measures, in the manipulations - taken once each week - and with the precautions taken, the body should continue - WILL continue - on the improve.  Know within self, as self always realizes, that there is within self that which may be awakened to make the perfect normal [[Physical Body|physical body]].  We are through with this reading.

Copy to Dr. Lydic (to be delivered to patient on leaving hospital)
"    " Ass'n office
"    " [[Cayce Hospital|Cayce Hospital]]

> R2. 3/11/30 EC's letter to [1180] and [2519]:
> 
> 
> Dear [1180] and Mrs. [2519]-
> 
> I haven't heard from you all since Mrs. [2519] went home.
> I'm just wondering how you are getting along.  I had intended
> to write before this, but of course Mrs. [2519] went off
> while I wasn't here - I didn't even get to tell her goodbye.
> I'm sure that you were glad to have her back at home.  I'm
> sure she was glad to be home.  I'm hoping and trusting that
> you see enough difference in her physical condition to feel
> that the time was well spent, and I hope you and she both
> will continue to feel a real interest in our work and our
> undertaking, and if there is ever any suggestion, or any
> ideas that you would like to offer - why don't hesitate to do
> so.  I'm sure it will be done in the proper way, and I want
> to assure you that the suggestion will be received in the way
> that it is meant; for oftentimes people who have been to an
> institution of this kind are able to judge it better than
> those that are so closely associated with same.  On this line
> I wanted to have a good long talk with Mrs. [2519] before she
> left.  I know it is a new place and has to go through a lot
> of changes, and so forth.  I hope that whenever you find
> the opportunity you will drop in and see us.  I am sure you
> recognize the fact, [1180], that each and everyone here fell
> very much in love with your wife, and I'm hoping she will
> always feel that everyone here thinks of her and loves her a
> great deal.  You know, Old Man, how I've always felt toward
> you.  So you will come to see us whenever you get the
> opportunity, won't you?
> 
> Glad to say that we are all getting along fairly well.
> Nothing particularly new going on now.  We are continuing to
> have a great many more people coming in at the hospital.  The
> work is going along very, very, nicely.
> 
> With very best love from all,
> 
> Ever the same,  [EC:GD]
> 
> R3. 3/13/30 Dr. Lydic's letter to Mrs. [2519]:
> 
> 
> Mrs. [2519]
> ..., [[Ohio|Ohio]]
> 
> Dear Mrs. [2519]
> 
> Very glad to receive your letter of March 7th and the
> suppositories you spoke of are the ones meant.  If I were
> you, I would keep two dozen, and use them every other day
> until six are used; taking the sitz and douches as outlined.
> Repeat in two weeks, if necessary, three or four times.
> 
> I know Dr. Patrick [Tracy M. Patrick, D.O.] quite well.
> He is a very able man and would be glad if you could keep
> in touch with him.  Sorry I have not the pleasure of Dr.
> Roozers [H. H. Rouzer, D.O.] acquaintance.
> 
> I would not strap the hip for more than a week at the time
> with a belt, doing so would weaken the ligaments; but you
> could use it, following a treatment, for two or three days
> until the soreness and pain subsides.  Am sorry you are
> bothered with the arm and side but, as I told you, it
> will take several months to cure a neuritis.
> 
> Things are going quite well - we have sixteen patients, at
> present.  The weather is very lovely.  My wife sends her best
> - she expects to return to [[Ohio|Ohio]] in a couple of weeks.  Hoping
> you continue to improve, and with best personal wishes, I am
> 
> Very sincerely,  Dr. L. A. Lydic  [LAL:SCA]
> 
> R4. 3/19/30 Husband [1180]'s letter:
> 
> 
> ..., [[Ohio|Ohio]]
> 
> My dear Mr. Cayce:-
> 
> Yours of the 11th to hand.  Well so all of you fell in love
> with my wife, I thought some one must be in love from the
> length of time she stayed out there.  But I love her too
> and am glad to have others think the same of her.
> 
> Yes, I can see considerable improvement in her in many ways.
> But her entire right side is very sore, and she has a great
> deal of pain in her right breast, and hip which we do not
> understand.
> 
> Have been taking her to an [[Osteopathy|osteopath]] twice a week but think
> we shall change, as he does not agree with the readings.  He
> seems to think the appendix or intestines are giving trouble,
> as that soreness has been there so long and he suggested that
> if it did not get better soon that it would be advisable to
> have an xray to find out the trouble.  She says if there is
> anything like that the readings would have said so.
> 
> I am getting along pretty good with my well, have a thousand
> feet of solid lime.  Carries water enough that we must drill
> it all wet, making it very slow.  If I do not get a well this
> time I do not know what I will do, as this is the fourth well
> I have drilled since coming home from the West.
> 
> Mrs. [2519] is writing you what she thinks of the hospital.
> 
> Personally, I think it is a wonderful institution and am only
> sorry, I cannot do something for it.  Of course as you say
> it takes time to get a new thing going right, but I think
> you have done real well.
> 
> The bill I owe there worries me a great deal but I will pay
> on it as fast as I can.  When she came we did not think she
> would need to stay more than three or four weeks.
> 
> R5. 3/19/30 [2519]'s letter to EC:
> 
> 
> ..., Ohio
> 
> My dear Mr. Cayce:-
> 
> I realize we have been rather negligent in writing you since
> my return home.  I have had such an awful time with the pain
> along my right side, that I have been rather lazy, manage to
> get [1180]'s breakfast and pack his lunch, and the rest of
> the time I have been having to lie down.  When I came home
> I had planned on doing a lot of work, I started out alright,
> but I soon found out I could not stand it.  I feel much worse
> when I am on my feet a great deal.
> 
> I am sorry I did not get to see you again and bid you
> good-bye before I started home.  I had intended to stay until
> your return from [[New York City|New York]] but when I did not know how long
> you would be away, I thought it best to start home as [1180]
> thought it was about time I was on my way.
> 
> I certainly hated to leave, every one there seemed so near to
> me, it seemed like leaving home.  I shall always have a warm
> place in my heart, for the [[Cayce Hospital|Cayce Hospital]], and every one
> there.  Of course it seemed good to get home again and [1180]
> was certainly glad to see me, as he had quite a time while I
> was out there, he was sick and could hardly get around, and
> here all by himself.  He was hurt out at [[Well Shaft (Pyramid)|the well]] and it
> caused his feet and legs to swell, he would not write me
> about it as he thought that would be that much more for me
> to worry about.
> 
> We both, hope to see you all some time this summer I am glad
> to hear that the work there is going along so nicely.  [1180]
> would like very much to have a reading, before he finishes
> this well, as he does not know what is the best for him to do
> then, providing he don't get production in this well.  I hope
> it will be convenient for you to get it in soon as he feels
> that it would direct him right.
> 
> Have you started your new building yet?  Suppose you have
> the garage completed by this time.  We are having nice spring
> weather here trees are budding, lawns are green, and some of
> the Easter flowers are in bloom.  Remember me to all as not
> a day goes by but what I have all the folks there on my mind,
> and I am always anxious to hear from those having treatment
> and to know that they are improving.
> 
> With love to all, and
> Best personal wishes to you, I am
> 
> Sincerely,  [2519]
> 
> Would like to hear from you when you have time.
>
