# TEXT OF READING 2675-7 M 28

**Reading [[2675-7]]** · Series: 2001-3000 · Year: 1927 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 3/9/1927  Sex: M  Age: 28  ReadingID: 9771

This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at his office, 115 West 35th Street, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 9th day of March, 1927, in accordance with request made by self - Mr. [2675].

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Mrs. Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading 
10:30 A. M. Eastern Standard Time.       [[Alabama|Birmingham]], [[Alabama|Alabama]].

1. GC:  You will give the physical condition of this body at the present time, giving advice concerning operation for appendicitis.

2. EC:  Yes, we have the body here.  This we have had before.

3. Now, we find conditions are somewhat exaggerated from those as we have had here before, and that the inflammation and uneasy condition as has been experienced by the body is sufficient to warrant the action as may be taken through the performing of operation to relieve condition existent in the region of appendix.  While this might be overcome, yet were the operation performed in the manner that will hinder any adverse condition arising from the inflammation, or from adhesions (and these would form unless special care in drainage is taken - then this would not be well), with the care properly taken, this we see would prove beneficial to the body, as will the rest and the diet that will follow such.  Well, however, that there is due preparation of the body, in cleansing same from all stimuli, and that the circulation is well in hand and the eliminations good, before such is performed.  When this would come about - in three to four days under special care - then it might be well.  We would do that then - have the operation to relieve conditions existent there.

4. (Q)  Could this condition be relieved without the operation?
(A)  Be relieved, yet the condition would re-occur unless different care was taken of the body from that as has been seen, see?

5. We are through with this reading.

EC's wire:  "Reading advises operation when proper preparation is made.  See complete information before acting."

> I received the Reading a few days ago and your letter

> been exceptionally well until this appendicitis complication
> came back.
> 
> During the Summer of 1917 I had my first serious illness.
> The leading doctors here in [[Alabama|Birmingham]] said that I had
> "chronic appendicitis" and urged an immediate operation.
> Soon after this I began to improve, and a few months later
> you gave me the first of these Readings.  Nothing was said in
> it about that trouble.  I followed the suggestions in it as
> closely as I could, and felt as well for a year or more as
> one could want to feel.  It is the same story with the ones
> since given, as you know.  These suggestions have been so
> successful in getting me by that I had dismissed all thought
> of appendicitis; indeed, I have doubted several times since
> that I ever had appendicitis.  I do not doubt it now however;
> and as soon as my strength will permit I am going to have
> that removed for good.  It is my belief that this
> complication has kept the relief resulting from the other
> applications from being as permanent as it would otherwise
> have been.
> 
> I am glad that I received the Reading when I did, and glad
> that I deferred the operation instead of acting then as I was
> urged to do, because developments of the few following days
> proved that I was in no shape for it at that time.  If this
> operation is not a complete success, or if I do not feel well
> afterwards, it might be well to have another Reading later to
> supplement this.  There are some terms hinted at there which
> are not quiet clear and about which I would like to ask some
> questions.  In case developments indicate this necessary I
> will let you know in the meantime.
> 
> I want to thank you again for your patient personal
> attention.  I hope you are feeling well yourself.  With best
> wishes, I am
> 
> Your friend,  [2675]

> R3. 5/11/27 [2675]'s letter to EC:
> 
> 
> Birmingham, Alabama.
> 
> Mr. Edgar Cayce,
> 115 W. 35th St.,
> [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va.
> 
> Dear Cayce:
> 
> I am just back from St. Vincent hospital where I have been
> laid up for three weeks following the operation which I had
> performed during the latter part of last month.  I want to
> take this, my first opportunity to give you a report on what
> was found because you will be anxious to know how I am
> getting along and you will also want to make some comparison
> of these findings with the reading which was given.
> 
> Dr. Cunningham Wilson who did my work thought that whereas I
> had been having so much intestinal trouble with symptoms so
> complex and confusing that it would be better to make a
> general survey of conditions there by way of a large incision
> instead of performing an operation for appendix only.  This
> decision proved fortunate.  Contrary to my expection the
> appendix was not seriously affected, though of course he
> removed it as a precaution against future trouble.  The major
> cause was higher up, at the right curve of the colon, where
> ascending joins the transverse.  At this point the colon was
> dislocated, having draped and doubled back on itself forming
> several adhesions which made almost a total obstruction.
> 
> What could cause such a condition as this and how long it
> has existed no one can say.  I am told of at least one
> similar case for which there apparently is no explanation,
> the condition evidently having existed from birth.  Mine may
> be such a case.  If this theory is not correct, it is thought
> that the malformation may possibly have started with a case
> of "colitis" (I don't think that is spelled correctly, but
> I haven't a dictionary at hand) from which I almost died
> when a child only a year old.  I have always had serious
> trouble there, so it is probable that the condition had
> always existed - not in such an aggravated form perhaps as
> recently shown, but there, nevertheless.  But of course this
> is all more or less guess work about a question the answer
> to which is not important except insofar as it would help to
> avoid any future trouble.  What IS important is whether the
> condition has been relieved.  I think that it has.  A sudden
> and complete relief is felt from the mental depression from
> which I have suffered at times for a good many years and from
> which I was suffering constantly at the time the operation
> was performed.  Most of the physical discomfort is gone.
> Appetite, digestion and assimilation have greatly improved,
> and strength is being gained as rapidly as could be expected.
> I think I will be ready for work in a few more days.
> 
> The reading proved correct in at least one thing, the
> necessity for the operation.  It is clear enough that I
> could never have had much physical health working against
> such an impediment.  It is equally clear that there is no
> remedy for condition of this kind except an operation -
> whatever temporary relief and very great benefits one may
> receive from this treatment or that.  And, in this
> connection, whether this condition primarily caused or only
> aggravated the physical shortcomings repeatedly outlined in
> the readings - troubles which no doubt existed, else complete
> relief would not have been experienced when the various
> remedies suggested where applied - still, it is easy to see
> why these could not be made permanent.
> 
> There are many angles to our experience with this which I
> should like very much to talk over with you.  I am still
> entertaining hope that I may have a chance to visit you
> sometime in the near future.  If you ever have occasion to
> come to Birmingham, make sure to look me up.  I would enjoy
> a visit from you very much.  If the correction which has been
> made remains permanent with reasonable care I ought to have
> no future trouble.  I hope that it does, and I am told that
> it usually does.  In the meantime I will let you know how
> things are coming on, and I will write to you anyway because
> I want to keep in touch with you and the work you are doing.
> With best personal regards, I am
> 
> Sincerely yours,
> [2675]
