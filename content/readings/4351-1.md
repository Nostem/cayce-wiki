# TEXT OF READING 4351-1 F ADULT (?)

**Reading [[4351-1]]** · Series: 3001-5747 · Year: 1924 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 3/6/1924  Sex: F  ReadingID: 12178

This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at [[Phillips Hotel (Dayton)|Phillips Hotel]], Room 115, [[Dayton Ohio|Dayton]], [[Ohio|Ohio]], this 6th day of March, 1924, in accordance with request made by self - [4351], through Mrs. [4880].

[Edgar Cayce; Linden Shroyer, Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading                       William Penn Hotel, 
3:00 P. M.                            Altoona, Pennsylvania.

1. EC:  Now, we find this body very good throughout.  Above the normal in many ways, yet we find the abnormal also and the effect as is produced in the system by this condition.  This we find is of the structural condition, and produces a specific effect in the body.  The condition causing or producing this comes from times back, even when the body was very small in stature.

2. Now, these are conditions as we find in this body.

3. IN THE BLOOD SUPPLY, we find this very good.  Well balanced in its elementary forces.  Also in the division in the constituents necessary for rebuilding in the system.

4. IN THE NERVE SYSTEM, we find those centers that produce the condition in circulation and in the effect as is brought to the pulmonaries and the inhalation and exhalation in the body.  This center then that has the condition or pressure is that at the sixth and seventh dorsal, affecting the secondary cardiac plexus.  Hence, with this [[Census of Quirinius|taxing]] the strain in system in breath or shortness of breath and the pressure as is produced in lung and diaphragm proper, more of pressure felt than of other action in the system.  In the secondary cardiac plexus center the perineurium itself shows the inflammation and a lesion of the floating type.  Hence we have this depression only spasmodically, and when the nervous system becomes overtaxed, so the condition may arise from the excitement to mental forces from cold or from overeating, or any that causes taxation to any nerve centers, either of the hypogastric, pneumogastric, cerebrospinal or sympathetic, will affect at times this center (not present you see at this time).  In the sympathetic system this gives reflections and brings the tired, depressed feeling over the whole system.

5. IN THE FUNCTIONING OF ORGANS THEMSELVES, we find as this:

6. The brain forces very good.

7. Organs of sensory system function normally.

8. Throat, bronchials, thyroids, very good.

9. The lower portion of the lung shows how the nerve reaction through the aorta artery, when the depression comes to the plexus in secondary region, heart forces and lungs become affected, though not organic trouble, either in heart or lung.

10. Digestion in the cardiac end of stomach, the esophagus end, shows how that overeating, especially of sweets or sugars, affects the nerve system.  Well to keep away from too much of these (except honey in the honey comb).

11. In the organs of digestion very good.  All function in normal condition save when taxed through nerve condition.

12. In the organs of the trunk portion of body, and pelvis, very good.

13. Then, to relieve this condition in the dorsal would be to remove by deep manipulation, osteopathically taken.

14. When the depression is to the lungs and nerves would be well for the spasmodic conditions that arise to inhale through the mouth the fumes or the smoke from Jimson-Weed leaves, dried. Mix with third quantity of the seed from this pod of the Jimson.  This will not be necessary to be taken often, provided adjustments in dorsal regions are made.

15. Do that.

> Virginian-Pilot

> JIMSON WEED?  NO, THANKS
> 
> The Jimson weed is a black sheep of a large family of plants,
> the night-shades, which counts such honorable members as the
> potato, the eggplant, the tomato, the garden pepper, and the
> petunia.
> 
> Datura stramonium, or thornapple or jimson weed or Apple of
> of [[South America|Peru]], is poisonous from its root through its broad leaves
> to its trumpetlike flowers to the tip of its six-foot stalk.
> Alkaloids derived from the plant - atropine, scopolamine -
> can be medically useful in trained hands.  The thorny, hard
> pods yield brown seeds which may tempt the unwise.
> 
> The unwary who gulp the toxic seeds in hope of a
> hallucinating "high" court certain sickness and possible
> death.  The last previous outbreak of jimson weed poisoning
> occurred four years ago when a dozen youths in [[Norfolk Virginia|Norfolk]] and
> [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]] were hospitalized.  Investigators said drug
> pushers had been palming off the seeds as peyote, a true -
> and illegal - hallucinogen.  This week [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]] police
> reported 10 cases of jimson weed poisoning and [[Norfolk Virginia|Norfolk]] one.
> 
> History was repeating itself.  Some of the first English
> settlers at Jamestown (whence, by corruption, the present
> name) took an early bad trip on jimson weed.  Whether they
> did so for kicks or out of hunger is unclear, but they
> suffered for their folly.  The lesson is constant even after
> 3 1/2 centuries:  Those who trip out may trip up.
> 
> R2. 12/3/74 (Tuesday) Article:
> 
> 
> Ledgar-Star
> 
> JIMSONWEED
> 
> To the Editor:
> 
> Recently, in the Ledgar-Star, I read that some children in
> the [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]] area had been made ill by the seeds of the
> Jimsonweed plant.  Containing as it does the very poisonous
> alkaloid atropine, they are lucky to be alive.
> 
> Jimsonweed (Datura stramonium) is one of more than 2,000
> species in the Solanaceae, the nightshade family.  Many
> members of this family are poisonous, a few being
> Jimsonweed, Belladonna and Solanum.
> 
> Oddly enough, some members of this family produce important
> foods, such as red pepper, Irish potato, tomato and
> eggplant.
> 
> Atropine, the alkaloid present in Jimsonweed, is a white,
> crystalline powder.  It is very poisonous, but is very useful
> in the treatment of certain ailments when prescribed by a
> physician.
> 
> A number of well-known, common plants are poisonous.  The
> bark of the Wisteria contains a poisonous substance called
> Wisteria.  The leaves of the mountain laurel (kalmia
> latifolia) are quite poisonous, particularly to live-stock.
> A virulent poison is in the beautiful lily-of-the-valley,
> while the common buttercup can cause abdominal cramps when
> ingested.  The leaves of the lovely oleander (nerium
> oleander) are very poisonous.  The leaves and bark of the
> common wild-cherry contain a glucocide, "amygdalin," which
> under certain conditions can hydrolize into the deadly
> hydrocyanic acid.
> 
> The list is extensive.  Who would think that the fragrant
> "sweet shrub" (calycanthus sp.) is quite poisonous and that
> chewing the leaves of the common yew tree can cause death?
> 
> WILLIAM F. POND
> 
> Portsmouth
>
