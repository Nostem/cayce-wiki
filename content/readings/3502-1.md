> R1. 10/11/11 Dr. Wesley H. [[Wesley Ketchum|Ketchum]]'s lecture read in Boston,
> Mass., before the American Association for Clinical
> Research re Edgar Cayce:
> 
> When I was asked by Dr. Krauss while out in [[California|California]], to
> write a paper on "My Unusual Case" little did I think that
> I would come to the other extremity of the continent to
> describe in my feeble way, the results of my experimentation.
> Psychic Phenomena has always been of great interest to me
> for all, there is perhaps no field which affords a better
> opportunity for fakes and mysticism.  Now, in submitting to
> you a report of my investigations, as per request of this
> Society, I beg your indulgence and cooperation, provided it
> meets your approval, if it does not, I ask that you withhold
> your harshest criticism until we are able to make further
> research and investigation.  As no conviction is so strong
> as that preceded by honest doubt, I have great respect for
> an honest doubter.
> 
> Our present status of medicine is the outcome of
> experimentation, research and doubts.  I believe the psychic
> should come in for its share of investigation by honest,
> doubting, medical men, and confine it to the medical
> profession.  As fixed laws seem to govern all phenomena in
> nature, it is but natural to infer that some is true in the
> workings of the psychic.  In the course of my investigations
> I have only discovered one thing that seems to be absolute,
> - that is, - THAT THE PATIENT SHOULD IN SOME WAY SOLICIT
> help in his, or her particular case, otherwise, results are
> meager.  I say this because in several instances where he
> has been tried with patients who had no previous knowledge
> of such being done for them he only gave a rambling talk,
> hitting the subject only in high places, so to speak, or
> missing it entirely in one case.
> 
> I am here to offer no explanation as to how he works or the
> source of his knowledge, because I do not know; I am governed
> only by results.  In all the cases I have selected for
> experiment he had no knowledge, directly or indirectly, until
> after he was asleep, - after he is awakened and is shown
> a copy of what he has said he claims to have no knowledge
> of it in any way, or what the expressions mean.  His early
> education ended in the sixth grade, when he stopped school
> and went to work in a photographer's gallery, his education
> naturally limited.  After experimenting with my subject for
> several years, I can compare psychology only with aviation,
> which is just now commanding the attention of the mechanical
> experts of the world, and there can only be one outcome,
> success eventually.  The same is true in the psychic.
> Conditions in nature have always been as now for aviation,
> but men said "It is impossible" and, of course, nothing
> happened.
> 
> Heretofore the medical profession has assumed the same
> attitude, they could scarcely reconcile themselves to the
> teachings of those who belonged to different schools of
> medicine, and it naturally follows since they did not believe
> in each other that they surely would place little confidence
> in anything of a psychic nature.  Since my early tutoring
> and association was along this line I was inclined to accept
> nothing of a psychic nature until I was brought face to
> face with facts, vouched for by a man in whom I had implicit
> confidence along other lines and why should I not take at
> least one peep into its mystic origin?  My friend being
> a highly scientific man, a graduate of the [[Ohio|Ohio]] State
> University, was likewise impressed only when a demonstration
> was made upon his little daughter after she was given up as
> hopeless, a description of which I will present you under
> separate cover.  If you will pardon a person experience.  I
> will recite, in as few words as possible, how I was impressed
> and why I continued to investigate.
> 
> One day in the fall of 1906 [see 4135-1] I was attacked
> by a severe pain and accompanying symptoms which I thought
> was appendicitis; I immediately counseled two of my medical
> friends who assured me that I was unduly alarmed and
> suggested treatment, which I followed, with some relief, but
> only for a short time, when I again became alarmed, - this
> time I went to a near by city where I counseled two prominent
> men with about the same result as my first attempt.  The
> condition would get better for a time, but only for a little
> while and would return again.  Finally, after eighteen months
> had passed with no permanent relief, I sought aid in another
> medical center.  This time three men passed on my case and
> prescribed.  They told me I had a Nervous Appendix.  At this
> time I would have taken but little suggesting until I would
> have undergone an operation, which was not necessary, but
> when we are suffering, physician and layman alike want
> relief.  On March 29th, 1909, I was asked by a friend to
> try "The Psychic Freak" and see what he said about my case;
> previous to this I had not seen him for a long time as he
> was now living in [[Alabama|Alabama]], but he was visiting at his home.
> On the afternoon of above date he came to my office at the
> suggestion of my friend, who came with him.  He laid down
> and went into one of his usual naps.  Now he had been told
> nothing pertaining to whose case he was to tell about, and as
> I had tried everything suggested by seven men and no relief,
> of course I had little faith in anything.  But I was willing
> and anxious to have him try.  My friend said "Go over this
> man carefully, (giving my name) he is here in this room.
> Tell us what you find."  "Yes, we have him here.  A little
> over twenty-eight moons ago this man wrenched his spine, as a
> result of which we find an impinging on the vertebrael end of
> the nerves at the last dorsal and numbers one and two lumbar
> vertebrae.  We now find pain and irritation at the opposite
> pole, or in the inguinal region, worse on the right side,
> through the sympathetics.  We also find some irritation of
> the bladder.  As this was caused mechanically it will require
> mechanical treatment or manipulation to relieve it."  My
> wife, upon being told, immediately recalled the time when
> I wrenched my spine while feeding my horse.  It had only
> troubled me a few days and had completely passed from memory
> until it was recalled.  My faith in his statements was
> strengthened when I found this had occurred only two or
> three weeks before my first trouble in the Autumn of 1906.
> Treatment along the new idea followed, but not until I had
> consulted my Gray's Anatomy and found the land marks as
> he stated.  After three or four mechanical treatments my
> pseudo-appendicitis cleared up entirely and all soreness
> localized at the original seat in my spine.  After taking
> a total of nine treatments, or from March 29th until April
> 21st, all my trouble ceased and I had not been troubled
> since.  Then the thought came to me - how does he do it?
> Where is the source of his knowledge?  Was it just shrewd
> guessing?  In the cases and testimonials that follow I
> shall call on you to decide.

> Cayce in a long, long time.  He had some on tap that I'd
> never heard of.  The description of how he made contact and
> talked with Professor Dietrich regarding his little girl, Amy
> Dietrich, is just remarkable.  His story, too, of his
> readings that he got for his patients - even the man,
> apparently, who handled the [3502] case.  I have heard about
> this all my life and now I know from [[Wesley Ketchum|Dr. Ketchum]] why it has
> never been reported in public.  This was mentioned frequently
> but no one seemed to have kept any records about it that I
> know of, and certainly no details of it.  Dr. Ketchum's story
> on it was a honey.  Apparently, the [3502] family was very
> well-to-do in ... and the [3502] boy was sent home from
> college, practically a raving maniac.  He was very violent,
> talked violently and had to be restrained at times.  The
> family sent him everywhere, ending up at Mayos, with a
> diagnosis of dementia praecox and a complete statement from
> doctors that he would never recover.  Apparently, they went
> to Dr. Ketchum.  According to his story, he then went to
> Bowling Green with Dr. House and had a reading from Edgar
> Cayce which described the [3502] boy then in ...  The
> description of the condition and the outline of the treatment
> was something that was then fairly well known but apparently
> the condition was so covered up that it hadn't been diagnosed
> properly.  It was inherited syphilis, as Edgar Cayce
> described it, according to Dr. Ketchum.  The boy's brain was
> on fire and he outlined a medicine which Dr. Ketchum then
> began to give him and the boy recovered normalcy.  This one,
> I would certainly like to check a bit but I've heard Dad
> mention the [3502] case and I think we may have some records
> of it.  Of course this is not the kind of thing we can
> publicize or talk about, even now, because the family is
> still there.  The reading, according to Dr. Ketchum,
> indicated that it went way, way back as an inherited physical
> condition which would prove obviously embarrassing to
> everyone concerned.
> 
> Dr. Ketchum went on to talk about his early cases.  He would
> wander off every once in a while to describe his own
> experiences in various directions.  He talked his
> relationship with Mr. Noe and his difficulties with L. B.
> Cayce, but constantly spoke highly of Edgar Cayce and flatly
> stated that, in his opinion, he never saw a diagnosis which
> was wrong and that if the people followed the suggestions,
> they got well.
> 
> Now, this man is a practicing M.D. and has been in Palo Alto

> [[Thomas Sugrue|THERE IS A RIVER]] (we didn't get into that discussion) but I
> wrote to him at the time and tried to get him to correspond
> with us about it.  He didn't answer letters.  He was most
> friendly this time.  He is as chipper as he can be - filled
> with stories, and he went on for 3 1/2 hours that afternoon.

> tape.  He is very reluctant to publish any of it.  He still

> book or radio or T.V. series and his interest is not so much
