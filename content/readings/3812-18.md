# TEXT OF READING 3812-18

**Reading [[3812-18]]** · Series: 3001-5747 · Year: 1931 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 7/10/1931  Sex: F  ReadingID: 11408

This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at his office, 105th Street & Ocean, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 10th day of July, 1931, in accordance with request made by Mr. A. C. Preston.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno. L. B. and [[Hugh Lynn Cayce|Hugh Lynn Cayce]], and Mrs. Carrie S. House.]

Time of Reading 
8:00 A. M. Eastern Standard Time.

1. GC:  You will have before you the treasure buried near Kelly's Ford, Culpepper Co., Va., and the work now being done to recover it.  You will tell us if the operations in the last few days have been in accord with information given from these sources, and if not what should be done to correct these operations; giving specific directions for recovering this treasure, using such markers as are now visible as points from which to work, and you will answer the questions I will ask.

2. EC:  Yes, we have the operations here, as have been made in attempting to recover the treasure here, near Kelly's Ford, Culpepper County, Virginia.

3. UNDER existing physical conditions in the present, little may be accomplished very soon.

4. In the operations, as has been given, there should be made the check with the instrument and with information given. When these do not check, then cease operations until there is that understanding in the mind of each that are seeking same as to what, how, what for, and the conditions that have been set by each that have been seeking; for that as has been promised must be fulfilled, every whit.  This is not just a material circumstance with which these operating are dealing, for they have promised much to self as well as to the Forces, or Sources, from which information has been or may come.  Then, when there is cross feelings, or cross connections, there may be expected those things that will bring about those destructive forces; for, as we find, the treasure is there, and they are very near to same - it being closer, or toward the abutment.  We do not find that they would locate, until they the have made themselves right, that each may obtain or know within themselves that, that being sought is in accord with that which has been promised, as well as that they do.  Ready for questions.

5. (Q)  How can these individuals further prepare themselves?
(A)  Be consistent.

6. (Q)  Have the operations in the last few days been in accord with the information given?
(A)  Hasn't it just been given here that with the manner, and UNDER the physical circumstances and conditions, little may be accomplished in the present!  though seemingly in that direction in the present.

7. (Q)  Is the instrument being used working correctly?
(A)  Hasn't been used, or hasn't worked correctly, as yet!

8. (Q)  Any further advice and guidance to these seeking this treasure?
(A)  Consistency!

9. That's all the questions.

10. We are through for the present.

Copy to Edgar Cayce

> REPORT ON 2ND DOWSING FIELD TRIP (Sept. 9, 1973, Kelly's
> Ford, Virginia)
> 
> On a sunny Sunday morning, over 40 dowsers ranging in age
> from Wilburn Smith's 12-year-old daughter to senior citizen
> William Moore, attorney and counsel to the Society for the
> Application of Free Energy, met at the former Civil War
> battlefield site on the Rappahannock River in Culpeper
> county, Virginia.  There was much enthusiasm in evidence
> since the group has never come back "empty-handed" from prior
> trips, and has always found at least one large metallic
> object dating back to the Civil War period.  We had high
> hopes that we could continue this record and at least come
> back with some portion of the treasure that we have long
> sought.
> 
> At the site, group leader Carl Schleicher covered the
> history, background and plans for the day's search to recover
> the Civil War payroll treasure.  Each attendee was given a
> two- page write-up and commentary on the Edgar Cayce readings
> which were extracted from the Cayce Foundation at Virginia
> Beach describing the treasure and how it was buried by Union
> troops on 24 September 1863 when overrun by the Confederate
> army during the skirmish at Kelly's Ford.  They hastily
> buried the total Federal Army payroll, which consisted of
> numerous gold and silver coins, to avoid letting it fall into
> enemy hands.  This has been an elusive treasure through the
> years, and one reason may be that previous searchers failed
> to carry out to the letter Edgar Cayce's explicit
> instructions.  The group brought a "silver dowsing rod" as
> recommended by Cayce and several silver and gold witness
> coins to assist in the search.  Mr. Schleicher emphasized
> that one of the keys to success in dowsing is to adequately
> prepare oneself by programming the mind and mental thought so
> that the dowsing rod, which serves as an amplifier of the
> dowser's response to external stimuli, will only respond when
> the items for which he is programmed are in his immediate
> vicinity.  He noted that this is the biocybernetic basis for
> dowsing.
> 
> After a warm-up session at a site where the group
> had previously found several cannon balls and other
> silverware paraphernalia, the search Commenced.  Having
> hopped over the barbed wire fence, while keeping a wary eye
> on the many bulls on farmer Luke Poland's property, they
> continued the dowsing search where it had left off on the
> last trip in May 1973.  It will be recalled that in this
> area the general group had found a most attractive large
> carpenter's plane which [[Resurrection Appearances|appeared to]] be about a hundred years
> old.  Several of our dowsers, including Hannah Kelly and
> Phyllis Roos, had strong feelings at that time that the
> treasure would be found approximately 6 feet from a large
> tree adjacent to the river and the barbed wire fence.  This
> tree had been previously marked and designated by them, and
> Mr. Schleicher led a dowsing search in an area encompassing
> an arc of ten feet from this tree.
> 
> Several metal detectors, that is non-dowser type devices,
> were also used, and it was noted with amusement that all they
> were detecting were bottle caps, pieces of tin foil and other
> modern-day junk items.
> 
> The first hole was dug to a depth of 3 feet where several
> dowsers had rather modest reactions; however, a check at the
> 3-foot level revealed no Civil War objects in the hole.
> After a close scrutiny of why the reactions occurred, it
> appeared that some iron-ore bearing rock may have been the
> reason, since this would probably fit the mental programming
> of "metallic objects from as far back as the Civil War
> period".  Metal detector devices confirmed the iron ore
> content in these rocks.
> 
> The second hole was dug where there were very strong dowsing
> reactions, particularly by the daughter of Wilburn Smith, who
> incidentally had found one of our items of interest from the
> Civil War period on the last trip.  This reaction was
> confirmed by other dowsers including Carl Schleicher, and a
> prediction of 3 feet was made for the object sought.  Jess
> Bunch then dug a 4 foot diameter hole in soft clay soil.  At
> a depth of approximately 3 feet his shovel struck a very
> large, beautiful flat iron that [[Resurrection Appearances|appeared to]] be at least 100
> years old.  The iron had the number "7" and a "keystone"
> symbol on it.  The number "7" has appeared on previous
> cooking utensils found in this area and possibly indicates
> that one of the military units engaged here may have had this
> number in its organizational title.  Finding this handsome
> antique iron was a mind-expanding experience and certainly
> proved to those who witnessed this amazing "find" what
> dowsing can accomplish.  A search was also made on the north
> bank of the Rappahannock River at a place which Edgar Cayce
> followers from the A.R.E. thought might contain the treasure.
> This region, it was noted, had been thoroughly searched in
> previous years by the Edgar Cayce Foundation (A.R.E.).  Large
> pock-mark holes were noted similar to small-size moon
> craters.  It was believed that these were left by the Cayce
> group when dug there some years ago.  Members of the Cayce
> group indicated this area might still be haunted by spirits
> of the soldiers who engaged in mortal combat here; however,
> no one in the SAFE group confirmed this.  No strong dowsing
> reactions were noted in this area, so the main part of the
> group then proceeded to the island in the center of the
> river.
> 
> Led by Dave and Loretta Rhodes, these dowsers dug two very
> large holes on this island where strong dowsing reactions
> were indicated.  These were confirmed by other dowsers and a
> huge 8 foot hole was dug, but all that has been found so far
> on the island area is a buried 2 year old flash light and a
> golf ball.  The island area does appear to be a logical
> burial spot for the treasure, but unfortunately, time to
> fully excavate the holes where strong dowser reactions had
> been noted finally ran out.  On our next trip we hope to
> explore this island, which incidentally is within the old
> Kelly's Ford bridge area and could therefore be a logical
> hasty burial spot for a defending army undergoing heavy
> attack.  The main problem at this spot is that the river has
> many times overflowed and deposited much Sandy silt on the
> island.  Thus, any treasure buried during the 1860's would
> probably be at least 8 feet deep.  This will not daunt our
> dowsers, who hope to return to this area soon.  At least we
> have kept our record of never coming back empty handed, and
> did indeed find at least one object for which we were
> programmed - a credit to all who were there on this eventful
> day.
