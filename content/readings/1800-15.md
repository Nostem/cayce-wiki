> EDITORIAL
> 
> "There's a divinity that shapes our ends, rough-hew them
> how we will."
> 
> On Sunday afternoon, March 31, 1899, in the living room of a
> little house in [[Hopkinsville|Hopkinsville]], [[Kentucky|Kentucky]], three people watched
> a man who could not speak talk in his sleep.  The watchers
> were Mr. and Mrs. L. B. Cayce and a layman, the sleeper was
> Edgar Cayce.  The words spoken by the latter as he slept
> comprise what is known as the first reading ever given by
> Edgar Cayce.  Its results were immediate.  When he went to
> sleep Mr. Cayce could not speak, his vocal chords were
> paralyzed.  When he awoke they had returned to normal
> functioning, his voice was natural and clear.
> 
> It is a far cry from that little house on the bleak March
> afternoon of a dying century to [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]] of National
> Investigators, Incorporated, and the [[Cayce Hospital|Cayce Hospital]], at
> [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va.  It is a far cry indeed, and the vagaries
> and whims of human frailty carried the cry farther still, ere
> the inexorableness of Time and the certainty of Fate brought
> Truth to the harbor of Faith and Love.  Yet nothing
> worthwhile is won without struggle, and the pain and
> disillusion of Experience bring a greatness to [[Soul|the soul]], a
> sweetness to the heart, and a clearness to [[Ideal|the ideal]] that
> set us firmly on the King's Highway and direct our footstep
> toward the Kingdom of God.
> 
> It was a long, hard road for Edgar Cayce, from the first
> fairy voice of childhood to the first ground broken for the
> [[Cayce Hospital|Cayce Hospital]].  Man through his opaque mist of materialism
> sees little of the world beyond his length, breadth, and
> thickness, and because of his own conceit the attempts of
> truth boomerang from a solid wall of self.  How difficult it
> is to make people understand something they cannot contact
> with the senses!  How discouraging it is to see truth
> misinterpreted and misapplied, used as the individual sees
> fit, to suit best the desires of his own nature!  How long
> to the goal it seems when the road is so winding and rough,
> so studded with stubble, so littered with deadwood, so
> cluttered with debris!
> 
> To conquer self was the first task, and perhaps the hardest,
> for as Polonius said:
> 
> "This above all:  to thine own self be true,
> And it must follow, as the night the day,
> Thou canst not then be false to any man."
> 
> To convince himself that he was not a freak of nature, not
> an unconscious fraud, not a spiritual charlatan, was first
> of all the work of Edgar Cayce.  For years he lived in
> self-conscious embarrassment because of his strange
> peculiarity, for years he feared to give the aid requested
> of him because he did not know nor understand the thing he
> did.  For years his life was a mental torture, a seething
> indecision, a labyrinth of ideas, [[Ideal|ideals]], misbeliefs and
> disillusions.
> 
> From the left came a cry of service, from the right a whisper
> of gain.  Which way Parnassus?  No answer came.  Time passed,
> and the mind that could not know of itself, learned from a
> world that clutched at stars and dragged its [[Soul|soul]] in mud.
> Reality stumbling skyward in the illusion of smut taught a
> lesson etched in pain and failure.  The answer became
> obvious.
> 
> "Though I speak with the tongues of men and of [[Angels|angels]],
> and have not love, I am become as sounding brass, or a
> tinkling cymbal.  And though I have the gift of [[Prophecy|prophecy]],
> and understand all [[Initiation|mysteries]] and all knowledge, and
> though I have all faith, so that I could remove
> mountains, and have not love, I am nothing."
> 
> To Edgar Cayce had come [[Ideal|an ideal]], to him was shown the truth.
> And so he lived, and so he lives, and so he will live
> forever.  He survived the cries that branded him charlatan,
> quack, spiritualist, [[Clairvoyance|clairvoyant]], hypnotist, fake and fraud.
> He survived the misunderstanding, pain, disillusionment, loss
> of friends and attacks of enemies.  He survived the purgatory
> of ignorance of conquer himself and others, finding peace and
> happiness in the knowledge of truth and the possession of an
> ideal.
> 
> It was at the September meeting of the American Association
> of Clinical Research in 1911 that recognition and hope first
> was given for the work.  This meeting was held in the rooms
> of the Society of Natural History in Boston, and a paper
> describing the phenomena, written by Dr. W. H. [[Wesley Ketchum|Ketchum]], of
> [[Hopkinsville|Hopkinsville]], [[Kentucky|Kentucky]], was read.  The opinion of the
> Association was voiced in the sentiment that the work
> deserved further study and was deserving of commendation and
> support.
> 
> From this first bit of official recognition, this first gleam
> of hope that he was something more than a freak of Natural
> law, Edgar Cayce formed the [[Purpose|purpose]] and ideal of his life,
> a [[Purpose|purpose]] and ideal which is now that of the Association of
> National Investigators, and which is expressed in a single
> word - Service.
> 
> It was natural that Mr. Cayce should conceive of a hospital
> or sanitarium, where the people he diagnosed could be treated
> as he prescribed, under physicians in accord with the work;
> and it was natural that he should go to his own peculiar
> source of information for advice concerning it.  The first
> reading taken on the establishing of a hospital approved of
> the idea and outlined the method of going about such a
> venture.  On February 11, 1911, it advised [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]] as
> a site for this hospital.  On June 19, 1928, the first ground
> was broken for the [[Cayce Hospital|Cayce Hospital]].  What happened during the
> seventeen years elapsing between the creation of the idea and
> its material culmination?
> 
> It is a long time between the idea and the creation, the
> [[Dreams|dream]] and the reality.  In 1911 Edgar Cayce began working
> toward the establishment of a hospital.  On February 11,
> 1929, the first patient was treated in the Cayce Hospital
> at Virginia Beach.
> 
> There is not much use in reciting the hardships of those
> seventeen years.  Let us pass over them lightly, as we
> passed over those years before the faith and ideal became
> visualized.  From [[Hopkinsville|Hopkinsville]], [[Kentucky|Kentucky]], to [[Selma Alabama|Selma]], [[Alabama|Alabama]];
> from Memphis, Tennessee, to [[Alabama|Birmingham]], [[Alabama|Alabama]], and westward
> to the oil fields of [[Texas|Texas]], the trail of [[Dreams|dreams]] led this man
> and the loved ones who followed him.  It was a hard road, and
> a long one, strewn with bitterness and broken hopes.  Yet
> always the faith remained, the ideal was firm, and the
> readings repeated the admonition, go to Virginia Beach.
> 
> Why was such a wonderful work so difficult to begin?  Why was
> such an ideal so hard to attain?  Nothing is worthwhile until
> we fight for it, what comes easily is seldom appreciated.
> Though the faith and the love were firm, the mind and spirit
> and body had not yet been sufficiently tried and attuned.
> Those associated did not understand, there was not the
> harmony of an organization, in short, the time was not ripe.
> Many people and groups of people took up the work and planned
> to establish a hospital.  Each was unsuited to the task it
> seems, and eventually they dropped it.  The time was not
> ripe, the [[Vibration|vibrations]] and conditions of the Universal forces
> and the earthly conditions were not properly adjusted for the
> happening of such an event.  Time passed.  It seemed to Edgar
> Cayce that things were going round in circles.  The thing he
> saw so clearly, the thing he desired so earnestly, was but a
> misty haze in the minds of others.  Patiently he waited.
> 
> At [[Dayton Ohio|Dayton]], [[Ohio|Ohio]], in January, 1925, the first germ of an idea
> was born for The Association of National Investigators.  From
> that time on, like a babe in the mother's womb, it grew
> slowly toward an inevitable culmination.  Conditions had
> become right, people had come in accord, and Time had slipped
> into place.  The rest was merely adjustment and readjustment,
> smoothing out details and making arrangements.  The result
> was a certainty, the [[Dreams|dream]] a reality, the desire a
> gratification.
> 
> In September of 1925 Edgar Cayce and his family moved to
> Virginia Beach, establishing a residence at 115 West 35th
> St., an address grown familiar to many since then as the
> office of the Association and the place where all readings
> are given.  Here the work was continued in the old way,
> obviously.  But all the time the Association was planning
> the culmination of one dream and the beginning of another.
> 
> On May 6, 1927, The Association of National Investigators
> was incorporated.
> On June 19, 1928, the first ground was broken for the
> Cayce Hospital.
> On November 11, 1928, the Cayce Hospital was dedicated.
> On February 11, 1929, the first patient was treated in the
> Cayce Hospital.
> 
> The dream is a reality, the ideal is achieved, the WORK
> has BEGUN.
> 
> The world goes on unheeding.  The waves of the ocean have
> not ceased their beating, the season its changing, the sun
> its rising, the world its evolution.  Only something has
> happened.  Something definite has begun, something tangible
> has taken form, and a small group of people look toward a
> new horizon.  The world whirls on, unknowing.  Meanwhile we
> work.  Someday the world will stop its mad racing to listen,
> and heed.  Time only knows when it will be.  For the present
> let us make sure we will be prepared.  Let us take up our
> happy burdens and start forward, trusting the others to
> follow where we show the way.

> The time has come for beginning what this publication
> [[Purpose|purposes]] to do.  We have formed the nucleus, laid the

> to record in systematic fashion the advances and achievements
> of this association.  Journalism is the world's contemporary
> historian, and as a specific journalist it is the purpose
> of this magazine to reflect and record the work of our
> organization.  Though primarily for our members, it is
> inevitable that it will fall into the hands of those outside,
> who will wonder and question its meaning and contents.  To
> those people we extend an invitation to inquire and study
> our work, to visit our offices at Virginia Beach, and to join
> with us in a brotherhood of searcher for truth, for
> ourselves, for the world, and for God.
> 
> To the members of the Association who will receive this first
> issue we ask the tolerance of innovation and the assistance
> of co-workers.  It is your magazine, open to your views and
> suggestions, and we will gladly welcome and appreciate your
> criticism, contributions, and general support.

> WHAT IS TRUTH?
> 
> BY EDGAR CAYCE
> 
> [The following is one of the lectures delivered by Mr. Cayce
> at the Cayce Hospital, Virginia Beach, Va.]
> 
> We have spoken on various subjects from Sunday to Sunday.

> life and its projection into the physical plane.  From time

> As I have said, I am going to try to talk to you this
> afternoon about Truth - or "What Is Truth?"
> 
> I realize that I am assuming a big [[Book of Job|job]].  I've read but very
> little of the philosophers of the ages, but we know during
> all the years there has been the continual cry of, "What Is
> Truth?"
> 
> We often hear it said that Truth is evasive, Truth is naked,
> Truth will not be downed - but what is Truth?
> 
> We remember, if we turn to the books of the [[New Covenant|New Testament]],
> that possibly the greatest moment in history was when [[Trial of Jesus|Pilate]]
> asked of [[Jesus Christ|the Master]], "What is Truth?"  Possibly that was the
> ONLY time it could have been answered in one or two words
> that would have at least satisfied the greater portion of the
> thinking world today - but he didn't wait for the answer.
> Yet we know that [[Jesus Christ|the Master]] said that He CAME that He might
> show the way unto Truth.  All right.  Then we know Truth is
> something that we may be shown.
> 
> When we speak of A truth, or THE truth, or TRUTH, we possibly
> mean different things.  You will remember, those of you who
> have read the little story - and I'm sure most of you have -
> there were three men of Hindustan who went to see the
> elephant, but all of them were blind.  Now that's US!  We are
> all blind, but we are seeking the truth; seeking the truth
> because we are very much in the same position as the three
> men who went to see the elephant.  One of these stumbled
> against the side of the elephant, and he said, "I perceive,
> without a doubt, the elephant is very like a wall."  Was
> that the truth to him?  Another one, as he stumbled about the
> animal, found his trunk, and said, "I perceive with a
> certainty, the elephant is very like a tree."  Was he correct
> in his supposition?  The other man, as he stumbled about, got
> hold of the tail, and said, "I perceive the elephant is very
> much like a rope."  Now were they in error?  Did they have
> the truth?  Or did they have only a portion of the truth?  Or
> did they have any of the truth?
> 
> We often say that any movement of any character succeeds in
> so far as it has a portion of the truth.  We may be sure
> THAT'S truth; that any movement succeeds in so far as that
> movement has the truth.  Then don't worry because you
> disagree with any individual as to what your conception of
> any movement may be - don't think that they are going to hell
> because they don't think as you do!  Remember the three men
> that went to see the elephant - just remember that!  Keep
> that in your mind.  KNOW that they are right, but they are
> wrong as YOU would see it, with your eyes wide open.
> 
> As Mr. Blumenthal has been telling us, life in its projection
> into this material plane has been a constant growth.  Then,
> if we are to believe the things that have been presented to
> us, and AS they are presented to us, we know that even Truth
> ITSELF may be a GROWTH INTO that understanding in which we
> will be able to apply it in our everyday life.
> 
> I am sure that we believe God is Love.  The love for an
> individual, then, is an expression of the force, or
> manifested force, that we define as Love.  There was this
> experience that came to a man.  He was very much in love
> with his wife.  He thought more of her than anything in the
> world, but God saw fit to take her home, and when he erected
> a monument in the cemetery he had inscribed on it:  "The
> light of my life has gone out."  He couldn't find anything
> that could satisfy him with this separation, but after a
> while he met a young lady whom he fell very much in love
> with, and he was altogether mistaken about his first love
> being able to satisfy every thing in his life - there was
> something else that had been added, he had gained the
> knowledge of someone else being able to fill up a portion of
> his life.  The young lady, knowing about this inscription,
> said to him, "I might consent to be your wife, but I don't
> think I could ever do that as long as the inscription remains
> as it is."  So he went about to find a man who could correct
> this for him, and as the man who had charge of such things
> told him he saw how he could rectify this, he went ahead with
> the wedding.  When they came back from their honeymoon they
> went to see how it read, and this is what they found:  "The
> light of my life has gone out - but I've struck another
> match."
> 
> So, you see, those are individual experiences.  When we
> read, or get the idea of some particular thought, or some
> particular rule, gradually we build into OUR own selves the
> idea that we have GOTTEN the whole thing.  Now IS Truth such
> a thing that those who have been followers of Mohammed have
> ALL of the truth?  Have they who have been the followers of
> [[Moses|Moses]] [[Law of Moses|the law]]-giver, all the truth?  Or has it rather been a
> growth in our individual lives, and what may be truth to one
> individual possibly may not, in the experience of the other
> individual, answer at all?  Does that make the other any less
> true to the other individual?  Did this man love the second
> woman any less from his experience of being in love before?
> 
> Then, is it possible if this be true, that this is a
> changeable thing, is a growth?  Will it be possible for us to
> find something of which we can say, "THIS is truth," and KNOW
> that it will answer in everything or every way that life may
> present itself to us?  I believe that we can.  You may differ
> with me.  I don't think, however, that you will be able to
> REFUTE what will be my definition, or what I will be able to
> say IS Truth.  But many of you will say, "Well, have you been
> given some peculiar power, that you have knowledge of what
> Truth is, and can answer the question that has been sought
> throughout all the ages, so that we may know what TRUTH is?"
> Let's see if this will not answer the whole question in our
> lives.  First we must know that, if we are to accept any
> word, or any follower as a truth, or THE truth, or Truth,
> we must be sure of the AUTHORITY that we quote.  We must be
> very sure of our foundation, or platform, for what we have
> assumed.  Where would we begin, as we would say, to assume
> something as being true, or a place to begin?  Or how far
> could we go with this formula?  Or where would we begin with
> this formula, so that we might know, when we hear a thing,
> that to US it is Truth?  We must recognize these facts in our
> lives:  there is a [[Physical Body|physical body]], there is a [[Spiritual Body|spiritual body]].
> We know the [[Physical Body|physical body]] is dependent upon its physical
> attributes for its development.  It is also [[Temple of Solomon|the temple]], or
> the dwelling place of the [[Spiritual Body|spiritual body]].  The spiritual
> body, we know, is of the Creator - whatever we may call the
> Creator.  It comes from the same thing - even if we go back
> to the scientific reasoning and say it begins from the lowest
> form of life.  WHEREVER we begin, we have to say there is
> something beyond that where it has developed, whether you
> believe in evolution, creative evolution, or what not, it all
> has to come back to the very same thing, AND if I can answer
> this I don't believe I'm wrong in saying that you can take it
> from any standpoint you want to take it, but this IS Truth:
> 
> THAT WHICH KEPT BEFORE YOUR MENTAL MIND, YOUR SPIRITUAL MIND,
> WILL CONTINUE TO DEVELOP YOU UPWARD!  That which, held before
> you, will continue to develop you upward.
> 
> Now, let's see how this fits in with what Mr. Blumenthal
> says of evolution.  Let's see if this applies to any of the
> phases that may present themselves, or to any the questions
> you would want to ask:  THAT WHICH, HELD BEFORE YOUR MENTAL
> [[Visions|VISION]], WILL CONTINUE TO DEVELOP YOU UPWARD.  Not that which
> satisfies selfish purpose at all, no!  Not that which tells
> you whether it is wrong to go fishing on Sunday or not, or to
> play baseball on Sunday.  Not that which answers you in that.
> It answers you, yes - but that which will continue to develop
> YOU upward?
> 
> All right!  What do we mean by developing upward?  That which
> will enable you to hold the [[Visions|vision]] of that which YOU worship
> as YOUR God.  Every man, every individual, every OBJECT, has
> its conception of its superior position.  We would say,
> "Well, would it answer the Indian who is looking for his
> Happy Hunting Ground?  Why would it not answer?  That which
> will enable him to hold before him that which HE worships as
> HIS God is Truth to him.
> 
> Now, what have we taken for granted?  We have taken for
> granted that man has a mind, that man has a body - a physical
> body, a [[Spiritual Body|spiritual body]].  He has a SOUL, if you choose!  We
> have taken for granted that his [[Mental Body|mental body]] is controlled
> by his mental being.  His soul, or his spiritual body is
> controlled by his [[Subconscious Mind|subconscious]], or his spiritual mind.  That
> which will continue to hold before the individual that which
> he worships as his God will continue, then, to develop the
> individual towards that which he worships.  Now, does that
> answer your question?  Then you will say, "What would have
> been [[Jesus Christ|the Master]]'s answer to [[Trial of Jesus|Pilate]]'s question?"  "I came into
> the world to do no other than conduct you into that which is
> Truth."
> 
> Truth is not a thing, then, that we can see, that your eyes
> or body, or ears, or things of that kind - but it is the
> essence with which an individual builds faith, hope, and
> trust.  That is Truth!  That essence that we are enabled to
> hold before our mental [[Visions|vision]], is truth.  Will it build your
> body?  It will!  Will it heal the sick?  It will!
> 
> A few days ago I was talking to some people - they were
> telling me about a book that had been written by some of
> the masters from the far East.  I had never seen the book
> before, but when I opened it to read it I knew what was in it
> before I read it.  I don't know how, nor why, but I knew the
> experiences that I was going to encounter.  I don't know why
> except THIS:  I have been continuing to think of how I might
> answer this question.  With the first four or five pages I
> found that in this book, this ONE thing was continually held
> before the individual; that which you hold before yourself,
> to create that image you worship, that will develop you
> always upward, and will continue to enable you to know truth!
> 
> Truth, then, being a growing thing, truth being a thing that
> will develop you, is a something that is ENTIRELY IN ACTION!
> Not inactive, but IN ACTION!  That's what God is!  For in
> every MOVEMENT that has ever been, it has been a continual
> upward development, or upward toward that which IS Truth.
> 
> If you hold malice, you can become one of the meanest
> persons in the world.  You know that if you continue to
> send out thought (that may become a [[Miracles of Jesus|miracle]] or a crime),
> you create just those very same cross-currents in your mind.
> What is [[Prayer|prayer]] but simply attuning yourself to that which you
> are seeking assistance through?  That's all [[Prayer|prayer]] is -
> attuning yourself to that very same thing.  That becomes
> Truth when it becomes in action.  When it goes into action,
> to YOU it becomes Truth.  It's your own conception of what
> your God is.  If it makes you better to the very thing you
> worship, or more in accord with the thing you worship, THAT
> you become, whether it's downward or upward, you go whichever
> way your standard is set.
> 
> Q. Yet if we are going backward, how can we know?
> A. You can't make it anything else, because to you it's
> truth, because that's what you are following.  That becomes
> truth to you.  That which you continue to follow becomes
> truth whether it's negative or positive.  As we learn it from
> that viewpoint, it becomes true; that is, whatever we hold
> that continues to build upward is Truth.  Now, as to who is
> to say whether you are building up, or as to how near it
> brings you to that thing you worship AS your God, because God
> IS Truth, if you continue to hold that, you may develop to
> that, but to someone else whose God might be something else,
> that wouldn't be Truth.
> 
> Q. That applies right here in your hospital, etc.
> A. If it answers one, it answers all.  That's what it
> stands for; that which may develop in the individual that
> understanding of its relation to its Maker and to its fellow
> man.  Or, as we come back to the very same thing I said in
> Sunday School this morning, I've been studying a long while,
> trying to understand what is meant by the second commandment,
> and I never did know what it meant - that is, satisfactorily
> to my own mind - until the other night.  The first
> commandment, as we know, is, "Thou shall love the Lord thy
> God with all thy heart and thy mind, etc."  The second is,
> "Thou shall not make unto thee any graven image."  Why?
> Because if you make an image it becomes your God.  But if you
> have for your God that which is within your own individual
> self, you yourself being a portion of the Creator, you will
> continue to build upward, to it!

> MY CONCEPT OF GOD
> 
> BY MORTON H. BLUMENTHAL
> 
> Revised from a lecture delivered at the Cayce Hospital,
> Virginia Beach, Va., Sunday, September 22nd, 1929.
> 
> Mind of the Creator as Distinguished from the Mind of the
> Created.
> 
> My lecture today is entitled "My Concept of God."  I will
> lecture on the same subject two weeks from today.
> Thereafter, or four weeks from today, my lecture will be
> "Heaven and Hell."  We will discuss "Heaven and Hell" in the
> light of the conception we have formed of evolution.  Some
> of the questions that I will endeavor, in my own humble way,
> to take up, will be "What is Heaven and Hell?  Is
> de-evolution down to a Hell, possible as a condition of
> consciousness as well as evolution to that condition of
> consciousness call Heaven?  How do we attain through a
> process of de-evolution the condition called Hell, and by
> contrast how do we attain by a process of evolution the
> condition called Heaven?"  After this lecture I will
> conclude the season with a series of talks on [[Reincarnation|reincarnation]].
> 
> During the past summer months I have endeavored, in my own
> way, to present my concept of evolution and my experiences
> upon which that concept is founded.  Today, I will try to
> [[Initiation|initiate]] a series of two lectures that in reality will do
> nothing more than sum up all our past discussions regarding
> the subject of evolution.  I would not dare be so bold or
> officious as to insist upon the acceptance of my concept of
> God by the member of this intelligent audience.  My concept
> of God is the result of my own personal experience, just as
> is the case with every individual, and, (as James points out
> in his "Varieties of Religious Experiences"), as one's
> experience grows, the scope of consciousness becomes broader,
> and as a result one's concepts change accordingly.
> 
> Truth enters our consciousness through the route of
> experience.  Experience itself is a process of mind, physical
> or otherwise, that awakens in each of us an ever broadening
> realization of the truth.  Whether your own individual
> experiences have led you to the conclusion that mine have, is
> a matter only you yourself may determine.  My true aim in
> delivering these lectures is to help each individual, or each
> individual mind, or better, each individuality of mind, to
> become so broadened in experience as to be able to reach and
> enjoy a higher state of consciousness, and thus achieve a
> deeper and more useful conception of truth, or God.  As you
> hear me speak my heart, then, I am sure your own good sense
> will inform you of the fact that I am not seeking to
> establish or propound any new religion, or detract from,
> or add to, any existing order of religion.
> 
> Mr. Cayce's lecture last week on "What is Truth" dovetails
> beautifully into the pragmatic ideas of truth that I have
> just spoken of.  In considering then, the result of my humble
> efforts today, to give "My Concept of God," I would
> appreciate your considering in collaboration therewith,
> Mr. Cayce's lecture on "Truth."  The lecture was, and is,
> probably the best dissertation on the subject of pragmatic
> truth, as propounded by a former psychologist of Harvard
> University, namely, William James, that the layman has had
> the opportunity to hear since Mr. James wrote his work on
> Pragmatism.  Now, I know Mr. Cayce has never read Pragmatism,
> yet thinking his subject out in relationship to truth, he
> stood before you, and in very simple and understandable terms
> delivered a most enlightening lecture on the deep subject of
> Truth from a pragmatic viewpoint.  You, who heard this very
> excellent lecture last Sunday, will easily understand what I
> mean when I say I am giving a concept of truth or God, as it
> exists from my personal viewpoint.
> 
> Now, how do we know anything in this world?  Our Saturday
> afternoon class, studying Ouspensky's "Tertium Organum," is
> learning that the outside world and life is what our own
> inner sensations, as well as ideas founded upon sensation,
> tell us it is.  In other words, it is the operation of the
> mind that enables us to know anything, whether that knowledge
> is in terms of abstract realities such as good, evil, God,
> truth, values, etc., or whether it is physical realities in
> the form of houses, trees, sidewalks, oceans, lakes,
> mountains, fields, tables, chairs, etc.  The animal kind of
> operation of mind does not enable him to form concepts, and
> therefore, he cannot know of such realities as Good, Evil,
> Value, Truth, God, etc.  The operation of that mind enables
> the animal to know about the world in two dimensions only -
> i.e., the world outside of himself would consist of surface
> realities, each separated from the other.  The sides of what
> to us is a three dimensional house, would to the dog, for
> example, separate two dimensional realities - i.e., each side
> would be a separate thing existing in space by itself and
> would have no connection with any other wall or surface, just
> as two houses are two separate individual things existing in
> space, apart from each other to us.  On the other hand, our
> kind of operation of mind enables us to form not two
> dimensional perceptions (animal kind of mind), but also
> three dimensional conceptions or ideas, and thus obtain
> knowledge of such abstract realities as Good, Evil, etc.  It
> so operates as to make the outside world, to us, different
> three dimensional realities - i.e., houses, trees, oceans,
> sidewalks, chairs, tables, space, etc.
> 
> Science, however, has reduced these things that occupy space
> to a [[Oneness|oneness]], (in cause), with space.  Whatever space is in
> substance, that also our world and everything in our world
> and any other world as in substance, says Dr. Whitney.  It
> must be evident, then, that mind, being part of the animal
> and ourselves, must likewise be in substance this same one
> element, yet that part of the element that enables the latter
> to be conscious or capable of experience of some kind.
> 
> In our world it would be a consciousness or experience of the
> sensuous kind, made manifest in animal and man.  That this
> part of the original element, (call it life, creative energy,
> ELAN VITAL, Spirit, God, or what you will), called
> Consciousness is capable of different degrees of development
> is made manifest in the low form of consciousness exhibited
> in the vegetable and mineral kingdom, (flowers, plants,
> trees, etc.), and again in the low form demonstrated in the
> lower animal kingdom, and higher form made evident in the
> higher animal kingdom and still higher developed kind
> manifested in consciousness - not only of man.  In the latter
> form there is shown the ability of self consciousness, but
> the ability of the original element, creative energy, life,
> (or whatever name we may choose to give it), to know itself
> to be that which it is in the physical form called man, and
> therefore in any other than physical form, or in its
> elemental state.
> 
> As shown by science, this world, including ourselves, is
> but a manifested FORM of the original elemental substance.
> Therefore, that part of this elemental life essence
> (unknowable to our kind of mind) that nevertheless
> constitutes the substance of space and all that is in space,
> that part of this elemental essence then, called mind or
> consciousness, would manifest the kind of consciousness that
> corresponds to the kind of form the life element takes or
> becomes in any field of operation.  In our field of operation
> or in this section it operates to become a physical FORM
> both as regards that part of it that becomes the form of
> matter and that part of it that becomes physical
> consciousness, and this whether the latter is in the lower
> developed state of the mineral, vegetable and lower animal
> kingdom, or in higher developed state of mind in the higher
> animal kingdom, or still higher evolved state in man.  It
> demonstrates its material kind of consciousness to be that
> called physical or sensuous - the kind, for example,
> possessed by animal and man.
> 
> The substance of life, (of matter, mind and motion) with the
> portion called consciousness, does evolve to a point, that,
> changing into physical form, becomes intellectual man, and
> from this changed physical viewpoint, (a change called
> birth), would know itself to be that which it becomes in
> manifested materialized FORM - i.e., our own selves, or that
> which we mentally and physically know ourselves to be.
> 
> It is from this physical viewpoint that we know about life,
> whether it is about realities imagined such as ideas of good,
> evil, God, etc., or about lower evolved physical forms such
> as trees, flowers, houses, chairs, etc.  The latter kind of
> realities are formations of this life essence of a physical
> kind, whereas abstract realities such as good, evil, value,
> etc., are a higher evolved formation of life in physical form
> - i.e., a formation of life in its manifested physical form
> of man's mind.  Our mind represents life in physical form,
> and so does the world outside of ourselves represent a lower,
> and in the case of vegetable and mineral, a still lower
> developed physical formation of this one life essence the
> substance (whatever it may be), that manifests in
> materialized form the dual conditions of form plus
> consciousness.  In the form of rocks, trees, oceans, lakes,
> etc., it includes so low a degree of consciousness as to not
> be apparent to our observation.  In the form of the animal
> it includes a higher developed degree of consciousness.  In
> the man form it includes a still higher developed degree of
> consciousness - even self consciousness.  [[Thought Forms|Thought forms]] such
> as Good, Evil, God, Virtue, Bad, etc., do not possess the
> physical limitations of life in lower materialized from
> [from?] - i.e. are not measurable either in terms of one
> dimension of length, or two in terms of length and height,
> or in three dimension of length, or two in terms of length
> and height, or in three dimensions in terms of length,
> height and breadth.
> 
> Man in mind must not only be self conscious of being that
> which life is in physical form, but being in potentiality
> that which life is in its elemental essence, man must be
> potentially self conscious of being that which be - life is
> in internal substance, as well as externally self conscious
> of being that which be - life is in physical form.
> Intellectually he knows himself to be the latter;
> intuitively, subconsciously he knows himself to be the
> latter.  With his whole mind he imagines outside three
> dimensional formations such as forms of tables, chairs,
> houses, etc., or represents in his imagination internal 4th
> dimensional formations, such as ideas of good, evil, etc.
> Likewise through the use of the imagination in externalized
> physical form he self-consciously makes externalized physical
> contracts and relationships with other men and the outside
> world.  With this same imagination he, from within his own
> mind, self, being, makes internal elemental contracts and
> relationships with higher states of mind than the physical
> form state of other men, i.e., with 4th and higher
> dimensional beings or individualities that are life in its
> elemental state rather than in its physical form, and who
> imagine themselves to be this.
> 
> But what must this substance of space and of things of
> consciousness be to another kind of operation of mind - i.e.,
> what does mind not changed into a physical form (prior to
> birth or after death) becomes aware, for self, of this life
> essence being?  It is logical that if our sensuous mind tells
> us it is a physical kind of three dimensional space, and that
> the world and we ourselves are three dimensional things in
> space, then another kind of operation of mind other than the
> physical kind would enable the observer to know about the
> one substance of space, of the essence of things in space,
> of that creative energy that is doing the manifesting to be
> changed into the atom and of the same spirit, or to us
> unknowable quality, that in other fields of operation changes
> into other forms, possessing other MINDS of consciousness
> than the sensuous kind.  Such a being would cognize his own
> self and the outside world as being this one element, (i.e.,
> as being the ONE, his fourth dimensional viewpoint tells him
> life is), rather than being different three dimensional
> things, (including ourselves), occupying a three dimensional
> space, which is what the three dimensional viewpoint of our
> physical mind tells us life in physical form is.  The latter
> viewpoint would then represent that of life in physical form,
> i.e., of our own sensuous or physical mind.  The former
> fourth dimensional viewpoint would not be limited to finite
> observation, as already shown, (and in spite of our finite
> minds, we admit life or the universe to be infinite), but
> would be infinite or of a higher dimensional character, and
> represent the kind of viewpoint possessed by this one element
> or essence of life, or God, when not in physical form.  It
> would represent the mind that governs the life element that
> operates on it to create or change it into the different
> universal forms, one of which is the physical of this world.
> It would represent in man the potential or [[Subconscious Mind|subconscious]]
> ability to be conscious and self conscious of life in its
> elemental rather than physical form and state, or of man's
> potential, and submerged higher kind of operation of mind,
> and therefore innate, if submerged, higher dimensional
> viewpoint.  Not changed into the physical form of man,
> (prior to birth and after death) it would represent this
> higher kind of operation of mind itself - this higher
> dimensional viewpoint not limited to any three dimensional
> terms, and therefore, would be the creative mind, or that
> of the Creator, with all the greater powers and enlarged
> fields of application that must accompany such a higher
> dimensional viewpoint.  It would be the mind of the Creator,
> and would be self-conscious of being life in elemental
> essence, and according to the point evolved, would be
> self-aware of being the quality that is in many split
> operations, to make manifest in many states of consciousness
> and many different formations, only one of which is the
> physical and sensuous state life achieves in form and
> consciousness in this material world.  We are that split
> off-spring of life that this higher kind of mind has formed -
> created, and are that in substance which this higher kind of
> being is in substance, but we are now this in a manifested
> physical form.  From our physically formed three dimensional
> viewpoint we know ourselves to be that - i.e., that which
> this elemental essence operates to become after changing into
> physical form.  I am that, but being that in its physically
> formed state, know myself to be a physically formed
> manifestation of that.  We then are the created, and know
> God from the viewpoint of our limited three dimensional
> physical kind of mind.  But to know what God really is, and
> that we split into different created formations, even into
> different equal parts (we each of us), to know this we must
> reach into our own consciousness to obtain knowledge that
> only the higher dimensional viewpoint of our inner soul or
> subjective being may bring into our imagination or
> consciousness.  We must, in spite of our three dimensional,
> finite, physical viewpoint, understand the inner infinite,
> higher dimensional experiences that the inner [[Subconscious Mind|subconscious]]
> mind may be induced to bring through imagination into our
> consciousness, but which visioning, [[Intuition|hunches]], premonitive or
> [[Intuition|intuitive]] thoughts, experiences, or spontaneous ideas we will
> fail to understand, unless we know something of the logic, of
> the higher dimensional viewpoint.
> 
> Such experiences would be as illogical to our three
> dimensional viewpoint and three dimensional logic, as a dream
> of a three dimensional house would be to the two dimensional
> dimensional mind and two dimensional logic of the animal.
> The animal.  The latter could understand his dream if he
> could reason that four independent two dimensional surface
> realities could be so connected by angles as to form one
> whole, connected three dimensional reality.  Likewise we MAY
> learn that our subconscious or introspective experiences
> demonstrate the existence of the fact that what to us are
> spacially separated three dimensional realities, are really
> connected together, (the space and things that occupy space),
> to the fourth dimensional viewpoint, to form one-fourth
> dimensional whole or reality.  We may reason what the higher
> dimensional viewpoint must be, (see Ouspensky's "Tertium
> Organum"), and then experience it to be that, by heeding,
> recording and studying our introspective experiences, in the
> light of what we reason the higher than three dimensional

> EDUCATION
> 
> BY FRANKLIN BRADLEY
> 
> A. What is consists of (herewith).
> B. How to obtain it (later).
> 
> Theodore [[Franklin Roosevelt|Roosevelt]] said that "the only thing out of which we
> can make a man - is a boy."  It is your task and mine to see
> that our boys, if possible, become educated men, and our
> girls educated women.  This we will do both because of our
> interest in them, and because the future integrity of our
> nation requires it.
> 
> Education is a method of living - strengthened by experience
> - broadened by learning - governed by independent and
> judicial thinking.
> 
> An educated man has learned what to prefer, because at least
> mentally he has lived in the presence of preferable things
> and has an intense appreciation for them.
> 
> Educated men and women have always quietly smiled in the
> face of bigotry and superstition.  They have done more for
> freedom and truth than armies of crusaders.
> 
> Erasmus was a nearly perfect example of the educated man,
> and good examples are unfortunately rare.
> 
> There is no such thing as mass conscience, judgement,
> thinking, or education - it is all individual.  And only
> you as an individual can educate yourself.  That is why
> most schools have much teaching, some learning, and little
> education; because they deal in mass production only.
> 
> Students are of three kinds:
> 
> (1) Those in whom has been established a love for real
> education.
> 
> (2) Those who want specific knowledge to help them in a
> future vocation.
> 
> (3) Those who want to go through school.
> 
> We must not run our three classes of students all through
> in the same grist.
> 
> If it is worthwhile just to "go through school" - and in
> many cases I think it is - let us have schools that permit
> that - with just as much of better things thrown in as
> possible.
> 
> If it is worthwhile - and I think it is - just to give
> training to certain pupils in specialized lines, to help
> them through definite knowledge to a more efficient place
> as specialists, let us give them that and add all we can
> of work that will make them more truly educated.
> 
> But I make an especial plea for fostering schools that will
> permit genuine and deep-seated education - that method of
> living that is governed by independent and judicial thinking;
> a knowledge of the worth while and an ability to secure it;
> with a poise that comes only from self-mastery.
> 
> Some people are quite well educated who have never had much
> schooling.  Some people with many degrees from institutions
> of learning are poorly educated.
> 
> If all the world understood what it means to be educated, and
> how impossible is real progress without education; if every
> school understood it so that its pupils could be assisted to
> the full in acquiring what they need and are capable of
> assimilating, most of our problems would vanish.
> 
> I could have been educated but for the teachers I have had.
> In spite of that I hope and expect to become educated if I
> live long enough, as I fully believe that one who loves
> education cannot be kept from it.
> 
> Unfortunately most of the important subjects, as well as the
> best methods of teaching them or bringing them home, are not
> found as frequently as we desire; although this world does
> not hold real teachers and schools and curricula.
> 
> Possibly outside of the study of history many of the
> following subjects are as valuable as or more valuable than,
> most of the subjects taught as such:
> 
> 1. How to live in our bodies.
> We can never have but one body.  We must understand its
> functioning and how to make it a useful habitation for our
> minds and spirits.  Very few people coordinate their bodies
> with their minds and spirits, and as a result lose most of
> the joy and success possible.
> 
> This is not alone a study in physiology, although that has a
> bearing.  It is a study in doing those things, forming those
> physical habits that enable us to absorb helpful vibratory
> energy whether it be from fresh air, food, drink, or from
> visible or invisible radiations from the host of sources that
> fill our universe.  It is the arrival at a state of physical
> strength and elasticity always under control and resulting in
> ability to receive or give.  Together with a wholesome
> trained brain and spirit it completes the [[Trinity|trinity]] essential
> to an educated individual.
> 
> 2. Human relations.
> As my friend Dr. Hurt says, "When there are two of us talking
> together there are really six people present - you as you
> are, you as you think you are, you as I think you are; myself
> as I am, as you think I am, and as I think I am."  The
> confusion, the misunderstandings that can arise from this
> mixture of six personalities, is at the basis of most
> imperfect human relationships.  The study that goes to the
> heart of things in our relations with each other is that
> study that will give us happiness in our association with our
> fellowmen.  It will make them helpful to us and us to them.
> 
> 3. Imaginative faculty based on laws of cause and effect.
> The thing which causes us to rise through successive stages
> from one plane of life to another is the ability to look
> ahead, see the possibilities, and make them become actual
> facts.  It is the development of the imagination, not day
> dreaming, but a practical vision developed from within and
> contracting the things that are without.  It is the essence
> of growth.  The man who has it rides in the swiftest
> airplane.  The man who has none of it is chained to a stake.
> 
> 4. Logical thought and action.
> It is a rare and noteworthy individual that is capable of
> discussing at length any subject and sticking to the main
> issue throughout his discussion, beginning at the foundation,
> carrying out the argument with only illustrative side-lights,
> and pushing clear through to the final conclusion.  When we
> listen to such a one we are profoundly impressed, first with
> the efficiency of the discourse, second with the feeling that
> if this hewing to a line could be put into our daily lives
> they would become vastly more successful.  Most thought and
> performance are to a degree illogical, waste of time and
> constant error resulting.  Logical thought and action are
> fundamental in an educated individual.
> 
> 5. Play.
> Unfortunately when the word play is mentioned it is usually
> accompanied by the thought of children and their activities.
> 
> It is every bit as important that the spirit of play function
> through the entire life of any individual.  Play is primarily
> a relaxation.  It is a rest that comes from a change in type
> of activity.  More than that it is a removal from the serious
> side of everyday activity.  Its results are spontaneity;
> physical, spiritual and mental refreshment; longer life; a
> common cheerful ground on which we can approach our
> fellowmen; and a storehouse of energy to carry back to our
> work again.  It is rest in motion.
> 
> 6. Love of beauty and the spiritual in life.
> Love of beauty does not alone mean a liking for a beautiful
> face, a graceful tree, or flowers, or skies, or birds.  It
> means primarily an appreciation of the richness of all that
> the Creator has given us in our physical surroundings and
> secondly, in the things of our heart realms and of the
> spiritual world.
> 
> The attributes of veneration and respect and love are a
> result of appreciating beauties that may not be visible on
> the surface.  The student of beauty finds beauty wherever he
> goes.  The one who does not seek for beauty finds sordidness.
> A training in this field causes one to look for the finer
> things and enables him to find them, and the Creator himself
> is brought near and made very personal.
> 
> 7. Sincerity.
> Our standing in the world depends upon how our neighbors look
> at us, which is a result of how we look at ourselves.  We are
> the only ones who know whether we are always honest and
> honorable.  If, knowing ourselves, we can gaze out of eyes
> squarely at every other man, knowing that we are doing what
> we can in the right direction, the thing our fellowmen will
> see will be absolute sincerity, and we will learn that
> through it is produced the confidence, the esteem, and the
> honor of our fellowmen.
> 
> 8. Self-reliance.
> If most of the people who complained of being downtrodden and
> unfortunate in the affairs of the world would wear across
> their breasts a sign which read "I am a Leaner," they would
> stating to the world the definite reason why they have not
> made more progress.  A leaner has to be carried, a
> self-reliant man not only bears himself to great heights,
> but carries others with him.
> 
> 9. Travel.
> Travel as a study is that great subject which gives us a
> knowledge of our fellowmen and the environments which help
> make them what they are.  It is the comparative subject, and
> frequently we rise best by making comparisons.  Comparing
> homes, comparing habits and customs, comparing individual
> characteristics, comparing the effects of geographic and
> geologic conditions, gives us a [[Photography (Atlantean)|picture]] of the advantages or
> the handicaps of others, gives us an appreciation for what
> they have accomplished and the methods through which
> accomplishments have been made, and gives us ideas to use
> as stepping-stones for ourselves.
> 
> 10. Sense of humor.
> Frequently the possession or lack of sense of humor is the
> difference between a happy and an unhappy life.  It is the
> difference between a happy approach to others and an unhappy
> one.  Its possession gives us a standing as companionable
> and social human beings.  While it may be claimed that many
> of the above subjects, such as sense of humor, possession of
> imaginative faculty, etc., are rather born in a person than
> that they may learn and acquire these attributes, yet a vast
> amount of progress can be made through serious and
> comprehensive training.
> 
> 11. Values.
> The study of values will result in our leaving out the
> insignificant and the trivial, and devoting our time to the
> important things.  This study requires the analysis of every
> act of our waking existence and affects not only the things
> we think but the things we do.  As a result of the study of
> values the accomplishment in life of any given individual can
> be multiplied many times.
> 
> 12. Citizenship-service.
> No man has the right to live unto himself alone.  We have a
> duty to our fellowmen and to our country.  Only a small
> percentage of our men and women are actually good citizens,
> rendering adequate service.  There can be no greater subject
> for concentrated study than this.
> 
> 13. Diligence.
> The study of diligence may seem to be minor but it is not.
> A recent and a true statement has been made to the effect
> that given a man of good brain and diligence almost anything
> conceivable is possible in the way of accomplishment.  It is
> the "wool-gatherer" who loses out in the race of
> accomplishment.
> 
> 14. Mother Nature.
> The truly educated men of the world have without exception
> gone to Mother Nature for rest and refreshment and
> inspiration, and they have gone there because they have
> studied Mother Nature.  Every one of the five senses can be
> attuned to the vibrations there recorded during a proper
> course of instruction in this out-of-doors subject, and the
> vision we see, the symphonies we hear, the odors of the
> forest that assail our nostrils, the taste of the herbs and
> the bark and the roots and the berries, and the feel of the
> fresh air and the water and earth itself, all work together
> for a background of living that is free from artificiality,
> that is sincere, that is happy and that knows the fountain
> of eternal youth.
> 
> With the mental development arrived at through thorough
> training as indicated the individual becomes dominated by
> his appreciations.  He has become capable of differentiating
> between the helpful and harmful, between good and bad,
> between right and wrong, between worth while and not worth
> while.  His appreciations make him live a contented life,
> whether he is alone or in company with others.  He lives a
> life of completeness.
> 
> There is now established the Virginia Beach School for Boys.
> The director and the Advisory Board of that school believe
> in education as outlined above.  The three R's, etc., have
> their necessary and perfect place in the curriculum, but the
> thing sought for is a thorough grounding in appreciations - a
> complete education, that education (to make a re-statement)
> which results in a method of living governed by independent
> and judicial thinking, with knowledge of the worth while and
> ability to secure it, and a poise that comes only from
> self-mastery.
