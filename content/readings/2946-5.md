# TEXT OF READING 2946-5 F 49

**Reading [[2946-5]]** · Series: 2001-3000 · Year: 1944 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 2/15/1944  Sex: F  Age: 49  ReadingID: 10211

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at the office of [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]], Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 15th day of February, 1944, in accordance with request made by the self - [2946], Associate Member of the Ass''n for Research and Enlightenment, Inc.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno. (Notes read to and transcribed by Jeanette Fitch.)]

Time of Reading 
10:50 to 11:00 A. M. Eastern War Time.        ..., Illinois.

1. GC:  You will give the physical condition of this body at the present time with suggestions for further corrective measures, answering the questions, as I ask them:

2. EC:  Yes, we have the body here, [2946]; this we have had before.

3. As we find, conditions are not too good in the present. While there was the allaying and the disturbances in the body were eliminated, the conditions in the pelvic organs have grown to such a state that we find operative measures would be preferable to those conditions which become necessary under the present circumstances.

4. If the body had remained quiet, if the body had taken a period of six to eight weeks to rest and do the things suggested, these conditions would have been better.  Now, operate.

5. It will be necessary in the present - not that all of the organs of the body should be removed here; that is, the generative or genitive system, but don't leave it so that it will become irritating again.

6. Ready for questions.

7. (Q)  Should the left ovary be removed or a complete hysterectomy performed?
(A)  As just indicated, not the complete - but don't leave it so that there will be a general drain on the nervous system, but prepare the body.  It is well at this period, however, because of the [[Astrology|astrological]] influences, as well as physical condition.  The rest will do more good than most everything else, though.

8. (Q)  Is there any special preparation that could be suggested?
(A)  None other than seeing that there is proper coagulation in the system.

9. (Q)  Is the skipping of heartbeats a circulatory disturbance or a reflex of indigestion?
(A)  It's a reflex condition in the nervous system.  Nervous indigestion, as well as a general nervous condition.

10. (Q)  Would hormones administered by hypodermic be effective?
(A)  Be effective, but not to prevent conditions in the present.

11. (Q)  Is the body still short on female hormones?
(A)  Still short.

Do these things.  With the operation, no need to take the other things in the present.  During the rest period, have plenty of [[Beef Juice|Beef Juice]], beet juice and liver extract.  These should be parts of the recuperative diet.  Plenty of all that is body-building.

12. We are through with this reading.

Copy to Self - Air Mail - Phoned also
"    " Ass'n file

(2/15/44 GD's note:  See directions [which were enclosed] for preparing pure [[Beef Juice|Beef Juice]].)

> R1. 2/19/44 [2946]'s ltr. to GD:
> 
> ..., Illinois
> 
> Dear [[Gladys Davis|Gladys]],
> 
> Maybe I'm not grateful to you for rushing through a copy of
> the last reading.  And to Mr. Cayce, double-deluxe grateful,
> for taking the reading.  And to [[Gertrude Cayce|Mrs. Cayce]] who, I bet, asked
> that good question about special preparation.  And to [5070]
> for phoning me.  You are a WONDERFUL corp of specialists.  I
> was sorry [3188] was ill that day.
> 
> When the reading was taken I was so annoyed with this
> heart-skipping business that the idea of an operation had
> passed from my mind.  Well - "what fools we mortals be".  I
> didn't gather from last fall's reading that any particular
> period of rest was indicated.  I guess I'm the kind who has
> to be knocked down and chloroformed to get an idea in my
> head.  I did try to get more sleep and I got the Kaldak and
> have been taking that along with Vitamin A, B, D and
> what-is-there.  Also spent more time in [[Meditation|meditation]].  But I
> admit I have had a plenty heavy schedule.  Time I do all the
> things I ought to do at home and school there is no time to
> write - which I have to do.  So I do get up early and push
> kind-a steadily.  Now the pay-off.
> 
> Well, sir, I hope some of you can wade through this report on
> doctors and help me decide what next.  Last Wednesday night I
> hied me in to [[Chicago|Chicago]] to see one of these guys called a
> specialist.  First I called Dr. Ruth Taylor, whom [3188]
> knows well; she is a fine sensible doctor.  She made an
> appointment with Dr. Davis, head surgeon at Lying-In which is
> supposed to be as good a hospital as there is in the country
> for this sort of thing.  I haven't the remotest prejudice
> against Jews but this man is very Jewish and he knows
> everything.
> 
> He said he would do a curettage to find out whether there
> were any cancer in the uterus and then if there were none, he
> would either use [[Radium|radium]] to stop menstruation permanently or
> he would remove the uterus and both ovaries "as routine
> procedure".  I told him I didn't have any sign of cancer,
> that [[Woman of Revelation|the woman]] of my family menstruated until fifty-five or
> so etc. etc.  But he said heredity didn't mean a thing and he
> believed in taking everything.  Well, I didn't like the bird
> - as you may have gathered.  He's too brash.  So -
> 
> I talked with Dr. Taylor.  She, too, doesn't believe in
> wholesale excavation.  In fact she thought that prolonged
> rest might take care of the trouble - providing I'd always go
> to bed through a menstrual period hereafter.  I could tell
> her about the reading toward which she is somewhat
> open-minded.  But she said no one could operate while my
> blood pressure was 80.  She felt that two weeks preliminary
> rest was absolutely necessary; anyway ten days.  She thought
> I would sleep most of that time and I think so myself.
> 
> Item 3 - is anybody reading this far or am I addressing the
> empty world?  Friday morning I went to see Dr. Musselman who
> is the Best [[Osteopathy|Osteopath]] I ever met.  He wants very much to come
> to the Congress.  He is simple and un-proud, doesn't claim to
> know everything and has the utmost faith in the readings.
> Has given [[Thomas Sugrue|THERE IS A RIVER]] to several of his patients and a
> couple doctor friends.
> 
> He made the usual examination and decided the left ovary was
> enlarged and should come out but saw no reason for removing
> anything else.  He agreed with Dr. Taylor that the heart
> disarrangement and nervous indigestion were largely the
> result of deep fatigue; also agreed that no surgery should be
> undertaken until my blood pressure picked up and recommended
> complete rest for a week or ten days.  He wanted me to see a
> Dr. J. H. Bloomfield who operates at Passvant and who on the
> staff of Lying-in for 15 years.  Blood pressure 80.
> 
> So I saw Dr. Bloomfield.  He is a big friendly person who
> seemed to realize that patients were human.  I felt
> confidence in him and was ready to offer him my liver and
> pancreas along with a collection of ovaries.  But after his
> examination he said all the others were mistaken and the
> trouble was varicose veins in the left broad ligament.  He
> said the veins could be dissected out but he felt the uterus
> and left ovary should be removed in order to remove the
> pressure.  He said there was some slight repair work to be
> done also.  He thought none of the trouble would shorten my
> life but that I would have increasing pain and nervous
> symptoms unless the surgery were performed.  He also thought
> I ought to have some genuine rest before the system took on
> the added strain of surgery.
> 
> So here we are:  one good surgeon says veins and the other
> says 'innards'.  Only Mr. Cayce's magic eye knows which is
> right.  As far as I'm concerned I could dispense with
> everything between the knees and neck.  All I need is a head
> to think with and a couple of feet to walk around on.  But I
> have my heart and [[Free Will|will power]] set on being a thoroughly useful
> - and maybe even wise - child of God for the next fifty
> years.  I want to do the right thing and then I'm going to
> live circumspectly.
> 
> On Monday - February 21" - I'm going back in to Mrs. [3006]'s
> and have me a week's rest flat in bed.  Isn't that wonderful?
> No writing, no nothin'.  Bet I will sleep 20 hours out of
> every 24.  Of course I have a big speech scheduled for Day of
> [[Prayer|Prayer]], which is February 25", but I am trying to get a
> substitute.  Then I will plan to have the operation about
> March 3".
> 
> Now what I want to know from you-all is this:  do you think
> we could possibly make another short check-up on Tuesday,
> February 29"?  I will have had a week's rest by that time.
> I intend to spend a lot of that week in cleansing my [[Soul|soul]].
> Something tells me that Dr. Bloomfield would be open to
> direction from the readings.  It would be a wonderful thing
> if he were.  I don't mean merely wonderful for me but for
> many others who may have operations.  He is a distinguished
> surgeon and I'm sure the Lord could use him.  I'm going to
> pray for him as a person and you, [5070] & [3188], might do
> the same.  I hesitate to ask people to add responsibilities
> to their intercessory [[Prayer|prayers]] except as interest dictates.
> 
> I still believe that illness is sin and that direct
> contacting of Power is possible.  But there is probably too
> much scar tissue on my [[Soul|soul]] for me to do a good [[Book of Job|job]].  That's
> a silly thing to say, though.  Sometimes I think the Lord
> loves saving big sinners more than little sinners...
> 
> Thanks for the [[Beef Juice|beef juice]] note, [[Gladys Davis|Gladys]].  I never knew one was
> supposed to take only a spoonful or so at a time and I've
> sometimes made a cup of it and drank the whole works at once.
> Bet I could live on it.
> 
> Hate to write a letter so full of personal pronouns.  It even
> bores me.
> 
> A great deal of love is herewith enclosed.
> 
> [Questions at end of letter]
> 
> 1. Could a month's bed rest with proper diet, followed by
> regulated life in the country, correct the present trouble
> without surgery?
> 
> 2. The surgeon - Dr. J. H. Bloomfield - diagnoses the
> trouble as varicose veins in the left broad ligament.  Is his
> diagnosis correct?
> 
> 3. He plans to remove the uterus and the left ovary.  Is this
> the best procedure?
> 
> 4. Because a small amount of repair work is necessary, he
> suggests the possibility of operating through the vagina.  Is
> this the best procedure?
> 
> 5. Would Passavant Memorial hospital, [[Chicago|Chicago]], be a good
> place for the operation?
> 
> 6. Is any special pre-operative care indicated?
>

> 
> Dear Mr. Cayce,

> plain.  That ought to do a lot for [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]] all down

> 
> You've no idea how grateful I am for the reading.  Last night

> 
> One big thing you did for me was get me some sleep.  With the
> doctors' edict in my hand saying I had to get blood pressure
> above 80", I just stayed in bed yesterday and slept SIXTEEN
> HOURS.  My heart hasn't skipped a beat since.  Maybe the big
> lesson I have to learn is to stop running the world for God -
> who may have plans of his own.
> 
> Am going in to Mrs. [3006]'s tomorrow and sleep for a week.
> In my waking moments I'll be thinking of you all.  Will miss

> remember what you look like!
>
