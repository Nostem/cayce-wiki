> AMERICAN ENTERPRISE
> 
> MR. PRESIDENT, gentleman of the convention, and, I must say,
> brothers:  My message to you today concerns the preservation
> of the vital principle of free American enterprise.  This
> principle, at first thought, may appear to you vague and
> intangible.  Let us give pause and examine it together.
> Like all great principles, when thoroughly looked into and
> understood, it is simple.  We are all too human and in our
> sometimes frantic efforts to solve our daily problems, we
> lose sight of the few underlying principles which give life
> meaning.  As life goes on and develops, it becomes more
> complicated too, but these complications need not be a
> source of unnecessary trouble or despair.
> 
> There are only a few principles after all.  The trouble is
> that we too often lose sight of these few principles
> temporarily and become confused.  Do not let me confuse you
> now.  I wish to talk with you about principles and not mere
> formulas.  A story is told of a great oriental potentate who
> set his particular brain trust of the moment to the problem
> of devising a saying which would be good on all occasions.
> After many months of research and deliberation and almost
> endless discussions they returned to his court and presented
> him with this formula of magic words:  "And this, too, shall
> pass."
> 
> This maxim explains what I mean by a superficial formula,
> good on most occasions, but what does it amount to?  Nothing
> at all for practical [[Purpose|purposes]].  To illustrate further the
> importance of understanding basic principles, take the
> locomotive as an example.  You all know that it has been
> gadgetized almost beyond belief.  If an old timer of the
> pre-nineties era were to come back today he would be amazed
> at the never-ending line of improvements and gadgets found
> on the modern locomotive.  There is the mechanical stoker,
> the superheater, power reverse gear, automatic signaling
> devices and whatnot.  Yet the old timers - and they knew
> their stuff, too - would not necessarily get lost.  They
> would immediately see through it all; and beginning at the
> firebox follow the conversion of the energy of the fuel into
> steam.  They would see the same old FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE of
> the piston and the crank assisting to convert the energy of
> the steam into tractive power for the drivers.
> 
> Today we are all aware of the tremendous social and economic
> forces raging in unconfined fury in many parts of the world.
> As free men of this great republic it is our duty to
> ourselves, our families and future generations to prepare
> ourselves to preserve and to defend our institutions.  We
> can do this by vigilant and intelligent adherence to a FEW
> PRINCIPLES.  Knowing these principles, like the engineer
> knowing the simple steam creation and conversion process
> which makes the locomotive go, we can also become efficient
> engineers in the guidance of our own individual and national
> [[Destiny|destiny]].
> 
> FREE AMERICAN ENTERPRISE
> 
> We are all free men matured in the traditions of our
> pioneering forefathers.  If you had to choose the one
> principle which makes life worthwhile, and without it
> life unbearable, what would it be?  Think for a moment.
> Ponder that question now.  But let me ask you, are you
> thinking about the "power principle" which makes the
> locomotive go or the superheater and whatnot gadgets
> that only help it along?  Remember, it is the principle
> that we want.  You know what I am driving at.  The principle
> which I have in mind is the one which built this country, the
> one which expanded it, the one which maintains it, and the
> one which will continue to maintain it if we fully understand
> it, preserve it, and keep it working.  This great principle,
> which is essential to happy American life, is FREE AMERICAN
> PRINCIPLE.
> 
> "Enterprise" has been defined in many ways but we all have a
> pretty good idea of what the word means.  Dictionaries define
> "enterprise" as an undertaking, an attempt to perform
> something, to create something, to bring about something.
> In the background we all instinctively realize when we use
> the word "enterprise" that there must be power, boldness,
> originality, daring, spirit, creativeness.  "Enterprise" is
> a part of all of us.  It is ONE of the things which MAKES a
> man.  We must guard it carefully and cultivate and use it.
> We must guard against having our enterprise frustrated by
> shortsighted social and economic policies.  Without
> enterprise we are not worthy of the name of man.  Without
> enterprise there is no freedom and without freedom there can
> be no enterprise.  As Ruskin said in reference to
> ENTERPRISE, "YOU enterprised a railroad through the valley,
> you blasted ITS ROCKS away."
> 
> [[Photography (Atlantean)|Picture]] to yourselves those early pioneers who dreamed a
> [[Dreams|dream]] and made it come true.  They, who built the original
> railroads, spanned this continent with bands of steel, and
> helped to make us a great and powerful nation, intimate with
> two great oceans.  You are the direct heirs of those great
> pioneers.  They had the original freedom to build these great
> roads.  Yours is the duty to continue to man them in the same
> spirit of enterprise.  Yours is the duty not only to
> yourselves, to your families, but to the unlimited
> posterity of this great country to keep alive and maintain
> the great tradition of American enterprise.
> 
> Without it you will end up slaves; not necessarily with
> actual chains about your necks.  Labor can be the slave
> victims of inadequate wage or improper working conditions.
> Inadequate wages and working conditions mean a chain about
> your neck and a cast-iron ball forged to your foot.  These
> shackles can hem you in on all sides, restrict your freedom,
> and kill your spirit.  But there have been vast advances in
> these matters through the leadership your organization has
> manifested for almost three score years and ten.
> 
> By this time you will probably wonder why I am talking so
> much about this one subject.  It is because it is so
> important to all of us.  It is necessary that we understand
> it fully.  Once we do understand it, it becomes a part of
> us, and like the great athlete who has developed his
> muscles, we can also make our minds strong in the possession
> of this great principle.  Through the use and application of
> it we can never be enslaved or regimented.  You, who today
> are facing one of the greatest wage battles of your entire
> history, must go into the front lines clear-headed and
> clear-eyed, determined to fight for what you demand.
> 
> But first you must know and understand what you want, how to
> get it, and how to preserve it; consistent with the general
> good of your industry and the nation.  Your battle is just,
> it is right, it is necessary, like all such moves it involves
> great understanding and the use of intelligent and far-seeing
> strategy.  I have the utmost confidence in your generals.  I
> also have the greatest confidence in the leaders on the side
> of management.  I know from long experience in such affairs
> that something constructive will come out of this present
> action.  But in order to assure it, to make assurance doubly
> sure, you must understand this great principle of enterprise
> and stand as one man in back of your leaders.  It is not only
> a question of how much you will gain out of this present
> struggle, it is a question of how much you must also maintain
> for the benefit of us all.  And what must you maintain?  That
> is what I have been talking about.  It is enterprise.  It is
> freedom.
> 
> The labor movement as embodied in the idea of unionism
> represents a phase of American enterprise of monumental
> proportions.  Labor has undertaken many actions in the past
> and will doubtlessly undertake many more in the future.
> But with each action there is an inevitable and inescapable
> responsibility attached.  Labor, too, has understood and
> taken this responsibility.  Organized labor has been a large
> influence and an important element in achieving higher wages,
> shorter hours and better working conditions for all wage
> earners.
> 
> You are all principally occupied with a great struggle today
> involving your financial relationships with the management
> of the industry which you so efficiently man.  This struggle
> is right and just and your proved leadership will see you
> successfully through it as always in the past.  Consider
> from the larger social and economic aspects, your struggle
> has much greater meaning to you and to all of us than the
> settlement of new terms in your financial and working
> relationships.  Both you and management are working within
> the great area of our traditional freedom and joint
> activities are partly responsible for the ever increasing
> fruits of American enterprise.  It is the duty of both of
> you to see that the conditions and opportunities of free
> enterprise are cultivated and improved and not stunted or
> destroyed.
> 
> LABOR MUST NOT BE REGIMENTED
> 
> Elsewhere, in great areas throughout the world we have seen
> the hideous monster of ancient times and the dark middle ages
> arise in the form of a super-state to actually crush the body
> and spirit of men.  Such a thing cannot happen here if labor
> fulfills its obligation to society in general.  And what is
> the obligation of labor?  Part of it, as I see it, is to
> restrict with everything in their power the undue
> encroachment of dictatorial and arbitrary policies from any
> source which might endanger American enterprise.  Labor
> acting through the process of unionism is one of the
> greatest bulwarks for the protection of the American way of
> life.  Unionism is born in freedom and only thrives under
> conditions of freedom.  So, in order that unionism may
> continue to grow successfully and serve the community, it
> must insist at all times that the conditions and privileges
> of freedom be maintained.  Unionism must be ever on its
> guard to fight all attempts to reduce its freedom of action,
> it must fight efforts to totalitarianize labor in one big
> union.
> 
> In the days of old Czarist [[Russia|Russia]] there was little
> opportunity and no freedom for the worker to combine with
> his fellow workers and successfully create conditions to
> improve their lot.  And for all practical [[Purpose|purposes]], [[Russia|Russia]]
> today differs little from that of the pre-revolutionary
> country, and Nazism is no better.  Someone aptly said, not
> so long ago, that [[Russia|Russian]] Communism today was only Czarism
> in overalls.  In free and constitutionally-governed America
> we have witnessed the greatest and most successful
> development of intelligent unionism.
> 
> That should prove to you for all times that successful
> unionism only flourishes on FREE soil.  That should be the
> tip to you to keep the soil ever free.  Today, we see labor
> and unionism crushed and regimented in the dictator-scourged
> countries.  On the other hand, in free America you may
> witness instances of capital groups collaborating with labor
> to forward and preserve the independent action of labor
> organization.  As an example, a dozen years ago, as Dave
> Robertson knows, I joined him in an effort to sustain the
> right of labor to collective bargaining, and actively
> placed an association or railroad security owners behind
> that policy.
> 
> All of this should point out to you the necessity for
> obtaining your demands successfully through the use of
> native American methods.  Equally important, you must
> insist that only such native American methods be employed
> by management and government in their dealings with you.
> It was a wise man who observed that the frontier borders
> of Utopia would be found to be defended by raging
> intolerance.  You must always be vigilant to guard your
> many American opportunities to work toward a national
> Utopia, with jealous intolerance coupled with great
> tolerance.  Be intolerant of all foreign dictatorial,
> unconstitutional, and beastly attempts to wreck our American
> way of life.  But be tolerant of the other American groups
> which are concerned in your dealings.  Labor alone could
> not have built the railroads.  It took money, large amounts
> of money, to blast the way for a band of steel across the
> nation.  It took money and [[Visions|vision]] to buy equipment.  It
> still takes money to run a railroad.  Those who have
> invested in America by investing in the railroads deserve
> consideration.  Likewise, the American public has a stake
> in railroad performance.  You have a duty to all Americans
> as well as to yourselves in your dealings.
> 
> In all our dealings with other men, there unfortunately come
> periods of strain from misunderstandings.  These strains,
> when carried to the breaking point, sometimes result in
> serious struggles and even violence.  As intelligent
> individuals, we should always do our best to understand
> and to be understood.  This understanding can be best brought
> about through knowing as much about the relationship existing
> between ourselves and others as is possible.  In every
> relationship there are certain great fundamentals.  These
> fundamentals usually will be found along the line of
> enlightened self-interest.  There is nothing stronger
> in a normal man than the urge to promote his own best
> self-interest.  That is the basis of self-respecting
> egotism.  A healthy egotism is almost like the steam welling
> up against the piston head.  It turns the wheel.
> 
> Well, then, if we look into this business of self-interest,
> we find that labor and management have a tremendous field
> in common.  That all-important field is American enterprise.
> 
> Destroy that field and you not only destroy the relationships
> but you destroy both labor and management.  That field
> reaches from the [[Atlantic Ocean|Atlantic]] to the [[Pacific Ocean|Pacific]].  And in these
> days of growing hemispheric solidarity, the field reaches
> from the north pole to the south pole; it reaches from the
> very middle of the earth to the highest point in the heavens.
> 
> So you see labor and management may seem to be very far apart
> in some of the more secondary parts of their relationship.
> They are in reality one in their common responsibility for
> preserving American enterprise.  It is the duty of both to
> maintain all of their negotiations well inside of this great
> principle so that it may be preserved for both of them, so
> that they may both prosper through it, within it, and on
> account of it.  Now you can see even more clearly why I
> suggested that you be intolerant of any un-American attacks
> on this great principle.
> 
> The dictatorship countries would not have been possible if
> it had not been for the gradual but steady attacks on the
> free unionism movement.  It was through a process of
> continual chiseling and gradual wearing away of the rights
> of labor that the whole institution was undermined, degraded,
> and finally crushed.
> 
> That can never happen here, if you men will cherish and
> protect American enterprise.  And one way you can protect
> it is through a greater comprehension and appreciation of
> the varying viewpoints of your fellowmen.  By and large we
> are all pretty much alike.  We have similar appetites,
> desires and aspirations.  Furthermore, labor and management
> have a joint self-interest in the preservation and extension
> of free American enterprise.  This sameness in fundamentals
> throughout all human nature was cryptically expressed by
> [[Abraham Lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] when he said:  "I hold that if the Almighty
> had ever made a set of men that should do all the eating and
> none of the work, He would have made them with mouths only
> and no hands; and if He had ever made another class that He
> intended should do all the work and no eating, He would have
> made them with hands only and no mouths."
> 
> MUTUAL UNDERSTANDING NECESSARY
> 
> I know my own experience has convinced me of the truth that
> in fundamentals human nature is one - is the same everywhere.
> In the past two years I have been making a special study of
> human relationships and found some very remarkable facts
> developing from the process of getting people to understand
> each other.  We made a national survey within the past two
> years for the [[Purpose|purpose]] of determining the best method of
> finding out about these relationships between government,
> management, finance and labor.
> 
> Since that time we have held nine national round-table
> conferences with representatives of labor, management,
> finance and government gathered round the dinner table.
> In addition, numerous local conferences were held with the
> same setup in a number of different parts of the country.
> Besides the valuable material developed, the results were
> always the same; a better understanding and appreciation
> of the other fellow's point of view.  This understanding
> arose from the fact that the four groups, upon coming into
> personal contact, discovered that they had a great deal in
> common when they brushed aside their surface attitudes and
> thoughts and really got down to business and talked about
> fundamentals.
> 
> In connection with your own problem, you should not only
> know and act upon these fundamentals but you must insist

> with the work may enjoy the opportunity it offers for making
> a vital contribution toward the establishment of peace,
> happiness, and security - not only for the members
> themselves, but setting an example for all mankind.
> 
> In order to keep pace with the inevitable changes that are
> taking place over the world, it is most important for those
> of us who have an interest in the kind of world in which we
> live, and which posterity will honor us for, to begin making
> plans for its structure.
> 
> To start making these plans, "will of course," involve some
> sort of decision, you might ask at this point upon what a
> right decision should be based?
> 
> To that query my answer is, on that which gives you a wider
> field of service, so that all your powers and gifts, your
> acquired talents and initiative may be called into play and
> give you that rounded out development which will equip you
> for a most useful life of activity in the world of the
> future.
> 
> Another important factor which should be taken into account,
> is also, that which can be done best by you and you alone.
> 
> In the pursuit of happiness, the main problem for some of
> you, "I'm sure," as well as my own, has been to find your
> real place in the scheme of life where you could hammer
> away on the castle of your [[Dreams|dreams]].
> 
> Forget not, there are certain activities which burn a person
> out without giving him much satisfaction along the road of
> life, even though the task at hand, is performed in seeming
> perfection.
> 
> While the aim in this collaboration must be fluid in
> adjustment, definite steps will be taken for giving one the
> chance of a better day in vocational activities wherein he
> may enjoy life to the fullest, in his daily work and along
> the lines which means most to himself.
> 
> In the discovery of one's self, he also discovers his
> responsibilities and his brothers as fellow pilgrims on the
> path.  He, consequently, pledges himself to the goal and
> thus, by superseding his own will by the purpose, he
> achieves that balance of nature, regardless of material
> successes, that is so essential in seeking first, the
> Kingdom of God and His righteousness.
> 
> It has been said that union does everything when it is
> perfect.  It satisfies desires, simplifies needs, foresees
> the wishes and becomes a constant fortune.
> 
> To secure this perfect union, I can say with confidence that
> the plan must embody certain fundamental principles that are
> ranged on the side of [[Law of Moses|the Law]].
> 
> It makes no difference whether one is starting a new
> business, sponsoring a Law, running a railroad or writing
> a play.  The human nature aspect cannot be escaped.
> 
> If the play or project does not check with human nature, it
> simply will not work out.  The answer is written in advance.
> 
> It is not my purpose to particularize on the plan tonight,
> but basically, I should say the most acceptable standard
> would be to construct its frame work upon a foundation which
> typifies in its pattern the fundamentals upon which the ideal
> family life are based, consisting partly of young and some of
> older [[Soul|souls]].
> 
> It is a work that must be carried on entirely with no attack
> upon any political party or group and no criticism of any
> leader or national activity.
> 
> Such old methods have long been tried but have failed to
> bring peace on earth.  This is a movement that will stand
> before the bars of judgement and in the eyes of the world
> be awarded, "in justice," according to its value to the
> human society, whom it seeks to serve.
> 
> Up to now, the plan is only in the paper stage, but as
> Mrs. Bailey has stated, after seeing the revised script,
> which apparently gave her a more complete version of the
> idea, it is now time to call a group together and begin
> to put the idea into a concrete form, and so, to that end,
> my purpose now is to discover an individual here, and there,
> who have the faith and foresight, the needed patience and
> courage to identify and to dedicate themselves as a charter
> member of a seed group for promoting this pioneering
> enterprise in the cause of Christ and humanity.
> 
> Then leaving its unfoldment and growth to the Great Laws of
> God, which provides every blessing in abundance and ample
> means for every good enterprise.
> 
> The ideal way, "I believe," to get going would be to carry
> out the suggestion indicated by a reading given on the plan
> by Mr. Edgar Cayce before his death.
> 
> That is to call together a group of three, five, nine,
> twelve, or fifteen to prepare and draw from the plan and
> ideals a charter for such an organization.  This necessitates
> of course, the employment of a lawyer in order to go through
> the regular channel.
> 
> According to this authentic source of information the
> preferred number to be chosen for this procedure is twelve,
> though, as stated, it could be done with three but not less
> than three.
> 
> I hold that it is possible as a unified group, or groups of
> people with a conscious social aim, to discover a system
> that is fashioned after the Natural Laws of the Universe.
> A system that is so simple and orderly arranged that the
> law of supply and demand will carry all of its people
> "especially" the workers and the poor to an even higher
> standard of life, a standard that is limited only by out
> [our?] effective desires and our united wills.
> 
> In this undertaking, there are three things to do.  They are
> to think clearly and calmly then keep going, but the most
> important of these and the most difficult, "I believe," is
> to start.
> 
> In arriving at your conclusion upon the study of this work,
> I would like to ask that you keep in mind the flexibility
> of its purpose, as it has nothing to do with controlling
> nor dictating one's religious views, except, of course,
> to strive earnestly in the directing of thought along the
> channels of truth and then leaving the individual to the
> processes of his own nature.
> 
> The main requisite for affiliation with the movement is first
> of all, sincerity, then honesty, integrity, and tolerance,
> and last but not least, is a firm belief that the happiness
> and success of one's neighbor is as important as his own
> and that he is willing to seek in behalf of others, the same
> things that he asks and seeks for himself.
> 
> Neither oath nor vow will be required, why?  Because they are
> neither needed nor wanted.  True Brotherhood is automatic.
> When a man becomes a member, one of two things will happen,
> either he will catch the spirit and be an asset, or he or she
> may fail to register in accord with [[Law of Moses|the law]] and drop out.
> 
> In the former case an oath or vow is needless, and in the
> latter, a handicap.  Furthermore, this Brotherhood will have
> nothing to keep secret.  In fact, it should have everything
> to broadcast.
> 
> As you look forth upon this idea for instant response to
> every emergency, I would feel very grateful for any
> consideration you may think it deserves.
> 
> The discourse tonight has been mainly for proclaiming the
> plan without divulging its particulars, but if God is willing
> and you will be kind enough to permit one or two assemblies
> at some future dates, I should be glad to give you more
> definite details, and try to explain in a more specific
> manner, some of the objectives, as well as the ways and
> means which are proposed for attaining same.
> 
> Now, my "friends", in conclusion, I want you to know that
> many factors beside those I have stated are responsible for
> this endeavor.
> 
> While the letters and comments I have received concerning the
> work have been fine and encouraging, without the sustaining
> fortitude which I have experienced through Soul stimulant and
> inspiration from within, it would have been impossible, "I
> believe," to carry on for so long in the privacy which I have
> endured, especially so in the face of pressing circumstance
> which have crowded about me in the performance of ordinary
> duties in my material work.
> 
> Though these duties have been hard and binding from without,
> the freedom within, which to me, has been a witness of the
> Spirit, has afforded me great inner joy and satisfaction all
> along the path, for under such circumstance, one has the
> opportunity to observe the opening and closing of doors,
> and thus, he soon learns that the disciple in training has
> to cultivate that spiritual response which enables him to
> know through which door his Soul desires him to pass.  [1757]
