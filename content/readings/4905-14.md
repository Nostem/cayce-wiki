# TEXT OF READING 4905-14

**Reading [[4905-14]]** · Series: 3001-5747 · Year: 1925 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 1/29/1925  Sex: M  ReadingID: 12939

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at his office, 322 Grafton Avenue, [[Dayton Ohio|Dayton]], [[Ohio|Ohio]], this 29th day of January, 1925, in accordance with request made by Mr. M. B. Wyrick - an involuntary reading given without the suggestion being presented, immediately following a physical reading for Case [4844].

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading                               Bosque County, 
10:35 A. M. [[Dayton Ohio|Dayton]] Time.                      [[Texas|Texas]].

1. GC:  Now, as to the conditions regarding the developments in the lands of Madison Byron Wyrick, in North Bosque County, [[Texas|Texas]], we find there have been hindrances and delays in the operations, both by the physical conditions of those about and connected with operations, of conditions in the hole itself, conditions with the tools and weather.

2. EC:  Now we find these are improving, and only needs keep on keeping on, until we will bring the desired results, for this will prove a successful operation.  Then, give as this: Conditions on improve.  Best push forward, and do not be discouraged.  Success is coming.

---

> OSCAR GUMBINSKY DIES SUDDENLY; WAS CAPITALIST
> 
> Oscar Gumbinsky, well known capitalist, died yesterday of
> heart failure.  He was stricken at the breakfast table and
> was dead before a physician reached his home.
> 
> For some months his financial affairs have been in the
> courts, he having been engaged in a controversy with [[Joseph (Husband of Mary)|Joseph]]
> Byfield over the Boone Woolen mills, of which he had been
> president.  The woolen mills brought suits against him for
> $100,000 and for $250,000.  He sued Byfield for $2,000,000,

> 
> He was the promoter of the International Lamp company, but
> recently lost control of it.  Another of his financial-legal
> tangles was his suit against the owners of the Ashland block
> to sell him the building for $4,000,000 under a contract he
> said existed.
> 
> Mr. Gumbinsky was 39 years old.  He lived at 4755 Drexel
> Boulevard.  He is survived by his wife, Anne, whose maiden
> name was Ciralsky, and one daughter, Ruth.
> 
> The funeral will be held Thursday at 10 o'clock from the
> chapel at 936 East 47th street.  Burial will be at Waldhelm.

> Dear Mr. Wyrick:-
> 
> I'm certainly glad to have yours of the 21st.  To be sure,
> we've been quite anxious regarding the conditions around

> troublesome bit and I certainly am in hopes that you will be
> successful in getting this out without much further trouble.
> I hope that having to drill down on top of this won't so set
> this that it will make it difficult to get out.  The under
> reaming I am sure has been slow and tedious.  Glad to know
> though that you've gotten it in good shape and that you have
> the show of the oil and gas as indications of success.
> 
> I have been most anxious regarding your own physical
> condition, and if I had known where to find you I think I
> would have taken a reading on you before this.  I certainly
> hope for the very best results with the observations you are
> of necessity having to have made, and I certainly trust it
> will prove most beneficial to you.  I'm glad though that
> you're feeling pretty good and I trust that you will continue
> to do so, and I am sure with the properties that you must
> be taking at this time it will help you to keep in the right
> way, provided you don't overtax yourself physically, which
> I'm in hopes you won't do.  You realize that you must not
> allow yourself to become too much worried just at this time,
> for worry will create as many conditions to be overcome as
> incorrect diet would, under the circumstance.  Not that you
> don't already know this, but merely as a reminder of the
> condition.
> 
> I certainly will thank you to let me have a wire from you
> as soon as they're able to get this bit removed and you're
> ready to go on with the operations.  If you should feel it
> is possible for me to be of any assistance, of course don't
> hesitate.
> 
> I certainly thank you for the little clippings [re Mr.
> Gumbinsky.  See Par. R2 and R3, above] you enclosed.  I'm
> indeed sorry to know that this came to such a point that
> it just worried the existence out of this man, for as you
> know, while I was in [[Chicago|Chicago]], I came to know about these
> circumstances from first hand, as well as the information
> we obtained in the reading and realized the effect they were
> having on Mr. Gumbinsky.  He was warned of this condition,
> but like so many of us pressure from other places was so
> great that he was not able to heed it as he should.  This is

> out of him.
> 
> Yes, we have had some very interesting readings and feeling

> furnish as much to you as you would like, yet we don't want
> to worry you too much with them.  I'm enclosing you, then,

> think we would have been able then to have had a whole lot of
