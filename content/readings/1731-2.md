# TEXT OF READING 1731-2 M 35

**Reading [[1731-2]]** · Series: 1501-2000 · Year: 1938 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 11/28/1938  Sex: M  Age: 35  ReadingID: 7932

This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at his home on Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 28th day of November, 1938, in accordance with request made by the self - Mr. [1731], Associate Member of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc., through Mr. [849].

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading 
10:50 to 11:00 A. M. Eastern Standard Time.    ..., [[Florida|Florida]].

1. GC:  You will have before you the Life Reading given November 3rd for [1731], born June 6, 1903 in Buffalo, N.Y., now living at ..., Fla.  [[Entity (Cayce Term)|The entity]] having chosen to undertake the writing as suggested in this Reading seeks further and specific guidance for beginning and carrying forward his work in this field.  Please correlate the urges further regarding this ability and give directions that will be helpful in taking the next steps.  You will answer the questions he has submitted, as I ask them:

2. EC:  Yes, we have the information as given for [1731].

3. In considering all circumstances in the present, as we find it would be well to accept the offers for the material security in part offered; but well that [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]] apply self in preparation for cultivating the abilities in the field of repartee as may be considered in those things given.

4. Under the circumstance, and connecting with the duties thus involved, it will require that there be such a connection as might be obtained through association with such as Hamilton Institute for preparation in that character of endeavor.

5. As to those efforts already made, in such publications as Sunset Magazine - or through such agencies as may be designated or indicated through connection with such an institution as Hamilton, may be an outlet for the endeavors.

6. Considering, then, that which has been indicated in the information from the life experiences, there is seen the MENTAL attitude, - and if this is then made with the innate and manifested desire to be constructive in such, there only needs be the effort put forth to bring the greater opportunities, and opening ways - through the efforts - for the succeeding of [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]]'s efforts in those directions.

7. Ready for questions.

8. (Q)  Is the writing, as being done by the entity, of the nature required?
(A)  This as we find is leading in the right direction.  One that the entity might read, for the awakening of the abilities, is [[John (Apostle)|John]] Kendrick Bangs.

9. (Q)  Should the entity attempt such as newspaper column, advertising, fiction, radio script, etc.?
(A)  As has been indicated, with the trainings, it will ALL be a part of the entity's efforts at times.  But those such as booklets, or as might be an inducement or incentive for others in conversation or speech AS repartee, would be the more efficient outlet for the entity; though the advertising may become an ACCEPTABLE part, AN outlet for the efforts of the entity.

10. (Q)  Considering all, what should be the first steps toward this preparation?
(A)  As indicated.  As circumstances, now, are to be somewhat altered, - through [[Air Shafts (Pyramid)|the channels]] as indicated would be the better manner.  But by ALL MEANS prepare self FOR the abilities in the writing field!  No matter how much time or how much effort may be necessary for the material conditions, prepare the self mentally and materially for the exercising of the abilities.

11. We are through for the present.

Copy to Self c/o [849] - Special Delivery
"    " Ass'n file

Wired to [849]

> R1. 11/30/38 Mr. [849]'s letter:  "As you probably have guessed,
> Mr. [1731] is more or less in a bewildered state about his
> new life but his reading this morning seemed to satisfy him
> and he went off determined to follow it."
> 
> R2. 6/19/39 [1731]'s letter to [849]:
> 
> 
> Dear [849] -
> 
> After three or four attempts to produce something even
> remotely constructive, things have become so serious, I beg
> for your help.  It's Jamie.  [Fictional character of one of
> [849]'s books.]  The moment I write a word he starts his evil
> tactics, zipping across the table, sticking his tongue out
> and more often than not, his thumb is at his nose while his
> fingers wave frantically.  When I attempt to correct a page,
> he jumps into the little cupboard, slams the lid down noisily
> and rings the bell, shouting, "He don't know nothin'.  He has
> to re-write."  But that's not all.  Commas bring out his true
> self.  "Go back to school, you lug," he sneers.  Twice he
> broke my pencil point and when he was still outraged because
> they continued to appear, he knocked my pencil out of my hand
> to the floor.  I have removed the bell but to no end for he
> flips the lid as if it were the catch on an ice box door,
> jumps in an effort to beat his last mark and shouts, "You
> can't concentrate, your story's like a fifth grade essay,
> boy, what slush."  This morning he had all three of our
> watches, demanding to know which one was his.  I plead with
> you to finish with him, get him bound so that he will be
> proud and contented until a critic takes a whack at him like
> he is doing to me.
> 
> Your brutish friend blubbered tears of anguish in his coffee
> because he carried you wrong side in and I expected he was
> going to take to strong drink.  However, I talked him in to
> going fishing so that he might forget.  Later I noticed a
> high tide so I think he went swimming instead.
> 
> I hope you and [1521] made Washington without being overcome
> by nature's unrestricted sense of duty, although I presume
> [1102] might be the object of no little concern inasmuch as
> it was her first flight.  Anyway, may you be resting easy and
> drugless when you read this wish that your trip was not an
> ordeal but a pleasure.
> 
> [Wife] thanks you for your thoughtfulness; to wit:  (there
> goes Jamie again).  Thanks, [849].
> 
> Here's luck to you.
> 
> but I say, Let's stop talkin',
> Get to walkin'.
> 
> I suspect it's been difficult for you to read this far but
> paper and pencil cage my thoughts and I pray the forthcoming
> reading will assist me.  I feel like a traveler who is lost
> but believes he could make the journey if he knew the proper
> direction and the distance of it so that he might conserve
> his food and strength.  The following questions may not be in
> proper form for the reading, so please edit them as you see
> fit.
> 
> You will have before you the body and enquiring mind of
> [1731], ..., Fla., who seeks information, advice and guidance
> as to his life work, and just how he may proceed to get
> started on it.  You will answer the questions he has
> submitted as I ask them:
> 
> 1. Should [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]] write short stories for magazines?  If
> not what should it write?
> 2. If [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]] continues it's efforts as it is now doing,
> could returns be expected this year or should it seek
> employment to bring about financial assistance?
> 3. If employment is necessary for [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]], where could it
> be found?
> 4. Will the entity be able to make a living by writing, in
> the near future?
> 5. Is the Hardy agency the proper outlet for the writings of
> the entity?
> 6. Is the entity on the right course in thought, effort and
> work, to bring about happiness and security for it's family?
> 
> In regard to the first question, you know how much I am at
> sea about what I should do, if and when I can.
> 
> Best of luck for a swift recovery and please thank Mr. Cayce
> for his kind interest in me.
> 
> Just a five o'clock boy.  [1731]
> 
> P.S.  As I read the above questions, I'm sure you can clarify
> them so as to bring out the TYPE OF STORIES and the course I
> should pursue to LEAD to accomplishment, as well as what
> further preparation is necessary.
> 
> I'm sure you understand that it is not PATIENCE that I lack
> (as I did before) but it is vital that we conserve our small
> bank account until I get something other than a rejection
> slip.
> 
> R3. 7/12/39 See 1731-3.
