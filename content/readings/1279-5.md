# TEXT OF READING 1279-5

**Reading [[1279-5]]** · Series: [[801-1500 — Psychological and Dream Interpretation Readings|801-1500]] · Year: 1936 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 10/20/1936  Sex: F  ReadingID: 6898

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at his home on Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 20th day of October, 1936, in accordance with request made by the self - Mr. [1066], Associate Member of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno. L. B. Cayce.]

Time of Reading 
3:55 to 4:05 P. M. Eastern Standard Time. 
(There is a myth or legend of Capt. Calico Jack Rackham, 
notorious pirateer and pirate, having buried booty on an 
island in the Everglades, [[Florida|Florida]], supposedly north of Oyster 
Bay.  Can or will you locate this, and tell us what you can 
about how it might be recovered?)

1. EC:  Yes.

2. As we find from those contacts here, this has been given through these channels as respecting same, that Rackham's booty, on the island in Lostman's River, above the tide and at the juncture of a tributary some nine and seven-tenths miles from what would be then the coast, or coastal area. [See 1279-1 through 1279-4.]

3. This when put there was in what was a portion of an old tree, inside.  This as we find, owing to settlements or activities that have gone on, is covered with debris; but may be located best with the measurements and then with the mercury and the electropoid that is sensitive to gold - though this treasure is not all gold but other booty also.

4. We are through.

Copy to [1066]
"    " Ass'n file

> Dear Mr. Cayce:

> Received your letter some while back and enjoyed it very

> regard to the history of your life work.

> I have a most unusual request to make.  It has been brought
> to my attention that a considerable amount of gold coin is
> located in a certain section of a certain state.  The source
> from which I have received this information is presumably
> authentic.  The exact topographical position of this money is
> not known.  I presume you have not given up hope that someday
> [[Atlantic University|Atlantic University]] will be established and in view of this
> fact is it possible that the information could give the exact
> location if more specific details were supplied you.  Let it
> be understood that if this money is located it is NOT TO
> BENEFIT ANYONE PERSONALLY.  This, in my opinion is a strange
> request, but I would like to know just what you think about
> the matter.  It might be someone's foolish fancy, but I would
> like to know what is in back of it all.  Please advise in
> haste at the above Hotel.
> 
> Best regards to you and yours, [1066]

> 
> 
> Dear [1066]-
> 
> Thanks for yours of the 26th, which just came yesterday.  I
> hope I don't miss catching you at the address you gave.
> 
> We are always glad to hear from you.
> 
> Under separate cover we are sending to you a copy of [[Thomas Sugrue|Sugrue]]'s
> preface to the book.
> 
> Now, regarding the attempt to locate coin, this has been
> undertaken quite a number of times through this channel and
> with some very disturbing results.  Some of the best
> friendships I think I have ever had have been broken up over
> disputes when the readings gave such information about the
> location of treasure.  Some of the best friendships have
> possibly been cemented by being able to help locate something
> that has been helpful.  So, I wouldn't know what to say.
> I have often felt like that there are such unusual
> circumstances usually concerning the locating of a thing of
> the kind that in seeking through this channel from possibly
> those who were connected with such a treasure, the attitudes
> are not always in keeping with the [[Ideal|ideals]] and [[Purpose|purposes]] of
> the individual seeking nor with my own.  Consequently,
> confusions, disturbances, and the lack of any direct data.
> Of course, I understand that your idea that no personal
> benefit from such a seeking would come to anyone is
> idealistic.  Naturally there would be some expense, possibly
> a lot of work, and there might not be anything come from it
> at all.  But if you think you have such a proposition that
> could be gotten in shape, and you wanted to undertake it,
> with the understanding that all of these things that may work
> out might be nothing, absolutely nothing at all, why we will
> be glad to do what we can.  I always want to help; I think I
> have some [[Ideal|ideals]] - maybe I don't always act in a way to
> further those properly.  But I'll be glad to at least
> entertain such a proposition and to see what can be done.
> 
> Thanking you for your letter and  hoping this finds you
> feeling the very best, I am - with all good wishes from each
> and every one here -
> 
> Sincerely, [EC:GD]

> tidal wave.

> discovered by two negro brothers - Cato and Scipio Edwards,
> who came upon a curious mound one day while wandering through

> struck through the thin layer of topsoil with a boathook and
> discovered that an upturned ship lay beneath.
> 
> "With an axe they cut through her ancient timbers and entered
> a dark, evil-smelling cavern almost filled with hanging

> know his way.  After searching for several years without
