This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at his father''s home on South Virginia St., [[Hopkinsville|Hopkinsville]], Ky., this 27th day of November, 1924, in accordance with request by Mr. [3775]''s brother, Mr. [244].

[Edgar Cayce; L. B. Cayce, Conductor; Miss Frith, Steno. Mr. [3775]''s sons, [...] and [1427].]

Room 322, Jennie 
Time of Reading                           Stuart Hospital, 
11:00 P. M.                               [[Hopkinsville|Hopkinsville]], Ky. 
(Physical Suggestion)

1. EC:  Yes, we have the body here now.  We find there are serious conditions with the body at the present time.  These conditions have been produced by specific conditions that have existed in the body for times back and conditions that have been accorded the body have aggravated the conditions and made it of a specific nature.  Now these are the conditions, as we find them, in the body of [3775].

2. First, in the blood supply, this we find is carrying, and has been carrying for some time, that baccilli [bacilli] or that stretococa [streptococcus] that produces inflammation and pus in the system.  These we find are produced first by a congestion in the system.  Then the taking of virus in the body, brought to the circulation that condition that produced in the blood system, and especially in the cappilary [capillary] circulation.  These pus forming sacs and this produces temperature at times in the system, especially at certain cycles in circulation, when equilibrium is wont to be made in circulation.

3. In the present conditions we find these pus forming bacilli have become of a nature that the whole system is becoming saturated with this condition and in the lower limb, in the left side, shows condition that brings this to a specific place from a fracture as received in the frontal bone in times back.

4. This reaching that stage where conditions have taken laxness to the creating of structural operations, makes the more specific condition.  By the conditions as shown here these without caustic rusal [removal] will bring malignant conditions unless the scraping of the bone proper and the sealing of same, unless these show their reduction in the system there will be found the necessity of trefinding [trephining] the limb proper, then removing the marrow, for this is showing destructive forces here.  Then, to bring the better conditions to this special portion of the system, necessary that this be done:  Remove those conditions in the limb where these sacs form in the tissue and give internally Life Everlasting.  See?  With the sealing of this fracture, this would reduce under normal conditions this response necessary that trefinding [trephining] operation be preferred by burning and then boring into the bone, and boring them with the formation found in strong solutions, carbolic acid, in the center of the bone.  See.  Then reducing the temperature in system.  In the nerve system we find the reaction from stimulus too, the nerve forces too in the functioning organ we find the system wasting; two conditions. Follow those suggestions as given would we bring [help] to this body of [3775].

[11/27/24 Following above reading, the second son, [1427], reminded his brother of the injury their father had in 1923 on his left leg.]

> R6. 8/5/72 Report by Kenneth O. Cayce, Sr. [son of [3775]]:
> 
> 
> Cayce-Yost, Incorporated (letterhead)
> 
> [[Hopkinsville|Hopkinsville]], [[Kentucky|Kentucky]]
> 
> EDGAR CAYCE
> 
> 1. I have been asked to tell or write about my knowledge of
> Edgar Cayce, who was my first cousin.  His father, Leslie
> Burr Cayce, and my father, Delbert D. Cayce, were brothers
> and the sons of Thomas Jefferson Cayce and Sara Thomas Cayce.
> Edgar Cayce was born in Christian County, [[Kentucky|Kentucky]], about 10
> miles south of [[Hopkinsville|Hopkinsville]] on what was known as the Palmyra
> Pike near Beverly Store.  He attended the Beverly School
> which was one room building, all classes in the same room.
> Some books have been written saying that he was an illiterate
> person, however, this was not true.  He was born on Sunday
> afternoon, March 18, 1877, the only son of Leslie B. and
> Carrie Major Cayce.  He had four sisters.  The only one
> living as of this date is Mrs. Sara C. Hesson, Nashville,
> Tennessee.  Edgar was custodian of the Liberty Christian
> Church as a boy and was an excellent Sunday School teacher at
> Liberty.  He read and studied the Bible, was a very
> interesting person to talk with.  He joined the Liberty
> Church at the age of 12 years in 1889.
> 
> 2. Edgar's father, Leslie B., was a man of neat appearance,
> clothes well pressed, shoes shined, and clean shaved,
> although a man of very modest means.  He was one who was
> always looking for new inventions of his day and several
> instances taking exclusive sales rights for such items as
> patent gates and patent churns, being what we might term in
> today's business early Franchising.  Later, he was an agent
> for the Woodmen of the World Insurance Company.  He was
> friendly, and interested in people.
> 
> 3. His mother was a very quiet person, interested in her
> family and her home.  My recollection is that the family
> lived at 705 West Seventh Street.  They also lived at two
> other locations on West Seventh, at 503 South Jessup, and on
> West 15th Street.  His last home was in [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]],
> Virginia.
> 
> 4. As to my association with him and his readings, I regret
> to say that when he lived in Hopkinsville about 1910, having
> an office then over Hopper's Book Store where he gave
> readings almost every day, I did not take advantage of the
> opportunity to witness readings and did not believe in his
> work.  At no time did he claim to be a doctor or did he
> charge for his readings.  He was later a photographer in
> Bowling Green, [[Kentucky|Kentucky]], and Selma, [[Alabama|Alabama]].  After the death
> of his family, he came back to visit relatives in
> Hopkinsville and was in my home on some of these visits.  He
> usually arranged to be here on Sunday and went with me to
> Liberty Church for the morning service.  There, he met
> friends and relatives of his youth.  A very quiet man, soft
> spoken, but a most interesting person to talk with, well
> informed on many subjects and able to quote scriptures in his
> conversation.  Almost every week I am visited by persons
> wanting to know of Edgar Cayce, asking where he was born,
> where he lived in Hopkinsville, the store he worked for,
> which was located at 704 South Main and is one of the very
> few buildings that has had the same store front on it as it
> did in the early 1900's, known as Hopper Brothers Book Store.
> Many ask if there is any marker or where his grave is
> located, being in front of the chapel in Riverside Cemetery
> on North Main Street.  There is also a marker located just
> north of the main gate of Riverside.
> 
> 5. Many interesting events in his life are given in the
> numerous books published regarding his life and readings.
> 
> 6. The only reading that I witnessed was in Nov. 27, 1924,
> when my father, Delbert D. Cayce was very ill and an
> operation was to be performed on the following day.  An uncle
> of mine called about 9:00 p.m. and said that Edgar was here
> visiting his family on South Virginia Street and would give a
> reading on my father.  We secured a stenographer and went to
> the Leslie Cayce home for this reading.  My brother, Delbert
> D. Cayce, Jr. was present.  Edgar reclined on the couch,
> removing his shoes, loosening his collar.  Very soon his
> father said to him, "Edgar, you are now asleep.  (He was
> breathing very calmly.)  We have the body of Delbert D. Cayce
> before you - go over the body and tell us what you find" -
> Edgar repeated the words "Delbert D. Cayce" two or three
> times - saying "we can't find the body" - "Oh yes," his
> father said, "it is in room 322, Jennie Stuart Hospital."
> Immediately, Edgar said, "Yes, we have the body" - beginning
> to tell how my father was affected, gave him many
> instructions as to how the operation should be performed,
> also saying that the trouble was caused by a very severe
> injury to his left leg and that this had happened about a
> year ago.  Edgar had not seen my father for more than three
> years and had not talked to him while he was in Hopkinsville.
> I had no remembrance about the injury.  However, my brother
> reminded me that [[Lord's Prayer|our father]] had walked across Main Street
> just about dark something like a year ago.  A boy come down
> the street riding a bicycle and the pedal struck my father's
> left leg, tearing his clothing.  However, the wound
> apparently healed and was given no further consideration.
> After being reminded of this I remembered the accident.
> 
> 7. The reading was typed and I went to the hospital the
> following morning.  After the operation, I called the nurse
> who had witnessed it and asked her to read the report.  She
> said that they found the condition as stated in the reading
> and performed the operation as he had recommended, only using
> a different medicine by name but resulting in the same
> effect.  At that time, we would not have dared to show the
> reading to the doctors as they would have given up the case.
> However, thirty years later in talking to the surgeon, who
> operated, Doctor Gant Gaither, he said that he believed in
> Edgar Cayce's readings and his being able to diagnose.  He
> also said that if penicillin had been known about at this
> time, 1924, he could have saved my father's life, as he died
> April 6, 1927.
> 
> 8. I regret that I did not keep a register of the many people
> who have called on me in the last 25 years asking questions
> about Edgar or to tell me of some experience with his
> reading.
> 
> 9. They have been from every walk of life:  doctors,
> teachers, lawyers, ministers, engineers, sometimes they would
> bring their families to listen to what I would have to say.
> 
> 10. In the last few months I have had phone calls from
> several cities, people asking for information.  In the early
> 1950's, a Mr. Russell from Louisville came to see me, he
> spent the night in Hopkinsville and asking the hotel clerk
> where he could find a Cayce.  He said he just wanted to
> relate his experience with Edgar, saying that his wife had
> been ill in the early 1940's and the doctor in Louisville had
> told him that her condition was incurable and she would
> probably have only a short time to live.  About this time, an
> article appeared in the magazine section of the Louisville
> Courier Journal regarding Edgar and his readings.  Mr.
> Russell had a sister, a school teacher, in [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]] and
> he called her to ask about Edgar Cayce.  She said she had
> never heard of the man but would ask some of the teachers at
> school the next day and asked if she could get a reading on
> her sister-in-law.  One teacher knew about him, also she was
> a friend of Miss [[Gladys Davis|Gladys]], Edgar's secretary.  She arranged for
> a reading which was given, a copy being sent to Louisville
> with a certain medicine being recommended.  Mr. Russell said
> he went to several drug stores and could not find one that
> knew about this medicine.  He then called [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]] and
> asked if a mistake had been made.  The answer was no, but
> Edgar gave another reading and was asked where the medicine
> could be obtained.  He immediately gave the name of a drug
> store in Louisville and also the location of the medicine on
> the shelf.  Upon receiving this information, the medicine was
> located.  Mr. Russell said that in three weeks' time his wife
> had recovered and went to the grocery.  At the time he was
> talking with me, she was living in Louisville and in fairly
> good health.  Then he said, "That is why I wanted to talk to
> a Cayce."
> 
> 11. Another instance, a doctor from Kansas City came to see
> me one afternoon saying he saw a sign on 41 Highway South
> with the name CAYCE-YOST and decided to stop in Hopkinsville
> and spend the night.  The motel clerk told him where the
> store was located.  After a few minutes' conversation, he
> said that in the early 1900's, possibly 1910, he was a
> medical student at Harvard University and that one of his
> professors, a Doctor Munsterberg, was absent from class for
> about a week.  Upon his return to the class he said that he
> wanted to tell him of a visit to Kentucky where he went with
> the avowed [[Purpose|purpose]] of exposing a fake, a man who was
> exploiting the public.  He then said that he wanted to report
> that he had failed in this and telling of some of his
> experiences.  The doctor said that Dr. Munsterberg was a very
> intelligent man and that he was convinced after spending some
> time with Edgar and witnessing a reading he felt that he had
> an unusual power.
> 
> 12. I could relate many instances of persons giving their
> personal experiences and also conversation with Edgar.
> Reading books that are available form [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]] for
> Research and Enlightenment of Virginia Beach, Virginia, would
> give anyone interested much information and interesting
> facts.  Also, there are chapters in many cities in the United
> States.  These associations hold regular meetings studying
> the readings and discussing information.  The location of
> these chapters can be obtained from A.R.E. at Virginia Beach.
> 
> Edgar told me of one experience he had in Bowling Green in
> his photographic work.  Buffalo Bill's Wild West Show came to
> this part of Kentucky usually in September or October.  They
> were showing in Bowling Green and Edgar said he wanted to get
> a [[Photography (Atlantean)|picture]] of one of the Indians with the show.  At that time,
> pictures were taken with a [[Photography (Atlantean)|camera]] on a tripod and black cloth
> used with the [[Photography (Atlantean)|camera]].  Edgar was about to take the picture
> when the Indian ran from the shop.  He called him but he did
> not stop.  That was the end of the experience at that time.
> However, almost twenty years late, Edgar was lecturing in one
> of the hotels in [[New York City|New York]] [Yr. 1921, Hotel McAlpin?],
> something that he did quite often.  After the lecture people
> came to speak to him, one being an Indian.  When he took the
> Indian's hand, he said, "Brother Cayce, you don't remember me
> but I know you."  Asked when he had met Edgar or where, he
> replied, "In Bowling Green, Kentucky."  "Oh, you were the man
> whose picture I tried to take.  Why did you run and leave me
> before I got the picture?"  The reply was, "I saw something
> in your eye I didn't like."  Another experience was told to
> me by Dr. Phillip Haynes, who was a physician in Hopkinsville
> for may years.  Dr. Haynes said he was called by Mr. Thomas
> B. House, Jr. to attend his wife when their daughter was
> born.  As the House family had never had Dr. Haynes, he asked
> why they selected him for this occasion.  They said that in a
> reading given by Edgar Cayce he advised them they should have
> Dr. Haynes.  With that explanation, he said that he took the
> case.  After the baby was born, the mother's milk disagreed
> with it.  Edgar recommended that the child be put on a
> Carnation milk formula.  After changing to this milk, the
> child improved and was in good health.
> 
> [signed] Kenneth O. Cayce Sr.
