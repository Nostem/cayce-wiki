## Reports

> [!quote]- Reports & Follow-up
> R1. 8/31/32 See her daughter's reading 605-1 indicating EC had
> given a reading for the mother during pregnancy [early July
> 1919].
> 
> R2. 12/5/33 EC's letter to Mrs. [844], aunt of [605]:  "You do
> not know how happy it makes me to feel that I may have been
> of some little help to [605]...  I do not know that I would
> recognize her on the street, but the word that her mother
> [4925] sent me (which I take to be the truth) for the reading
> that was given her just a few days before she passed on, has
> been a real joy to me, to know that possibly through this
> information a little peace, a little rest even, was hers
> before this child came into the world.  For, the reading told
> her at the time there could be certain things done that would
> bring her physical relief, and that the child would be born
> and would be well, but that she would pass on.  And to have
> been of some little help possibly, as you say, to the child
> in fitting herself to meet the emergencies of life, to meet
> the things that may arise in her own experience, I know must
> bring happiness and a joy we cannot understand to that
> mother."
> 
> R3. 9/22/34 See 605-3 indicating "(the mother would help here
> also)."
> 
> R4. 7/27/57 Sister's letter:  "My sister [4925] was the patient
> referred to in 'EDGAR CAYCE, MAN OF [[Miracles of Jesus|MIRACLES]]' [Page 154-155
> of the paper back edition] by [[Joseph (Husband of Mary)|Joseph]] Millard.  This reading
> on my sister has meant so much to me and my other sisters.
> We would have been worried about my niece [605], because the
> doctors had said she could not live.  She [605] is now 38,
> and I heard her say recently that she has perfect health.  I
> think she is definitely exhibit A for anyone who wants to
> PROVE Edgar Cayce possessed powers beyond human
> comprehension.
> 
> "My sister, [4925], died ten days after the birth of her
> daughter.  Her readings were given in 1919.  I can tell you
> a lot about her for we lived together and there were three
> doctors:  Dr. Haggard of Nashville, Tenn., Dr. Gant Gaither
> - later President of [[Kentucky|Kentucky]] Medical Society, Dr. Ed. Stone,
> all in attendance on my sister, all said this baby could not
> live and did not think the mother would live to give birth to
> the child."
> 
> R5. 5/9/60 Sister's (another sister) report:  "My sister [4925]
> had a condition which now perhaps, with X-Ray, could have
> been detected in its early stages, an operation performed,
> and I think her life could have been saved.
> 
> "We will never be able to prove the unlimited gifts of Edgar
> Cayce.  Referrals were usually made to him when the licensed
> and recognized physicians had exhausted their resources and
> the patient was on the incurable list.
> 
> "In the case of my sister all hope had been abandoned by
> numerous doctors and nurses.  We followed the instructions
> of the local practitioner, but with his limited facilities of
> 1919 he was treating her for a disease she did not have.
> [4925]'s condition grew steadily worse - a baby was expected
> in August - it was obvious that there were complications that
> would require the services of a surgeon.
> 
> "The most famous surgeon in the South was called into
> consultation and assisted by our local surgeon, preformed two
> operations, too late to benefit the patient.  It was
> predicted by the three doctors, and numerous nurses, that the
> mother would not live long enough to give birth to the
> expected baby.
> 
> "At this time of crisis Edgar Cayce was consulted - all hope
> had been abandoned for both mother and baby.  Edgar Cayce
> was in Selma, [[Alabama|Alabama]].  My sister [4925] was in ...,
> [[Kentucky|Kentucky]].  Edgar was told nothing of the nature of the case.
> After the usual preliminaries he said:
> 
> "'We have the body here - there are two living to be
> considered.  It is too late to save the mother but she will
> live to give birth to the baby.  The baby will live, and let
> there be no fear for her.  The condition under which the
> mother is living during pregnancy will not affect this baby,
> and she can live a normal, healthy life.'
> 
> "He prescribed an unheard of concoction comprised of simple
> ingredients with a base made of a brew from the bark of
> slippery elm.  We went to the forest, obtained the bark of
> the slippery elm, prepared the formula, gave it to my sister
> as directed.  [4925] became more comfortable.  The baby,
> expected in August, arrived July 18th on the stroke of
> midnight.  My sister died easily, after naming her child.
> The baby was pathetically weak - so tiny - the doctors
> advised us not to give her the name suggested by the mother,
> they said we would be wasting a family name.  A good
> Christian mother heard of our distress and offered to nurse
> baby [605] with her own child.  This offer we accepted and
> after about six weeks the baby was put on a formula, gained
> weight rapidly, and is now married and has two lovely
> daughters.  She [605] was born July 18, 1919."
> 
> R6. GD's note:  See [605] series for subsequent readings on the
> child [daughter of [4925]].
> 
> R7. 2/16/61 Sister of [4925] took out membership in A.R.E. for
> Mrs. [605], saying:  "This is the baby (now 41 years of age)
> that Edgar said would live when three doctors said she would
> NOT.  [[Hugh Lynn Cayce|Hugh Lynn]] conversed with her when he was here and KNOWS
> she is very much alive and is the mother of two very fine
> daughters."
> 
> R8. 3/1/66 Mrs. [844]'s letter:  "My husband [486]'s sister,
> [4925], had two daughters and the younger one, [605], was
> reared by [486]'s mother when she was 65 yrs. of age.  She
> had to be taken at birth and her mother, [4925], died of
> cancer later."
