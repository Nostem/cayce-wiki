# TEXT OF READING 4905-70

**Reading [[4905-70]]** · Series: [[3001-5747 — Miscellaneous Physical and Life Readings|3001-5747]] · Year: 1927 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 4/25/1927  Sex: M  ReadingID: 12995

This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at his office, 115 West 35th Street, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 25th day of April, 1927, in accordance with request made by Mr. M. B. Wyrick.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Mrs. Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading                               Bosque County, 
4:45 P. M. Eastern Standard Time.             [[Texas|Texas]].

1. GC:  You will have before you [[Well Shaft (Pyramid)|the well]] that has been drilled on the lands of Madison Byron Wyrick in Bosque County, [[Texas|Texas]]. You will give the conditions of this well at the present time, and the possibility of doing anything more with this hole.  Would it be advisable to drill another well?  If so, how and where will it be financed, and where should it be located?

2. EC:  Yes, we have [[Well Shaft (Pyramid)|the well]] as was drilled here.  This we have had before.

3. The possibility of making the present operations a producing well, under the circumstances as exist at present, is little; yet this may be accomplished by the continual pounding or drilling right beside the present hole, were the casing removed from the hole.

4. The better would be to locate at the place as first given, [See 4905-1 and 4905-2] some fifty (50) yards from this present hole.

5. The financing of same would be a question, until the circumstances and conditions as have surrounded the present operations were fully and completely known and understood. Then there might be gathered sufficient funds, with the present acreage holdings and the tools as are on the site, to complete another well here - for, as has been seen, there are sufficient indications that there could be no question of the hole being made a PRODUCING territory, were the hole drilled.

6. These operations may be financed in St. Louis, Tulsa and Ardmore, and in [[New York City|New York]] and [[Chicago|Chicago]].

7. In Ardmore, such as are seen in Purdue.

8. In St. Louis, those that have already been interested would, within their own selves and those that are near to same, assist in such financing.

9. In [[Chicago|Chicago]], all those that have been interested in same would be in the re-financing for continued operations.

10. We would not give up the project for oil and gas will be produced in this territory at much less depth than was drilled in this hole, as has been thoroughly illustrated or demonstrated by that as has happened in these operations.

11. DO that.  Follow these out.  These are worth while.  We are through for the present.

> R7. 5/7/27 Mr. Wyrick's letter:
> (Morgan, Texas)
> TO ALL SHAREHOLDERS:-
> 
> I have the following report and recommendation to make to
> those interested with me in drilling for oil in Texas.
> 
> In our second well, we had drilled to a depth of 6105 ft.
> The last 161 ft. of this was made in what is known as the
> Pennsylvania Lime and is the formation that overlies the oil
> producing sands in the oil fields of North Central Texas in
> the vincinity of our holdings.  We experienced considerable
> difficulties by the cavings of a 45 ft. strata of soft shale
> that laid directly on top of the lime.  On December 12, 1925,
> we were so unfortunate as to lose the bailer that in falling
> stopped in the cave referred to and in a position cross-wise
> to the hole.  Since that date we have tried by every means
> known to the drilling profession to remove or get by this
> obstruction without success.
> 
> On March 31, 1927, we shot the well with a charge of
> nitroglycerin placed on the obstruction which we had driven
> down the hole in the lime to a point about 100 ft. from the
> bottom.  After the shot we got a considerable flow of gas and
> a spray of oil that was comparable to a high gravity
> gasoline.  This showing was choked off by bridging or closing
> of the hole above the shot but a few days later started
> again, when considerable water had entered the hole and the
> gas came out with such force that it raised the water in
> large quantities to the top of the derrick, which is 84 ft.
> above ground.  The water was well charged with oil.  By
> drilling operation since in attempting to open the hole to
> the cavity of the shot we have found that thru a mishap in
> shooting, 1400 ft. of the casing was broken off and so
> mangled that it is impossible to remove it or drill by it.
> 
> We now recognize the fact that it is impossible to make a
> paying producer of this hole and we are confronted with the
> question of what we will do next.  After consulting a great
> many of those interested in the project, I find that all whom
> I have talked to are of the same opinion, i.e., we should not
> give up our holdings and that we should arrange to drill
> another well as soon as practical.  Up to the time of the
> showings had after the shot in this well, we have placed our
> investments on the strength of opinions and recommendations
> of men of experience in oil production who recommended that
> we were in an oil producing area and should encounter
> production if we picked up the black lime and drilled thru
> it.  We found the lime in greater thickness than has been
> found in any other field in Texas and we now have material
> evidence (by the showing of gas and oil) that we have touched
> a producing strata.  It would seem then that the only
> conclusion that we can reach is to continue our effort,
> applying our energy and money until we successfully drill
> thru the lime.  When we succeed in this, if the production we
> encounter is anywhere near as great as our advisors
> anticipate, every $100 invested will mean a fortune to those
> who have had the nerve to take their chance and place their
> money in this project.
> 
> Before we can start operation on another well, we must have
> cash on hand and subscribe to insure the success of drilling
> another well.  The present well has cost a little more than
> $100,000.00, but a large percent of this has been spent
> during the last year and four months in our effort to clear
> the hole of the obstruction referred to above.  Now before we
> can make arrangements for drilling the next well I would like
> to hear from each and everyone interested and would like for
> you to consider this matter seriously and advise just as
> closely as you can estimate the amount of money you will be
> able to subscribe for each month's operation.  Our success
> depends entirely on a liberal favorable report from you.
> 
> Before we start drilling again we have some preliminary work
> to do.  We will rewrite all leases extending the terminating
> date so that we may have ample time to drill another well
> before leases expire.  The land owners are agreeable to give
> us their leases as long as we continue to operate.  We have
> made application to the State Authorities for permission to
> increase our capitalization so that we may put our finances
> in good form by issuing certificates for money paid in in
> excess of our present capitalization and provide enough to
> finance the drilling of another well.
> 
> Please read carefully the copies enclosed - letters from Mr.
> and Mrs. Claude Martin, Mr. Moran and Mr. Bateman.  The
> sincerity and honesty of their statements, I can vouch for.

> M. B. Wyrick,
