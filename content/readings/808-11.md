# TEXT OF READING 808-11 F 32

**Reading [[808-11]]** · Series: [[801-1500 — Psychological and Dream Interpretation Readings|801-1500]] · Year: 1939 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 10/27/1939  Sex: F  Age: 32  ReadingID: 4873

This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at his home on Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 27th day of October, 1939, in accordance with request made by the self - Mrs. [808], Active Member of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading 
11:20 to 11:30 A. M. Eastern Standard Time.         ..., Va.

1. GC:  You will give the physical condition of this body at the present time, with suggestions for further corrective measures; answering the questions she has submitted, as I ask them:

2. EC:  Yes, we have the body here; this we have had before.

3. As we find, while conditions have improved a great deal from those we have had at various times, in the present there continues to be more of the recurrent disturbances than there were when there was sufficient of the diathermy AND the treatments that were accorded during such periods.

4. These are the disturbances which arise, causing greater disorder in the Periods, or the menstrual flow, and the concurrent conditions as arise from the [[Glands (Esoteric)|glandular]] forces in the present.

5. As we find, there may be the retaking of the adjustments AND the low diathermy to heat the body, STIMULATING the better activity of the [[Glands (Esoteric)|glandular]] forces through the [[Glands (Esoteric)|thyroid]], especially, and the system as related to the functioning of the glands in reproduction as related to same, or those just above or associated with the kidney area.

6. This stimulation with the heat or diathermy may be done by the [[Osteopathy|osteopath]]; or it may be done by the use of small quantities of injections that will add the hormones IN the glandular secretion to aid these in functioning in their normal manner.

7. But if the injections are taken, follow the suggestions of the one administering same.

8. If the [[Osteopathy|osteopathic]] manipulations are taken, take the diathermy and the stimulation for those particular areas; with the diets, the rest and the other activities as we have indicated.

9. Ready for questions.

10. (Q)  Would adrenalin injections given by Dr. McCue build up my glands to normal?
(A)  If they are given properly, and the proper considerations given as to that which has been the depleting of same, in the form of the poisons that caused same.

Then, this would necessarily be followed very closely - in the history as well as the applications made for the adjustments of same.

This would be very well, if that method is chosen by the body.

11. (Q)  How often should they be given?
(A)  As has been indicated, it should be under the SUPERVISION of the one administering same, following the history as well as the general conditions!

12. We are through for the present.

Copy to Self - Special Delivery
"    " Ass'n file

> R4. Pamphlet on Eschatin printed by the Parke, Davis & Company
> to the Medical Profession:
> 
> ESCHATIN (SUPRARENAL CORTEX EXTRACT)
> Eschatin is a highly purified extract of the adrenal cortex,
> containing not more than a few tenths of 1 per cent of solid
> extractive matter.  It is substantially free from Adrenalin
> (less than one part in 200,000)
> 
> It is standardized after the manner described by Harrop,
> Pfiffner, Weinstein and Swingle (Proceedings of the Society
> for Experimental Biology and Medicine, 29:449, Jan., 1932),
> and contains not less than 25 dog units per cc.
> 
> THERAPEUTIC INDICATIONS
> 
> Eschatin is indicated in cases of insufficiency of the
> suprarenal cortex.  The only definite indication at present
> is Addison's disease, for which it appears to be valuable as
> a diagnostic as well as therapeutic agent.  Possibly the
> extract will prove in the end to be even more valuable in
> conditions of hypoadrenia, other than Addison's disease,
> in which most of the adrenal tissue has already been
> destroyed.
> 
> The therapeutic action of the adrenal cortex extract has been
> described by Rowntree, Greene, Swingle and Pfiffner (J. A. M.
> A., 96:231-235, 1931).  Patients in a state of collapse were
> given intravenously as much as 20 cc. daily, divided into two
> or three doses, the treatment being followed by remarkable
> general improvement.  Weight gain, increase in strength, and
> disappearance of anorexia ensued within a few days under
> treatment, but the effect upon blood pressure was less
> striking.
> 
> The spectacular results observed in several of these early
> cases are not to be expected in every instance, since the
> response, no doubt, is dependent upon the degree of
> suprarenal insufficiency.  Moreover, it becomes apparent, now
> that many cases have been treated, that large daily doses of
> extract will be required in patients approaching total
> deficiency.  In those having moderate involvement, however,
> treatment will sustain life and health for a considerable
> period of time, although naturally the hormone is not a cure
> and cannot check progressive hypoadrenia due to infection.
> 
> Both laboratory and clinical observations have shown that
> administration of sodium salts is a valuable adjuvant in the
> management of Addison's disease.  R. F. Loeb (Proc. Soc. Exp.
> Biol. & Med., 30:308, 1933) reviewed experimental studies of
> sodium chloride in adrenalectomized animals and reported the
> result of salt administration in the case of a woman with
> Addison's disease.  The beneficial effect in this case was
> striking.  George A. Harrop, et al. (J. A. M. A., 100:1850,
> 1933), in a study of four cases treated with high salt diet,
> showed that the regular administration of sodium chloride had
> a markedly beneficial effect.  This author also finds the
> daily administration of from 1 to 3 ounces of cottonseed oil
> of value.  In severe relapse salt may be administered by
> intravenous injection or by protoclysis.  Recent work has
> shown that an alkalinizing salt mixture containing either
> [[Baking Soda|sodium bicarbonate]] or sodium citrate is superior to the use
> of sodium chloride alone.  One suggestion is that sodium
> chloride and sodium citrate be used in a proportion of two
> parts of the former to one of the latter (Harrop, et al.,
> J. Exper. Med. 61:839, 1935; W. D. Allers, Proc. Staff Meet.
> Mayo Clin. 10:406, 1935).  When there is no need for
> emergency measures, adequate amounts of salt mixture may be
> given by means of a high salt diet together with
> administration of 5 to 10 Gm. daily in capsules.
> 
> DOSAGE
> 
> In the preliminary demonstrations of the efficacy of the
> cortical extract, intravenous treatment was used exclusively,
> but, although it may be necessary at times to administer
> Eschatin intravenously, the almost universal practice is to
> give it subcutaneously.  Patients in a state of collapse may
> be given daily as much as 20 cc. of Eschatin, preferably in
> divided doses, in addition to hydration and sodium chloride
> therapy.
> 
> If the condition of a patient with Addison's disease has not
> reached the stage of extreme exhaustion and collapse,
> probably much smaller doses of Eschatin will suffice.
> Clinical data indicate that doses of 1 to 5 cc.
> subcutaneously are effective.  This same dosage and route of
> administration every two or three days, or even once each
> week, may be all that is required for maintenance after the
> phase of severe relapse is past.
> 
> PACKAGE
> 
> Eschatin is marketed in 10-cc. vials, each cubic centimeter
> of extract representing at least 25 dog units.
