# TEXT OF READING 1797-3 M 40 (Stock & Bond Broker, Protestant)

**Reading [[1797-3]]** · Series: [[1501-2000 — Philosophy and Universal Law|1501-2000]] · Year: 1940 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 1/27/1940  Sex: M  Age: 40  ReadingID: 8109

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at his home on Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 27th day of January, 1940, in accordance with request made by the self - Mr. [1797], Associate Member of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno.]

Time of Reading 
10:45 to 11:20 A. M. Eastern Standard Time.   [[New York City|New York City]].

1. GC:  You will have before you the body and enquiring mind of [1797] at his home near ..., Mass.  In an effort to understand better the information he has previously received, and because he is anxious to learn just what [[Purpose|purpose]] he should fulfil in this material existence, and also because he wishes to lead a truly useful life employing his talents and abilities to their best advantage, [[Entity (Cayce Term)|this entity]] presents a list of questions, which you will answer, as I ask them:

2. EC:  Yes, we have the body, the enquiring mind, [1797]; the desires, the intents and [[Purpose|purposes]] of [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]]; the conditions which exist in the experience in the present; together with the information which has been indicated as to channels through which [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]] may better manifest, - thus fulfilling those [[Purpose|purposes]] for which the entity entered this present experience.

3. In answering, or giving expression to the questions, the desires and conditions, it might be well to give from here the premise from which all of these are to be judged, or analyzed; that, in determining the manner of activity, the entity might be more in line with choosing the best activity to pursue.

4. First, - the entity finds self a body, a mind, a [[Soul|soul]]; in a three-dimensional world.  In the physical or material we see, the more often, the manifestations of the mental and the material successes, failures, or confusions in the experience of individuals.  Life - as manifested in this three-dimensional plane - is a combination not only of the present experiences, the present problems, the present urges (as has been indicated for the entity), but of ALL the experiences; and the awareness of same is only manifested through that phase or portion of the mental self as it seeks to know its source of activity, - or the spiritual or [[Soul|soul]] self.

5. The spiritual or [[Soul|soul]] self is the eternal.  Hence the mental is both of material and of spiritual, or divine origin.

6. Hence, the individual who aided man in setting forth laws, rules or regulations - or who is known as the lawgiver - gave expression to that which, if it is wholly understood in the consciousness of an individual, puts before him all the problems and yet the answers to same, day by day.

7. Then, as he gave, it is not who will descend from heaven to bring you a message, or who will come from over the seas or from without to make you aware; but lo, the whole answer is within thy own consciousness.  For, as ye become aware of this, in thy relationships, there is the realization within self of that spiritual awareness which may enable self to DO that which is ever the constructive exercising of [[Free Will|the will]] in materiality; for:  Today there is set before thee good and evil, life and death; choose thou.

8. That factor of the spiritual self, or of the soul ([[Free Will|the will]]), then enables the individual to cooperate with that spirit, that truth, that fact which has ever been set before man, - "If ye will be my son, I will be thy God - If ye call, I will hear - Behold, I stand at the door and knock; I will enter, if ye ask."

9. Then, these are not mere sayings!  They are FACTS, truths, life itself!  but the individual is not made aware of same through the material things nor material-mindedness; rather through spiritual-mindedness, as to purposes and activities of the soul in its lessons, its tenets that it has carried through its expressions in the earth.

10. Then, in that which has been given through this channel as to the activities of this entity in the earth, there has been pointed out to the entity those urges which are innate, and in which the entity may find the greater harmony, greater fulfilling of purposes and desires as they are brought to material expression in its associations and activities with its fellow men.

11. These, as has been pointed out, are in association or affiliation with things or conditions having to do with the products of the earth, the soil, - or those that indicate their willingness, or activity, or desire, to be the producers of those things which take hold upon the storehouse of nature.

12. Not that these are the ONLY sources, nor the only means or manners.  For, in whatsoever field of service the entity chooses to give expressions of its abilities, these same principles as indicated may be applied.  But, those activities IN SUCH FIELDS AS INDICATED would find the least resistances, - once the entity has found or established self in those directions.

13. Ready for questions.

14. (Q)  For what purpose, or for what particular work, did I enter this present material existence?
(A)  This has just been answered in that indicated.  Not that there is already a line set out.  There IS the line in which there will be found least resistance, once established upon that line; and that is in association or connection with things or conditions dealing with the products of the soil.

As to whether this is to be the establishing of farms, or through government associations, or in other directions, is to be the [[Free Will|choice]] of self.

For, as the entity has seen (and with a little analysis and study of same it will be seen the more), there has gradually come the awakening within the consciousness of those in charge of the rules, the regulations or problems of man's existence in the earth, that there must be more and more a return of man to the closer relations or associations with the products which supply life and sustenance to man in all walks of life.

Then, whether the exercising of such associations is to be in conjunction with the government, or with organizations or groups that would enable individuals to finance themselves in such a proposition or such outlets, is to be a choice of self.  But it is in this field, in this direction, that greater harmony, a greater feeling of complying with that which is creative in the lives of those with whom the entity may come in contact day by day, may be experienced by the entity.

15. (Q)  In what particular department or division of this work should I devote my efforts to find my greatest usefulness?
(A)  As just indicated, in that of aiding or assisting others; or it may be in ANY field as would pertain to the handling and the distribution of such products.

16. (Q)  I am considering the possibility of joining with others, in the business of advising corporations on financial needs, arranging for reorganization when necessary and advising on other corporate matters.  Would this work offer a truly useful life for me, and would I be successful in helping corporations on such matters?
(A)  This work offers a truly useful life, but - as has just been indicated - it is not as close in keeping with that which offers the greater opportunities and abilities of the entity, as would be that in connection with the soil, or the development, or organizations or activities that would deal with PRODUCTS of the soil.

17. (Q)  I have been considering the possibility of buying farms, rehabilitating them, putting them in production on a small scale and then selling them to those interested in such properties.  Is this a work which I should undertake, and would it be a work in which I would be truly useful?
(A)  This is a VERY good work; yet, as may be indicated and surmised, it would require a great deal of capital, a great deal of knowledge of the products of the various vicinities or communities, and the needs, and the distribution of such products.  However, it is very close to those forms of activity as we have suggested; and if the choice is made in these directions for the activities, these may prove to be most successful operations.  But it will necessitate a great deal of study upon the productions of same in the various vicinities, communities, and throughout the land.  And it should NOT be an organization or activity in one local vicinity, but should be so varied that in the various communities there should be the production of that FROM the soil in each vicinity to make for the constant flow of same not only to the markets but to outlets that may be established with such an organization for the continuous use or distribution of such products, in a most helpful manner to a great many individuals.

18. (Q)  I have considered two lines of endeavor which may have been indicated from information previously received, that is: (a) the work of an investment counselor or (b) the work of a farm cooperative.  Are either of these activities those in which I may be most useful, and if so in which may I use my abilities to the best advantage for others?
(A)  In either of these the abilities may be used for aiding others.  Remember the premise from which we have been reasoning, or from which judgement or analysis may be drawn. As we find, and as indicated, that which will have more to do with the products of the soil will eventually prove the MOST congenial, the easier, and with less disturbing factors. However, as an investment counselor, if considered in the FIELDS as we have indicated, the abilities may be used VERY well.  PREFERABLY, we would advise that which would have to do with products of the soil.  As indicated, this may be done by cooperative manners, or by the study of that which the government has done and is undertaking in the re-settlement, the re-establishing, the rehabilitating of many of just such products throughout the land.  Such studies may be used as a means for gaining the knowledge necessary, or this may be used as a channel through which the studies of such might enable the forming later of individual or private corporations to do just the same thing!  But as outlined, as indicated, there should be those that would cover the vegetable market, or those vegetable products of either southwest [[Texas|Texas]], or [[Florida|Florida]], the peach growers of Georgia, the melons of Georgia, and such conditions or products peculiar to Louisiana, the Carolinas, also other portions of the land, where there is still a continuous flow of all such products to a central market that might be indicated or established by just such corporations.

See, it would be - first - the understanding of how it might be accomplished - that is, the lands, the workers upon same - a cooperative organization; and the supplying, then centering in [[New York City|New York]], Philadelphia or the like, to which ALL such would be sent for a central distribution of same, see?  That is, the cooperative force throughout.  It is to meet the needs not only of all classes or masses, but it is the manner in which the government and individual or private corporations or activities may be engaged for a successful operation.

And don't forget, - you may sow the seed; God alone gives the increase.  You may sow the seed in thy mental association, - God alone must give the increase.  You may sow the seed in an organization for cooperation in distribution, - God alone may keep these in the way.  Take God with you, then, into all thy activities!

19. (Q)  To whom should I go for advice and help in starting, or in preparing myself to start, this work?
(A)  As has been indicated, the preparation for same may best be had by the study of, or association with, government projects that have been undertaken, are being undertaken, are IN OPERATION throughout the land!  These exist in [[Texas|Texas]], Arkansas, Mississippi, Louisiana, and especially in the South where there has been so little of the educating of the groups that they have become a part of the large landowners.

It isn't that the entity should own land and then bleed the people, as many a community or county - or even state - has!

20. (Q)  Should my wife and I continue to operate our farm on a subsistence basis only, or should we endeavor to produce certain products for profit?
(A)  This may be taken as an example.  But, as indicated, there are certain products here, in this vicinity, that may be used, or that may supply the needs of certain demands.

For, in the consideration of the whole, it will be found that products grown in individual localities are best suited to the needs of individuals living or dwelling within those same localities.

Not that there are not to be supplies that are interchangeable, - but this may be used as an EXAMPLE of just that as we have outlined for the entity.

21. (Q)  What products, or animals, should we specialize in raising on our farm?
(A)  As indicated, make a study of these, - through information that may be gained from the government, or through association with the government that has made a study of just what is especially adaptable for growth in certain communities, certain kinds of land, certain animals, and certain other things.  All of these would be studies that would be as a part of the training for the greater work.

22. (Q)  In what way might we best use our farm and its products for the use of others?
(A)  As just indicated.

23. (Q)  Please give such advice as will help me to find the work thru which I may fulfill most completely the purpose for which I entered, so that I may lead a truly useful life.
(A)  If the abilities are analyzed from that first premise from which we have been reasoning, it will be seen that in any field of service the entity may lead a useful life; but if there is the analyzing of self, self's desires, self's purposes, self's abilities, it will be seen that the field as indicated offers the greater opportunities, and not just that which is appealing for the moment.

Then, study thyself, - and it will be seen that the line has been laid out.

24. (Q)  Is there any particular department or division of this work in which I might find my greatest usefulness?
(A)  This has ALL been indicated here.

25. We are through for the present.

> R25. 7/44 Thomas Sugrue's letter to EC, used in 1944 A.R.E.
> Bulletin:
> 
> Dear Boss [EC]:-
> 
> It is a week today since we sat with the radio turned on
> all through the morning and afternoon and night, hearing the
> first news of the invasion.  Between the brief, tantalizing
> bulletins, that said so little and suggested so much, we
> prayed that not too many would have to be killed, not too
> many maimed, not too many blinded, shocked, disfigured;
> for they were dying for our sins - to save a liberty we
> imperilled by denying it to others; they were dying to save
> a world that had very nearly ceased to be Christian.
> 
> We felt very strongly what the invasion means to us:  Theirs
> is the sacrifice, and theirs will be the [[Resurrection|resurrection]].  Ours
> is the responsibility to spread and maintain and defend the
> faith they watered with their blood.  This, we must say to
> ourselves as we make the peace, is their body; this is their
> blood.
> 
> We had not much to give them in the way of faith when they
> left.  Our temples were bright and shining, but most of them
> were empty.  This was told me by the boys themselves.  They
> went there and found them so.  They said to me, "What shall
> we believe?"  I said, "Believe in God.  He did not ruin the
> church.  Believe in yourself.  God made you.  You are a
> participant in His creation.  Reach out with your heart and
> your spirit to take your heritage.  Your mind will follow,
> no matter what obstacles are put in its path."
> 
> On last Tuesday night at 11:15 I lay in bed listening to
> a broadcast from the invasion coast - one of those records
> made by the on-the-spot reporters.  This fellow was Charles
> Collingwood, of Columbia Broadcasting System.  It was a hot
> night; the full moon was high and [[Virgin Mary|Mary]] was sitting on the
> porch outside the window, also listening.  Collingwood said
> he was soaked through, that their landing craft was loaded
> with TNT, and that he was frankly nervous.  The sound of
> firing was audible.  He said, "I see a Naval captain stopping
> to look curiously at my rig.  Perhaps I can get him to say
> a few words.  Will you come over, Captain?"  Then I heard
> a voice - a voice very familiar to me - say, "I'm not a
> captain.  I'm just a lieutenant, but after you've been on
> this beach a little while you look like almost anything."
> 
> "It's Jerry!  Jerry Danzig!"  I said to [[Virgin Mary|Mary]].  We listened
> closely.  There could be no mistake - the phrasing, the
> [[Free Will|choice]] of words, the laugh, all were Jerry's.  But he sounded
> so calm, so matter-of-fact, that I could hardly believe
> he was not standing beside me, instead of supervising the
> unloading of a batch of dynamite on the invasion beach of
> Normandy, with shells dropping all around him.  Briefly,
> expertly, he told how the boys were doing.  Then the
> broadcaster said, "Will you tell us your name, Lieutenant?"
> 
> "No," Jerry said.  "I work for a rival network in New York.
> I don't want to spoil your broadcast."  That was Jerry -
> even under fire he was the complete gentleman.
> 
> What I want to tell you and the congress about Jerry is this:
> As you know, Jerry was publicity director for the Mutual
> Broadcasting System when war came - he is an old and dear
> friend of mine and one of our earliest Life Reading babies,
> being, in addition, one of our [[Egypt|Egyptians]].  He sought a
> commission in the Navy, hoping for action but pretty well
> convinced he would be put into some kind of work resembling
> that which he had done in civilian life.  He got his wish,
> however, and was made second in command of an LST, one of
> those strange craft that go right up to the beach and
> discharge smaller ships, men, and tanks, taking back the
> dead and wounded.  He took his ship to Sicily for the
> invasion, then to Salerno, then to Anzio.  When last he
> wrote to me he was himself in command.  He said in the
> letter:
> 
> "During the past year I have taken to good, solid [[Prayer|prayer]].
> So has every man on my ship.  Years ago you told me that
> a spiritual revival was inevitable.  I was not very much
> interested then.  I am now.  Is this it?"
> 
> Over the invasion coast the bombers flew.  In one of them two
> boys named Jim and Joe crouched at their guns - one in the
> top turret, one in the waist of the ship.  They had come to
> see me before their crew left Drew Field in [[Florida|Tampa]], to fly
> overseas.  They were nervous, Jim particularly so.  Both
> had been in [[England|England]] and completed the allotted twenty-five
> missions.  They had returned to this country with the
> understanding that their combat days were over - they would
> be used to instruct.  But there was a shortage of men; after
> a furlough and a stay at a rehabilitation center they were
> assigned to a new crew - a green crew - and a new ship.
> Their nervousness came from their feeling that they had run
> out of their luck.  Joe's bomber was the only one left after
> twenty-five missions over [[Germany|Germany]], the lone survivor of its
> squadron.  Could he hope for the same good fortune again?
> Jim was even more depressed.  He was one of five brothers,
> all in the service.  Two had been killed; one had returned
> home, blind.  He believed he had survived twenty-five
> missions because of the skill of his pilot; now he would
> have a new one, a green one.
> 
> Joe was from my home town.  I knew him and his family.  He
> began to question me, to draw me out.  Suddenly I realized
> what he was after.  He knew I studied psychic phenomena,
> religion, philosophy.  He wanted me to talk about these
> things, particularly death.  I began to speak, casually.
> 
> "Well," I said, "You can't lose.  You're holding a royal
> flush.  If you get back, the world is yours, and you will
> have memories to make the rest of your life rich.  If you
> don't come back, you go over to the other side with money in
> the bank.  Few of us get a chance to die for a good cause -
> with most of us it is a selfish lingering, and if we leave
> behind anything but our insurance we are lucky.
> 
> "The only important thing is the pattern.  The body is a mess
> of atoms grouped around the pattern, the way iron filings
> group around a magnet.  Take the magnet away and they are
> free to do as they please again.  Your plane isn't much good
> without its crew.  The crew is the pattern.
> 
> "Once a pattern is free, it can operate in other places -
> look at the sky when you're on a night run.  Every star is
> a sun.  There are more worlds than there are people on this
> earth.  On some of them things will be easier.  We may not
> have to eat, or dig ditches.  Our bodies may be lighter.
> Instead of taste, smell, hearing, touch, and sight, our
> five senses may be love, kindness, humility, appreciation of
> beauty, and longing.  If so, there would be things for them
> to exercise upon, as there are things here for us to exercise
> our eyes and fingers and ears and nose on.  Wouldn't that be
> wonderful - to taste love the way you taste a steak, to smell
> beauty the way you smell a flower, to touch humility the way
> you stroke a kitten, to hear longing the way you hear the
> wind, or music?
> 
> "Those worlds are in all stages of evolution.  They need
> strong, brave [[Soul|souls]], to do the things necessary for their
> unfoldment.  You boys would qualify - a world may need a
> [[Ohio|Columbus]], to discover new continents, an Edison to discover
> electricity, a Washington to free a nation, a Wright to
> discover the airplane.  You might get the assignment.  You
> would deserve it."
> 
> I talked on in that vein, watching their reactions.  Slowly
> their imaginations took fire.  They were unmarried, young,
> adventurous - I painted a [[Photography (Atlantean)|picture]] of the cosmos which sounded
> as attractive as the Wild West was to me in my boyhood.  They
> began to make comments, to ask questions, to joke about what
> they would be and where they would go, after death.
> 
> "This place," I said, "is just a mudball.  I'll be glad to
> leave when my work is done.  But I want to be sure the work
> is done, and done well, before I go, otherwise I'll be
> demoted instead of promoted.  That's where you fellows have a
> sure bet.  You take the sins of the world on your shoulders
> when you fly into battle."
> 
> When they were ready to leave Jim shook my hand and said,
> "Thanks for what you told us.  I'm going to be all right now.
> I'm going back there and do just what they tell me to do.
> I'll be all right."
> 
> I was puzzled at these remarks until [[Virgin Mary|Mary]] told me - after
> they were gone - that Joe had told her Jim was so depressed
> he was ready to quit, to accept a dishonorable discharge and
> go home.  A few days later they left for [[England|England]].
> 
> When they come back?  That is our problem; it is what every
> one of us should ponder.  They will have saved us.  Will we
> be worth it?  Will the temples still be empty?  If not, then
> we should be busy filling them now, with love, beauty, faith,
> and the light of a new and expanded hope.  Only this, a new
> Christianity, will answer the soldier who comes back to our
> land and, looking at it, says to himself, "This is my body,
> this is my blood."
> 
> Our soldiers now are facing death, with clear eyes, strong
> hearts, and serene spirits.  Can we face them in the same
> high way when they come back?  If we have been about Our
> Father's business we can.
> 
> My love and best wishes to you for the congress, where those
> who gather will, I am sure, be seeking to do His will.
> 
> [signed] Tom

> Association and to the members.
> 
> "WHAT WILL YOU DO WITH THIS MAN?"
> 
> England, May 24, 1944
> 
> May I add my welcome and greetings to you who are attending
> the meetings of the Thirteenth Annual Congress of the
> Association for Research and Enlightenment, Incorporated!
> 
> For several days, I have been trying to select a subject for
> this paper; something more than hello and good-bye, but
> obviously not a treatise on anything demanding technical
> study and research.  The army routine hardly permits time
> for that; certainly not just now.  The result has been
> considerable thinking about a subject which has as many
> sides as there are individuals involved:  namely, WHAT IS
> THE [[Purpose|PURPOSE]] OF THE ASSOCIATION?
> 
> Frequently, my father receives letters containing a tirade
> over the necessity of joining a society in order to secure
> a reading.  For many individuals the blank is only a form,
> something to be filled out in order to secure the reading
> in question.  The signing of membership forms constitutes
> the [[Alpha and Omega|first and last]] act for too many as members.
> 
> Part of the indifference has been due to confusion on the
> part of those who constitute the Association's policy forming
> group and the members of the office staff.  Much of the
> confusion is due to a wide variety of concepts held by
> important members.  All of the indifference and confusion
> are not without basis and can be traced to some consistent,
> but hard-to-interpret, statements in readings given over a
> period of many years.
> 
> There are two horns to the dilemma.  On one hand there is the
> constant warning that the Association must not become a cult
> or ism with any formal system of beliefs, and on the other
> there is need for concerted study and unity in presenting
> "the work."  This is neatly phrased by the readings "in
> decency and order."  Since I do not have the readings in
> front of me, I cannot go into this in any exhaustive manner.
> Everything from details of membership fees to broad
> principles are contained in readings on the Association.
> 
> A careful study of all these past readings would be in order
> in preparing any expansion of the membership activities.
> 
> In the development of the Association program the privileges
> and work of members form the backbone of the organization.
> Stated simply, the purpose of the Association is to serve its
> members.
> 
> For example, an individual seeking a reading becomes a member
> of the Association, for only members of the Association can
> receive readings.  This reading is a privilege, a service
> rendered to the member.  For it he pays a membership fee,
> either as an Active or Associate member.  Any function of the
> organization can be interpreted in terms of service to the
> members, and reversely, only through members can the work of
> the organization be carried on.  There is no need to follow
> this further.  I wanted to establish a simple premise from
> which we can examine certain general principles.
> 
> Each person will come seeking something different.  You know
> what these are, for you were motivated by these desires:
> some come seeking help for a suffering [[Physical Body|physical body]]; some
> want help for a loved one; others have a problem in human
> relationships or a decision which must be made regarding
> their work; a few are interested in the possibilities of
> scientific research, in studying the laws which govern the
> unusual manifestations of the readings; many are attracted by
> the strangeness of the whole matter and come out of
> curiosity, fluttering about for a time and then either they
> settle down or drift off; a growing number find new
> spiritual stimuli.  These are common interest, easily
> recognized by all of you.
> 
> The Association program must be broad and free if it is to
> serve all of these needs and interests.
> 
> Yet, we must pause and consider still more basic aspects of
> the principles of service involved.  All of the above
> attractions of the work are only means to an end.  In
> serving its members the Association is committed to
> stimulation of the spiritual nature of man.
> 
> This does not mean that the organization has any set formula
> or any system of beliefs.  It is freedom from all these which
> gives the Association its greatest opportunity.
> 
> Let us state this a bit differently.  The readings point out
> that each individual is part of a great system of spiritual
> evolution.  Every physical reading, every life reading, every
> mental and spiritual reading, is concerned with giving man a
> better understanding of his relationship with God and with
> his fellow man.  Every department of the Association, every
> phase of its program, must recognize this principle and lead
> to this end.
> 
> There are expressions describing methods which are essential:
> freedom of thought and expression; simplicity and clarity;
> cooperation; honesty.
> 
> The time has come when we must cease being vague about
> membership in the Association.  It is time for those
> interested in the work to stop saying that the Association
> is an organization which permits Edgar Cayce to give psychic
> readings without [[Law of Moses|the law]] attacking him.  Edgar Cayce does not
> need the Association.  This is a strange statement, you say.
> Isn't it for Edgar Cayce that we are working as members
> of the Association?  Certainly not!  A great many of our
> members, some of them prominent in its affairs, have for a
> long time been assuming that they are rendering a personal
> service.  There is no doubt that some are drawn to this point
> of view because of past relationships which bind them
> karmicly (if I may be permitted to build a word).
> 
> In becoming connected with the Association Edgar Cayce has
> limited himself and his work to the intelligence and ability
> of those who make up the membership of the organization.
> 
> In thinking through this matter let us examine some general
> statements which will help us think clearly about membership
> in the Association.  Without the readings in front of me, I
> may not be able to quote exact passages but the ideas will
> not be far from the information given through the years in
> the readings.
> 
> 1. There will be two classes of members; Associate and
> Active.
> 
> 2. Associate members hold a membership for one year and
> during that time may apply for readings.  They receive the
> monthly Bulletin.  Through the Bulletin and such letters or
> circulars as may be prepared for the purpose, the Associate
> members should be informed of the various activities of the
> organization and given the opportunity to take a more active
> part as an Active member.
> 
> 3. Active members SHOULD BE the real Association.  The most
> important of their functions is one which is ignored (as it
> is in civic life;) namely, the selection by vote of the Board
> of Trustees of the Association.  This Board should consist
> of Active members who have taken a prominent part in the
> Association activities in their respective communities.  This
> answers the problem of Active members voting on people whom
> they do not know.  The work of the Active member is limited
> only by his initiative, for every effort has been made not
> to limit the direction or extent of an individual's study or
> participation in any phase of work.
> 
> 4. This brings us to the Board of Trustees, Active members
> who have shown a real interest in the work in the eyes of
> other Active members in their community.  After this vote
> is taken each name is presented to a reading and thus checked
> as a future Board member.  The existing Board of Trustees is
> bound by the Association charter to accept this final list
> and so elects them yearly to serve for three years; thus
> one-third of the Board is retired each year.
> 
> It is this Board that selects the officers of the Association
> including President, Vice-President, Secretary, Manager,
> Treasurer and delegates the power of the selection of the
> office staff.  It is this Board that controls the policies
> of the organization, plans its major operations and works
> out the program.
> 
> Briefly, what are some of the services now being rendered the
> Active members:
> 
> A. The opportunity to secure readings through Edgar Cayce.
> The assurance that these readings will be recorded accurately
> and [[Records (Atlantean)|the records]] preserved carefully.  Members have a right
> to expect prompt handling of all their affairs pertaining
> to these readings, and our organization should endeavor
> to maintain a schedule which will make this possible.
> 
> B. Active members receive free all publications of the
> Association, including mimeographed and printed booklets.
> They have a right to expect a publication program which will
> cover all phases of the work.
> 
> C. The Active members have free access to the Association
> library and extracted records of the organization.  (No one
> but the Secretary and one working on the extracting have
> access to the personal records, which are filed under a
> classification number.)
> 
> D. Active members are supplied with leaflets for
> distribution, are encouraged to organize study groups and,
> if they have time, act as Regional Representatives who
> assist in the interpretation of readings, prepare questions
> and follow up important case studies.
> 
> Look ahead and visualize some of the services which can be
> rendered Active members:  I will only list them now, perhaps
> you will help us plan and fulfill them.
> 
> 1. A monthly printed magazine giving a far more comprehensive
> [[Photography (Atlantean)|picture]] of the work than can be achieved through our
> Bulletin.
> 
> 2. A radio program which will reach many more thousands of
> people with the information and inspirational material.
> 
> 3. A lecture program that will bring outstanding members of
> the Association to their communities - frequently.
> 
> 4. A well organized study group program.
> 
> 5. An expansion of the printed booklets to include every
> phase of the work, prepared by authorities on the various
> subjects.
> 
> 6. Use of one of the finest libraries on psychical research
> in the world.  (A mail exchange should be worked out.)  We
> have only scratched the surface on this library program.
> 
> 7. A research program which will provide helpful and valuable
> information direct to the members based on the finest
> possible experimentation handled by the best men who can be
> secured.
> 
> 8. A series of books which will amplify THERE IS A RIVER.
> 
> 9. Adequate provision for lecture, study and library space
> near [[Records (Atlantean)|the records]], and near Edgar Cayce.
> 
> 10. A complete index system on the thousands of readings in
> the files which will make all the information available by
> subject.  This will be supplemented by an extracting system
> which will provide hundreds of looseleaf books in the library
> containing data under a variety of subject headings.  This
> extracting system should be extended so as to make it
> possible for extracts from readings to be furnished by mail
> to members interested in special subjects.
> 
> Let me return now to the first premise.  The purpose of the
> Association is to serve its members.  All activities must
> be pointed toward an ultimate stimulation of the spiritual
> consciousness, leading to a better understanding of the
> relationship between man and his God and man and his fellow
> men.
> 
> There are those of you who will say that I have not stressed
> enough the importance of Edgar Cayce, the importance of
> working with him, for him.
> 
> To those of you who think this:  The life of Edgar Cayce has
> been dedicated to the service of his fellow men.  There are
> few of us who will ever (in this life) be permitted to serve
> as he serves.  In building a structure which will give all
> men the full benefits of his work we are serving in a way
> which cannot be measured in time or space.  It is not his
> work, it is our work!  People like you and I, and thousands
> of others who have come to know him, have an opportunity, a
> chance, which will not come again, perhaps, for hundreds of
> years.  He has no problem, no question.  In every reading
> his life is fulfilled and God is glorified.  Ours is the
> question, "WHAT WILL YOU DO WITH THIS MAN?"
> 
> 294-202.  "God seeks all to be one with Him.  And as all
> things were made by Him, that which is the creative influence
> in every herb, mineral, vegetable, or individual activity,
> is that same force ye call God, seeking expression!  Even as
> when God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light.
> for, this is law; this is love.
> 
> "Hence those who seek in sincerity, in hope, in purpose, to
> know, receive only to the measure that they manifest their
> hope, their belief, their desire in a God-purpose through a
> promise made to a man!
> 
> "Thus ye have the source.  Thus ye have the manner.  The
> seeker answers.  For, know ye all:  Ye give an account unto
> God for every deed, for every idle word, for every purposeful
> hope you have made manifest.  Then, to all:  Let not thy
> heart be troubled.  Let it not be afraid.  For ye believe in
> God.  Then, as His children, for thy sake, for thy Lord's
> sake, act like it!"
> 
> THE BUILDING FUND
> 
> Those who have attended the last two meetings of the Congress
> have observed how crowded has been the library, which we have
> had to use for a lecture room.  Many have not been able to
> hear either lectures or readings because they found neither
> seats nor standing room for themselves.
> 
> The Board, recognizing this existing condition and realizing
> that as the years pass the attendance at the Congress will be
> larger and larger, began to plan for an auditorium, a larger
> library, more offices and, if funds are adequate, bedroom and
> other facilities that will meet the needs of the members of
> the Association, who will attend the Congress in the future.
> 
> The following are extracts from a recent reading to the
> members of the Association, calling attention to this
> important step in the progress of the work.
>
