# TEXT OF READING 4904-2

**Reading [[4904-2]]** · Series: 3001-5747 · Year: 1931 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 1/26/1931  Sex: M  ReadingID: 12924

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at his office, 115 West 35th Street, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 26th day of January, 1931, in accordance with request made by Mr. [[King David|David]] E. [[David Kahn|Kahn]].

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno. J. F. Drummond, W. W. Weaver, [[King David|David]] E. [[David Kahn|Kahn]], L. B. Cayce and Linden Shroyer.]

Time of Reading 
4:40 P. M. Eastern Standard Time.

1. GC:  You will have before you the bodies and the enquiring minds of [[King David|David]] E. [[David Kahn|Kahn]], J. F. Drummond and W. W. Weaver, present in this room, in relation to the Automatic Vending Insurance Machine on which information has been given through these sources.  You will also have before you David E. Kahn's associations with Harold Leopold of [[Chicago|Chicago]], Kirk Sullivan of Mills Novelty, and various enterprises.  You will answer the questions which will be asked you concerning all of these.

2. EC:  We have the bodies, the enquiring minds, and those associations, relations and conditions as respecting same.  Ready for questions.

3. (Q)  Has Kahn approached the best insurance group in [[Chicago|Chicago]] through Leopold?
(A)  Has approached a good group.  These may be combined and be made better.

4. (Q)  What would be the better companies?
(A)  Combination of two or three, or more, of those that have been suggested, or those affiliated companies with the Continental.

5. (Q)  What is the next move for Kahn to complete the negotiations?
(A)  After the completion of the machine, then gather together and complete the negotiations.

6. (Q)  What should contract contain with insurance company for protection to inventors and Kahn group and associations?
(A)  That should be determined by the various groups as to what shall be the FUNCTIONING of the various groups, as to whether into a holdings company for patents or such, as for the distribution of machines and such, or as of the insurance and such, or other affiliated ideas as associated with same, or as to ONE company and a holdings company, and a combination.  These should be determined among the group of those first interested.

7. (Q)  How many machines for first building?
(A)  That will depend upon the interest shown by the insurance distributors and their affiliated or associated companies.

8. (Q)  For what prices should policies sell?
(A)  Ten, twenty-five and fifty.

9. (Q)  What added inducements should we offer with each policy?
(A)  The policy should be the main feature.  The protection, the easy access for protection - that should be the main feature.  Easy access to protection for the loved ones. Very good slogan!

10. (Q)  What company should own the patents and give the agreement under which patents should be held?
(A)  Those patenting same!

11. (Q)  Should we ask the patent office now for any additional protection for future aid?
(A)  Not yet.  It's not necessary as yet.  This will only be necessary when there is the expansion of the patents in affiliated, or associated, or foreign countries, or added conditions to same as necessary through the various changes that may be found necessary in the putting together or the working out of the plans.

12. (Q)  Please warn our legal representatives how to write an interlocking agreement that will be protection to the holding company?
(A)  This may be made very easy, for this should be as a separate and distinct interlocking by the directorship, rather than by other forces.  Then, necessarily, it comes to the confidence to be placed in the individuals, and this is ever the case.  If you haven't confidence in individuals, don't associate yourself!

13. (Q)  Advise Kahn how to handle these details with greatest speed and to the satisfaction of the forces and associations.
(A)  These should be carried together as the various things, and let each outline that as their policy, that as their idea.  Then put them DOWN!  Not just outline them in speech! Then combine these, and then work them out on the legal phases.  Do that.  We are through for the present.

Copy to David E. Kahn
"    " Messrs. Weaver & Drummond Two copies to Ass'n office (Edgar Cayce's file)

> In addition to what was said yesterday, we have a few things

> 
> We understand that the Mills Novelty Company are engaged to
> make a model of the machine.  We think it good engineering
> practise to have this model inspected by and turned out under
> the supervision of our engineer, as it is essential to have
> good workmanship, and the understanding of the designer is
> necessary in case of refinements.  To illustrate:  there are
> several springs which have been figured for us by the Barnes
> Spring Company to perform certain functions and give correct
> pressures as the weight of the cards lessens and the platform
> rises.  There are other coordinating units that must be right
> and properly assembled.
> 
> Of course we are aware that the Mills Novelty Company has
> been building vending machines for years, yet a botch [[Book of Job|job]]
> is always possible, and would surely get us off on the wrong
> foot, which is a risk none of us would care to take.  As your
> own experiences as a manufacturer probably tells you, factory
> men are always liable to make mistakes.
> 
> An additional use for the machine which can be taken up
> later is that a deal be made with some of the big industrial
> insurance companies who sell small policies to the laboring
> classes.  These companies maintain enormous forces of "leg
> men" to sell and collect for policies at five or ten cents a
> week.  Machines could be placed in contiguous grocery or drug
> stores to do most of the collecting and the men released to
> sell new business and pursue the delinquents.  We could work
> on a commission basis or sell or lease the machines by the
> year.
> 
> We understand the Metropolitan has about thirty of these men
> in [[Norfolk Virginia|Norfolk]] alone.  Each has what is known as a "debit", or
> group of several hundred he has to visit each week collecting
> a few nickels from each.  In larger centers they employ
> vastly more men.
> 
> Elbert Hubbard said "It is not what we know, but what we need
> to be reminded of, that does us good, and in that spirit,
> we are enclosing our original forecast of the possibilities
> of the machines as made up when we first began work on it.
> There may be some points in this that would be helpful to
> you.
> 
> Another suggestion comes to us at this time:  If a machine
> is located in the New Yorker, a line on the bottom or face of
> the card might read "I am stopping at the NEW YORKER."  If in
> a Standard Oil Filling Station:  "I am using STANDARD gas and
> oil."  This could be carried out indefinitely, perhaps at a
> profit, or a saving in percentage pain to the owner of the
> location.
> 
> To repeat the suggestions made yesterday:
> 
> (I) Prizes may be offered to school children for the best
> composition on the benefits we offer.  The prizes should
> be big enough to induce every school superintendent in the
> country to announce it in his school.  A college education
> to the best and lesser prizes in each locality.
> 
> (II) A key letter may be printed on each card, each
> different, but when put together to spell the work "POLICY",
> and a prize awarded for the series.  In this plan, only a
> certain number of one of the letters is issued.  This would
> encourage buyers to collect cards.
> 
> (III) A specially colored card or two might be placed in each
> machine.  The person buying the card would get his insurance
> coverage, and in addition be paid $10.00 for the card by
> the Home Office.  A man winning a card would surely tell
> the world about it.
> 
> Now to take up a rather disagreeable subject; which we have
> already explained to Mr. Cayce:
> 
> You told us not to hold back anything, but to put all of
> our cards on the table; therefore we wish to explain that in
> developing this proposition we have spent what to us at this
> time is rather a large sum of money, as most of our resources
> are tied up in non-producing real estate.  Our financial
> position is therefore rather strained, and as we have
> obligations which must be met, it will be necessary for us to
> have a few thousand dollars so that our minds may be clear to
> be on the [[Book of Job|job]] to help put this proposition over.
>

> one cent, and as a matter of fact you have already spent
> considerable in traveling etc., which should be refunded
> to you promptly.
> 
> No doubt you can arrange it so that a comparatively small
> amount can be paid to us out of the first available funds
> to clear up this situation.
> 
> We will then be ready to go to [[New York City|New York]], [[Chicago|Chicago]] or elsewhere
> and work with you in every way.
> 
> An expression from you will be appreciated.
>

>
