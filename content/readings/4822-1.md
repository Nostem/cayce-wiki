# TEXT OF READING 4822-1 M 22

**Reading [[4822-1]]** · Series: 3001-5747 · Year: 1944 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 3/16/1944  Sex: M  Age: 22  ReadingID: 12811

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at the office of [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]], Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 16th day of March, 1944, in accordance with request made by his Aunt (?) [...].

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Steno. (Notes read to and transcribed by Jeanette Fitch.)]

Time of Reading              Ward M-5, Base Hospital, 
Unknown.                     Daniel Field, Augusta, Georgia.

1. EC:  We haven't the body here.  [See 4822-1, Par. R1.]

> 
> We were unable to get the reading for [4822] on March 16th.

> 
> Due to the fact that it will now be several months before
> another appointment can be made we are very sure that you
> would prefer having your membership fee returned.  We are
> enclosing herewith check for twenty dollars.
> 
> Regretting that we were not able to get this for you, I am
> 
> Sincerely, Edgar Cayce
> 
> EC/jf

## Background

> [!note]- Background
> B1. 3/?/44 Aunt's (?) note verifying appointment which had been
> made on 1/24/44:
> 
> This boy, since childhood, has been erratic and unmanageable;
> would not take an education nor stick to a [[Book of Job|job]].
> 
> He did not want to enter military service, but did volunteer.
> However, he has not submitted to authority and, I think,
> attempted to desert.  He claims that he lost his memory and
> does not know what happened to him during the two months
> before he was found.  The authorities seem to believe him and
> have him in the mental ward.  I do not know what they intend
> to do to him, nor does he.
> 
> He is beginning to think there is something wrong with his
> mind.  He has been in several bad car wrecks and was very ill
> when very young.  Several years ago I persuaded him to see
> Dr. Tucker in Richmond, who said he could find nothing wrong
> with the boy.  However, I cannot believe he is simply
> willfully bad, as he has a nice personality, manners, and
> [[Ideal|ideals]]; nor has there ever been anything of this kind in the
> family.
> 
> He is very much worried about himself and wants to do better.
> I think he would like to be reinstated and make good before
> it is too late.
> 
> I shall cooperate with you in every way possible, but do not
> think it wise to ask him to do so.  If his address is
> changed, I will notify you.  The hour and time suit me
> perfectly.  Of course, not having seen the place, I cannot
> describe it.  I hope you can go on with this information, but
> if there is anything further I can do, please let me know.
> 
> Yours truly, [aunt?] [...]
