# TEXT OF READING 1151-32 M 55

**Reading [[1151-32]]** · Series: 801-1500 · Year: 1944 · Sex: M

## Text

Date: 8/11/1944  Sex: M  Age: 55  ReadingID: 6483

This psychic reading given by Edgar Cayce at the office of [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]], Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 11th day of August, 1944, in accordance with request made by the self - through wife, Mrs. [1158], Active Member of the Ass''n for Research and Enlightenment, Inc.

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], Jeanette Fitch, Stenos.]

At the home 
Time of Reading                                of [1266], 
Set bet. 3:30 to 4:30 P. M. Eastern War Time.  ..., Michigan.

1. GC:  You will give the physical condition of this body at the present time with suggestions for further corrective measures, answering the questions, as I ask them:

2. EC:  Yes, as we find conditions are not so good as when last we had same.  For the pressures in the transverse colon have caused an engorging of the thrombose or the trachea and thrombose circulation, engorging the heart. Thus the shortness of breath, the cough which comes at times, which if there would be considerable less smoking would help, though not altogether.

3. But, as we find, the [[Colon Hydrotherapy|colonics]] properly given and the massages which will relieve the tensions throughout the alimentary canal, then the precautions taken in regard to taking too heavy meals, offer the bettered conditions for this body.

4. Ready for questions.

5. (Q)  Should I give up smoking altogether?
(A)  As has just been indicated, if it were altogether, it would be too much strain on the nervous system, but there should not be more than five or six a day.

6. (Q)  What meat is permissible?
(A)  Fish or fowl or lamb.  But none of these fried.

7. (Q)  What starches?
(A)  Brown bread, not any white bread ever, nor any macaroni. The rest we would find in moderation would be very good.

8. (Q)  Any capsules or vitamins?
(A)  Vitamin E.  This preferably with cereals.

9. (Q)  Is there a tubercular condition?
(A)  We do not find a tubercular condition.

10. (Q)  Is there a cancerous condition in throat or otherwise?
(A)  No cancerous condition in throat or otherwise.

11. (Q)  Anything else to be done for cough?
(A)  Do these things which have been indicated.

12. (Q)  Should I go to a heart specialist?
(A)  This will depend upon the responses which are made and the requirements of the activities of the body.  This as we find, might be more harmful than beneficial, unless necessity demands it.

13. (Q)  Could I follow some procedure to re-condition myself, without giving up my business?
(A)  As has been indicated.

14. (Q)  What living routine should I follow?
(A)  As has been outlined.

15. (Q)  Would I benefit from going to the Posture Clinic in Boston?
(A)  Not necessarily.  This can be done just as well and not nearly as expensive by the activities in the gymnasium.

16. (Q)  Any further corrections in diet?
(A)  Do these things as have been indicated for the better conditions as we find.

17. We are through with this reading.

Two copies to Wife [1158] - Special Delivery (one for Copy to Ass'n file                            Dr. Reilly) "   for indexing

## Reports

> [!quote]- Reports & Follow-up
> R1. 11/19/44 Wife [1158]'s letter [she sent small check to buy
> some [[Free Will|choice]] fruit or flowers for EC]:
> 
> "Dear Friends...with much love and gratitude for your many
> valuable services in the past, or use it in whatever way
> will contribute most to your peace and happiness.
> 
> "I want, also, to share with you a recent experience I had,
> which I believe has been a valuable one for me.
> 
> "Four weeks ago, Thursday, my dear [1151] fell out of his
> bedroom window at the ... Club, dropping 15 feet to a terrace
> below.  He got up in the night to open his window wider,
> placed his knee on the sill to get a better purchase (as the
> window stuck) and suddenly it released and flew up, and he
> flew out.  He broke 3 ribs; badly injured his left eye and
> face and forehead; cut in many places and bruised his right
> leg; sprained his left ankle and broke his left leg in over
> 200 places.  This happened between 2 and 3 o'clock in the
> morning.  He had expected to leave early the next morning
> for Philadelphia and was spending the night at the Club in
> [[New York City|New York]].
> 
> "At 6:45 A.M. of that morning, 10/19th, I was awakened by a
> phone call from the N.Y. State Police, announcing that my
> husband was in the Post Graduate Hospital in [[New York City|New York City]]
> with a possible fracture of both legs.  They would tell me
> nothing more except to contact the hospital where he had
> been taken about 2:30 A.M.
> 
> "My first thought was that 'God is in charge of this case,
> and I have no need for fear.'  I then called the hospital,
> but they would tell me nothing, not even what happened or
> where it had happened.  The two doctors attending him could
> not be disturbed as they were fighting for his life with
> oxygen tents and heart stimulants.  So, I said I would dress
> and come in at once.  First, I got breakfast for the children
> and saw them off to school at 7:45 (but told them nothing as
> there was nothing they could do at that moment).  It was an
> hour later before I had reached the hospital, only to find
> [1151] groaning and mangled in a semi-private ward, and no
> private nurse, and both doctors (who had struggled for his
> life for 6 hours or so) had left, and the house doctor had
> to go into an operation, and no one had authority to tell
> me anything.  All the nurse on the floor would say was:
> 'He is resting as well as you could expect, but you will
> have to phone the doctors for information about him, we are
> not allowed to give it out.'  His life was still endangered.
> Every 4 or 5 minutes the nurse would take his pulse, and
> every 25 minutes or so the intern would take his blood
> pressure.  The nurse did tell me, however, finally, that he
> had a concussion of the head, broken ribs and a dreadfully,
> badly broken left leg, and would be in the hospital a very
> long time before the repairs were achieved.
> 
> "[1151] was too ill to talk to me and say what had happened
> or how it had happened, and I could not reach either of the
> doctors (on the case) as they were off attending to their
> other patients; and were not expected in their offices until
> 2 P.M.  [1151], in the meantime, was pleading for something
> to ease his excruciating pain, but they would not give him
> anything because of his heart condition and concussion,
> fearing he might 'pass out' if they did.  He was in a
> dazed, semiconscious condition, but recognized me.
> 
> "I tried repeatedly to locate the doctors, by phone, walking
> down 3 flights of stairs each time I phoned, but to no avail.
> Then a nurse told me I was to report at the business office
> for an interview with the business manager.  He said I must
> pay AT ONCE a check for $287 on account, also I must sign
> that I would be responsible for all bills.  Also, he said,
> I must decide at once whether Mr. [1151] should have private
> nurses or not (they would cost $24 per day), also whether I
> should get a private room for him (they ranged from $10 to
> $20 a day, and I would have to take the first room available,
> irrespective of how much it cost, as they had waiting lists,
> and you had to take your turn and your chance on whatever
> the free room would cost when your turn came).  Besides this,
> the business manager told me that should I decide to take
> a private room, he wanted me to realize that all hospital
> charges IMMEDIATELY doubled, that is, X-ray pictures were
> doubled, operating room charges doubled, medicines and all
> services in hospital doubled.  He said in cases of fracture,
> such as my husband's, the X-rays were always high (in any
> event), ranging from $150 up, but, if I had a private room,
> these charges would all be doubled.
> 
> "I told this man I thought I had better wait until I talked
> with the doctors on the case to ascertain what they thought
> concerning the desirability of a private room, and nurses,
> etc.  Also, they might give me some idea as to how long these
> expenses would have to continue.  But he said he had to know
> at once, that it was difficult to get trained nurses, also
> private rooms were at a premium, and if I wanted either I
> would have to so indicate immediately.  He then told me that
> one of the doctors had ordered private nurses for the first
> 24 hours, on his say-so, but that nurses always wanted to
> know how long they were to be on a case before they accepted
> it.  That's why he asked me how long I wished to have them
> continued.
> 
> "I went to the phone, hoping to get a word of enlightenment
> from a talk with a spiritually minded cousin.  The phone
> booth had no light.  I dropped my change purse containing $65
> (all my money for house expenses which Mr. [1151] had given
> me the night before).  A few minutes after leaving the booth
> I discovered my loss, but the purse was gone when I returned
> to look for it.
> 
> "Then it came to me clearly, just like a great light, 'To
> him that hath (faith) shall be given, to him that hath not
> (faith) shall be taken away that which he hath.'
> 
> "I saw THAT I DID NOT HAVE FAITH, that all the huge bills
> that would have to be paid, for many months, could be handled
> 'under [[Grace|grace]]' by the Father.  Instead, I had fear possessing
> me that there would not be money enough, and therefore, that
> which I had (the $65) was taken away.
> 
> "When I saw the working of [[Law of Moses|the law]] I immediately turned about
> face.  Without any assurance from without, I marched up to
> the business office and signed for nurses and a private room,
> and said I would be responsible for expenses, though I did
> not know how.  Several days later a letter came from an aunt
> enclosing $20, and another sister sent $25, another $10, and
> our good old Mrs. [1309] (who worked for us for 17 years)
> left $10 at the house one evening.  In less than 10 days the
> $65 had been returned to me 'under [[Grace|grace]].'  Then $50 here,
> and $37.50 there, etc., came in to me from unexpected
> sources, and best of all Mr. [1151] had such a remarkable
> swift comeback, that in about 3 weeks he was able to begin
> doing some business over the phone, and has kept his Savings
> Bank Journal going, etc. (with cooperation with his office
> force).  So, to date, I can report all bills paid on time,
> and evidence of God being abundantly able to care for our
> every need, without leaning on human sources for help, or
> requesting help from human sources.  It has flowed naturally
> through them.
> 
> "Mr. [1151] will still be in the hospital for weeks;
> probably well into February, but I know that all is being
> divinely governed, and that 'everything works together for
> good to them that love the Lord.'
> 
> "The fact that I have no help living in the house, only a day
> worker, 1 and 1/2 days per week, makes letter writing almost
> impossible.  So, please excuse this letter, begun days ago,
> but not finished.
> 
> "I know the struggle you are all having will work out in the
> end all right, and great good will be the fruit thereof.
> Keep saying to yourself, 'NOW are we the sons of God - NOW.'
> This statement proved very useful to me, and still is.
> 
> "I know that physical strength will flow to dear [[Gladys Davis|Gladys]], and
> the others around about Mr. Cayce to help them meet their
> extra tasks - and I know God's presence is felt by you all,
> and that 'fear', (your only real enemy) will not be
> entertained by you.
> 
> "We will be celebrating Christmas in [1151]'s little 2 x 4
> hospital room, and I know it will be one of deep joy and
> peace.
> 
> "I know you, too, will find the peace and [[Healing|healing]] presence
> of [[Jesus Christ|the Christ]] in your midst.  Keep up your FAITH and see
> the Salvation of the Lord.
> 
> "We love you all and know 'All is well.'
> 
> "Finished 12/18/44.
> 
> "So Christmas cheer to you one and all, and may the New Year
> dawn bright and beautiful,"  Your faithful [1158] and [1151]
>
