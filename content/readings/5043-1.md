# TEXT OF READING 5043-1 F 6

**Reading [[5043-1]]** · Series: 3001-5747 · Year: 1944 · Sex: F

## Text

Date: 5/4/1944  Sex: F  Age: 6  ReadingID: 13163

This Psychic Reading given by Edgar Cayce at the office of [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|the Association]], Arctic Crescent, [[Virginia Beach|Virginia Beach]], Va., this 4th day of May, 1944, in accordance with request made by the mother - Mrs. [2428], Associate Member of the Ass''n for Research & Enlightenment, Inc., recommended by Miss [1298].

[Edgar Cayce; [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude Cayce]], Conductor; [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis]], J. Fitch, Stenos.]

Born October 1, 1937, in New Brunswick, N.J. 
22 Bayberry 
Time of Reading                                 Circle, 
Set bet. 10:30 to 11:30 A. M. Eastern War Time. Mountainside, 
New Jersey.

1. GC:  You will give the relation of [[Entity (Cayce Term)|this entity]] and the universe, and the universal forces; giving the conditions which are as personalities, latent and exhibited in the present life; also the former [[Resurrection Appearances|appearances]] in the earth plane, giving time, place and the name, and that in each life which built or retarded the development for [[Entity (Cayce Term)|the entity]]; giving the abilities of the present entity, that to which it may attain, and how.  You will answer the questions, as I ask them:

2. EC:  What a funny, funny experience!  New Brunswick, New Jersey!

3. Here we find [[Entity (Cayce Term)|an entity]] with abilities which might be said to be limitless provided the [[Ideal|ideals]] and [[Purpose|purposes]] are chosen in the directions in which there is the consideration of the [[Universal Consciousness|universal consciousness]], rather than that of sectionalism or denominationalism, yet very easily would the entity fall under the spell of oriental customs and these are not very orthodox in the occidental world.

4. Then rather would the warnings be to those who are and will be responsible for the environs created for this body.

5. The entity has been and was very close to the activities in the eastern or oriental activities, being one of the Brahma Samaj in Poona in the experience just before this.

6. Hence there is the intent of [[Visions|visions]].  The entity loves to tell of that which it has seen.  Don't question it, but do keep records of same.

7. Do, more and more, teach the 14th, 15th, 16th and 17th of St. [[John (Apostle)|John]] to the entity.  Do teach the child, [[Jesus Christ|Jesus]].  These should be the basic principles, beginning with [[Exodus|Exodus]] 19:5; then portions of the letter to the Corinthians, especially that referring to that which is within self.

8. For the body is indeed the temple of the living God.  Have this as a principle in the teachings to this entity, in its development, in its growth, and in its cleanliness not merely of body but of mind.

9. This shall be acceded to, should be kept as the first basic principle:  Do encourage, rather, the [[Dreams|dreams]] and [[Visions|visions]] of the entity, but don't question them.  Do record them, and as the periods of its unfoldment come the entity may enter into its work capable of ministering to the needs of this sin-sick world at this time.

10. When the entity, then, is in its thirteenth to fourteenth year, we would give the complete fulfillment of what may be accomplished, if the entity sets its star toward [[Star of Bethlehem|the Star]] in [[Bethlehem|Bethlehem]] and not in Poona.

11. We are through.

Copy to Mother
"    " Ass'n file "   for indexing

> BRAHMA SAMAJ, "the Union of God" (the term SAMAJ connoting,
> like "church" an organism, not a mere association), a
> religious reform society inaugurated by Ram Mohan Roy in
> Calcutta about 1800.  Study of the [[India|Hindu]] scriptures, of
> Islam, Buddhism in [[Tibet|Tibet]] and the Bible having shown him the
> perversions of the popular creeds, Ram Mohan embraced the
> monotheism of the Upanishads, rejecting the polytheism of the
> SHASTRAS, and in 1816 he drew a congregation of Hindus which
> recited [[India|Vedic]] and theistic hymns, but made no headway with
> that community at large.  He then joined a Unitarian
> committee, but it collapsed, and in 1828 he founded the
> Brahma Samaj.  The trust deed of the building dedicated in
> 1830 declared it to be a place of meeting for all
> descriptions of men for the worship of the Eternal under no
> sectarian name.  No graven image or icon, and no sacrifices
> were to be tolerated in it.  No object of worship venerated
> by other sects was to be reviled; but all [[Prayer|prayer]], hymns and
> discourses promoting charity, virtue and toleration were to
> be admissible.  Nevertheless, at this stage the new faith was
> based on the VEDAS.  Ram Mohan was, moreover, a keen social
> reformer and did much to bring about Bentinck's statutory
> abolition of suttee in 1829.  Soon after, however, he left
> India and his death at Bristol in 1833 left the Samaj to
> languish till 1841.
> 
> In that year Babu Debendra Nath Tagore, of a well-known
> Calcutta family, founder of the Tattvabodhini ("Truth-
> learning") Sabha ("society"), joined forces with the Brahma
> Samaj, gave it a printing press, and in 1843 started the
> TATTVABODHINI PATRIKA, a monthly journal which attained to
> great influence as an expositor of theism.  About 1850,
> however, Debendra and most of his followers denied the
> infallibility of the VEDAS, and redefined its creed as faith
> based on nature and [[Intuition|intuition]], though religious truth
> contained in any book was to be respected, man's beliefs
> being evolutionary.  The Samaj acknowledged the One Supreme
> God, endowed with personality, moral attributes and
> intelligence, without incarnations; the immortality and
> progress of [[Soul|the soul]] were inculcated; pilgrimages, ceremonial
> and penances were declared vain and caste distinctions
> worthless.  Stress was laid on moral righteousness.  For many
> years no social reforms were advocated, but in 1865 Babu
> Keshub Chunder Sen demanded the disuse of the Brahmanical
> thread by leaders in devotion.  This led to schism, and
> thenceforth there were two branches:  The Adi or "original"
> Samaj, small in numbers, which never increased, and the
> Bharatvarshiya Brahma Samaj, the Samaj "of India," which set
> to work on several schemes of reform.  But in 1878 Keshab's
> action in countenancing the marriage of his daughter at an
> early age to the young ruler of Kuch Bihar, despite his own
> teaching, led to a great secession from the latter branch and
> the founding of the Sadharan Samaj or "catholic" union, which
> professes a broad theism.  Keshad's reply was the
> organization of the "new dispensation" (Nava Bidhana), which
> went far beyond the older branches in its eclecticism.
