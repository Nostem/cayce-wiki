## Reports

> [!quote]- Reports & Follow-up
> R1. 11/42 Excerpt from [[Thomas Sugrue|THERE IS A RIVER]], page 191:
> 
> A few days after returning to [[Hopkinsville|Hopkinsville]], he again became
> a father.  [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude]] bore her second son, [[Milton Harrison|Milton]] Porter, on
> March 29th [actually, 28th, 8:30 P.M.].  Edgar was happier
> than he had ever been before.
> 
> But the baby became ill.  He developed whooping cough, and
> this was succeeded by colitis.  [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude]] did not ask for
> a reading, nor did it occur to Edgar that anything was
> seriously wrong until the doctors suddenly gave up hope.
> Then a reading [5784-1] was taken.  It was too late; acid
> had permeated the system.  The baby died [May 17, 1911].
> 
> Edgar was stunned.  The reading hadn't been taken in time,
> obviously.  He and [[Gertrude Cayce|Gertrude]] had depended on doctors.  In
> their smug way they had relied on the medical profession,
> while every day Edgar was going to sleep, diagnosing, and
> prescribing for other people.  It had never occurred to him
> to question whether, in an emergency involving himself, or
> his child, or Gertrude, he would turn to a reading for help.
> He had worried about whether to offer his powers to other
> people, and had decided that he should, because so many had
> been helped by them and so many believed in them.  But he
> had not really answered the question - even during that long
> night in the studio at Montgomery - of whether he believed in
> the readings himself.  He was a [[Prophets|prophet]] without honor in his
> own heart.
> 
> He would have quit had he not been under contract, and had he
> not begun to be deeply affected by the stream of letters of
> thanks which poured into the office.  Other people had faith
> in him; they seemed sure of his powers; they had been helped
> by the treatments suggested in their readings.  They thanked
> him profusely.  Some said they remembered him in their
> [[Prayer|prayers]].  Was it possible for a man to mean so much to
> others and so little to himself?
> 
> R2. 8/17/80 Inquiry in re pg. 191 of [[Thomas Sugrue|THERE IS A RIVER]], by Thomas
> [[Thomas Sugrue|Sugrue]]:
> 
> Dear [[[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|A.R.E.]]] Librarian,
> 
> Please forgive my not knowing your name; and forgive my not
> having an official form, for I'm sure one exists.  But I am
> a member and I would like to order the circulating files on
> ASTHMA and on ALLERGIES.  HAY FEVER.  If there is a mailing
> charge, could you please just bill me when you send the
> files?  Thank you very much.
> 
> I also have one other question.  On page 164 of my copy of
> THERE IS A RIVER (Chapter 11, about 14 pages in), Mr. Sugrue
> mentions that a reading was taken on Edgar Cayce's second
> son, [[Milton Harrison|Milton]] Porter, [5784] before he died:  "It was too late;
> acid had permeated the system.  The baby died."  Once when
> I was at the [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|A.R.E.]] I looked for such a reading but could
> not find it.  Since I have heard that all of Edgar Cayce's
> readings were transcribed, I wondered if you know of such
> a reading and if so, if I could obtain a copy, I would
> be most appreciative of any information regarding this
> reading.  Thank you very much,
> 
> Sincerely, [signed] G. W. Burgess
> 
> R3. 8/26/80 GD's response:
> 
> 
> Dear Mr. Burgess:
> 
> It is sad to say that a copy of [[Milton Harrison|Milton]] Porter Cayce's reading
> was not kept, back in May, 1911.  In fact, in Dr. Wesley
> H. Ketcham's little book, THE DISCOVERY OF EDGAR CAYCE, he
> reports on many of the early readings which he obtained for
> his patients of which we have no copies.  We do have some
> copies of readings made in 1909, 1910 and 1911, but certainly
> not all of them.  Some affidavits are on file regarding some
> readings given in those early years but we do not have the
> actual copies of the readings to go with those affidavits.
> Little bits and pieces are sifting in regarding those early
> days, from time to time, but I doubt if we will ever get
> everything.  Edgar Cayce stated in a letter to someone (a
> copy of which we do have) that his first reading which led to
> the restoring of his voice was given on March 29, 1901, but
> there is no copy of that reading.
> 
> When I became Edgar Cayce's secretary on September 10, 1923,
> he had on file a total of 600 copies of readings.  During the
> process of taking all the readings available, anonymous, with
> background and follow-up reports, complete with indexing,
> etc. (1960 to 1971, an 11 yr. process) we tabulated a total
> of 14,256 copies of readings.  These are the number that we
> have on file.  We have no way of knowing how many readings he
> gave in those early days of which no copies were kept.
> 
> Sincerely, [signed] [[Gladys Davis|Gladys Davis Turner]]
> Sec'ty [[A.R.E. (Association for Research and Enlightenment)|A.R.E.]] & E.C.F.
> 
> P.S.  I'm using this original as a master for making a file
> on Milton Porter Cayce, and sending you a copy.  I hope you
> don't mind.
